# V2-C0255: the powers-tensor rank answer fails for noncyclic matrices

Confirmed false answer, medium impact. Third edition, first printing
(September 1997), exercise 4.6.4-55 on printed 521 and its answer on
711 (file pages 533 and 723). Both original pages were inspected on
10 November 2026.

The exercise asks for the rank of the tensor whose matrix slices are
I,P,...,P^(n-1), for an **arbitrary** n by n matrix P. Here tensor rank
means the minimum number of products a_i b_j c_k whose sum gives its
entries. The answer gives 2d-q, where d is the degree of P's minimum
polynomial (the least-degree monic polynomial mu with mu(P)=0), and
q is the number of distinct irreducible factors of mu.

Take P=I_2 over the rationals. Then mu(u)=u-1, so the answer gives
2*1-1=1. But the two slices are I_2,I_2. Every slice of a rank-one
tensor is a rank-at-most-one matrix, whereas I_2 has matrix rank 2.
The tensor has rank exactly 2, as the explicit decomposition

    e1 tensor e1 tensor (1,1) + e2 tensor e2 tensor (1,1)

shows; e1=(1,0) and e2=(0,1) are the coordinate vectors.

This is not restricted to scalar matrices. For
P=diag(0,0,1,1), the minimum polynomial is u(u-1), and the printed
answer gives 2. The tensor with slices I_4,P,P,P has rank 4: its
identity slice supplies the lower bound and its four diagonal entries
supply a four-term decomposition.

The answer confuses the dimension of the algebra generated by P with
the size and multiplicities of its representation on the underlying
vector space. Similarity does not remove repeated invariant blocks.
Theorem W correctly covers the companion-matrix case; by similarity,
its formula also applies when P is cyclic, meaning similar to the
companion matrix of a degree-n minimum polynomial. Restricting the
exercise to that case repairs it, but leaves its requested arbitrary-
matrix problem unanswered. No general replacement theorem is claimed
here; the two exact counterexamples suffice to refute the answer.

err2.textxt. None corrects this answer. The older second-edition list
has a 6 July 1997 amendment on its page 651 replacing the factor-count
label r' by q'; that is a different correction and does not repair
