# Two shadow-minimization tasks omit the comparison shadow

Volume 4A, first edition, first printing (2011), Exercises 7.2.1.3–103 and 104, concluding inequalities,
printed page 388, file page 405.

The shadow of a subcube is obtained by replacing one asterisk with 0 or 1; the shadow of a binary string is obtained by deleting one bit. In each case the shadow of a family is the union of those smaller objects.

Exercises 103 and 104 end with `|partial A|>=|P_Nst|` and `|partial A|>=|P_Nn|`, respectively. The printed answers define P to be the first N objects in a specified order. The intended extremal statement in each case is `|partial A|>=|partial P|`.

The printed inequalities fail for these stated P. In exercise 103 take all twelve edges of the 3-cube: s=2 fixed digits, t=1 asterisk, N=12. Their shadow has eight vertices, less than |P|=12. In exercise 104 take all four binary strings of length two. Their deletion shadow is {0,1}, of size 2, less than |P|=4. These are failures of the written comparison, not of the cited shadow-minimization theorems.
