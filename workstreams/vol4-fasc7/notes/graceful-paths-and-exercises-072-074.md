endpoint to an endpoint; that choice determines its entire action
successively along the unique path. Both identity and reversal

## E72-1 — the complete representation invariant

On entry to P2 at level l, exactly l−1 edges have been chosen,
with differences n−1,n−2,…,n−l+1. On all n label vertices, they
form a disjoint union of paths, including isolated vertices.

For an isolate v, MATE[v]=v. For a nontrivial path's two ends
v,a, MATE[v]=a and MATE[a]=v. Every internal vertex has negative
MATE: it is the bitwise complement of its former opposite endpoint
when it most recently ceased to be an endpoint. Those negative
values are restoration records; their exact magnitude is not
needed to decide future admissibility.

At l=1 all vertices are isolated, so P1 establishes the invariant.
For the next difference d=n−l, possible edges have the exact
form(v,w)=(v,v+d),0≤v<l. An endpoint has spare degree precisely
when its MATE is nonnegative. Among two such distinct vertices,
they belong to the same nontrivial component precisely when
MATE[v]=w. Hence the three rejection conditions in P3 are exactly
degree exhaustion or cycle creation. They reject no legal join.

## E72-2 — all aliasing cases in the four assignments

Let a=MATE[v],b=MATE[w] before an accepted join. P3 writes

    MATE[v]=~a; MATE[w]=~b; MATE[a]=b; MATE[b]=a.

If both vertices were isolated, a=v and b=w; the last two writes
replace the first two, leaving v and w paired. If only v was
isolated, a=v: its last write makes v the new endpoint paired with
b, while old w becomes internal and retains~b. The other one-isolate
case is symmetric. If neither was isolated, v,w,a,b are four
distinct vertices because same-component joining was rejected;
v and w become internal with their saved values, and a,b become
the two new endpoints. These cases exhaust all allowed aliases.
Every unmentioned array entry is unchanged. Thus the new forest
and every stored restoration value satisfy the invariant.

The exact printed examples follow. After405, MATE is
(~5,1,2,3,5,4); after4052,13 it is(~5,3,4,1,2,~4).
Both complete six-entry arrays are reproduced, not merely their
endpoint values.

## E72-3 — exact restoration in P5

All deeper moves have been undone before P5 handles a chosen edge
(v,w). Read both current entries v'=MATE[v],w'=MATE[w] before
changing either. If v' is nonnegative, old v was an isolate:
every previously nonisolated endpoint would have become internal
and negative. Resetting MATE[v]=v therefore restores it exactly.
If v'<0, then~v' is its old mate a; restoring MATE[v]=a and
MATE[a]=v restores both endpoints of that old component. Apply
the analogous rule to w.

With two old isolates these are simply two self-pointers. With
one isolate, the other pair restoration repairs its opposite
endpoint, while the isolate's reset is untouched. With neither,
the two old endpoint pairs are disjoint. In all cases the saved
negative records at other interior vertices are unchanged. Thus
P5 restores **the entire old array**, not only the current degrees.
The checker compares that array with the saved pre-P3 snapshot
at every return. Reading both v',w' before either restoration is
part of this justification.

## E72-4 — completeness, normalization, termination, and cost

For l>2, P4 tries every v=0,…,l−1 exactly once. At l=1 there is
only v=0. At l=2 it intentionally keeps only v=0; the proved
complement formula makes this exactly the LO[n−2]=0 normalization
for n≥3. No subsequent level is similarly truncated.

At l=n the chosen n−1 edges form a forest of paths on n vertices.
A forest with n−1 edges has one component, so this is a spanning
path with all required distinct differences. Conversely, every
normalized gracefully labeled path selects one legal edge at each
descending difference; every prefix is a path forest, so the
algorithm follows it without rejection. Its LO sequence is unique.
This proves both completeness and absence of duplicates at the
equivalence-class level. Reversal changes no undirected labeled
edges; the normalization deals with complement.

The level is bounded between1 and n. Each finite candidate list
is exhausted in increasing order, and P5 restores its parent
before trying the next candidate. Consequently the finite search
terminates. At n=1, P2 immediately visits the sole path0 and P5
terminates; at n=2 it visits only edge01. Both small cases are
correct without applying the four-permutation multiplier there.

Each candidate test, accepted join, and undo uses O(1) word work.
If T candidate tests are attempted and S solutions are explicitly
written as full permutations, the cost is O(n+T+nS), with O(n)
auxiliary space. Reading a completed path takes O(n); this output
cost is not silently included in a constant-time visit. The signed
word must represent the labels and their complements, using
O(log(n+1)) bits. No precise historical mem count follows from
this structural cost argument.

## E73-1 — why the blurred state contains exactly the needed future data

Replace all negative MATE entries by a common marker. Isolates
are still distinguished by their self-pointer; available endpoint
pairs remain fully known; internal vertices remain unavailable.
Thus P3's three conditions, and the blurred result of every
successful join, are determined solely by the blurred state.
Induction over remaining differences gives identical trees of
future LO choices for two equal states. Past internal ordering
affects the completed permutation, but not any admissible continuation.

The state also determines its level. If it has i internal vertices
and2c endpoints of nontrivial components, those components contain
i+2c vertices and i+c edges. Hence l=i+c+1. Equal states cannot
occur at different levels. The printed partial paths1738092 and
1809372 each use differences4,…,9, have endpoints1,2, and leave
4,5,6 isolated. Their common blur is exactly

    (−,2,1,−,4,5,6,−,−,−).

Both occur at l=7 in the complete P10 tree. That tree has208
nodes including the two initial states, hence the stated206 in

and all candidate counts are saved.

## E73-2 — all changes to the depth-first procedure

Intern each blurred tuple in a dictionary, retaining its FIRST
branch-list head and LOC output marker. Reserve branch record1;
initialize the branch counter p=1 as corrected. After an accepted
P3 move, intern the new state α_l and append one record
(v_p,β_p,o_p), with β_p=α_l and o_p equal to the old FIRST of
α_(l−1). Make p its new FIRST. Each accepted choice therefore
appears exactly once in the linked list of its parent state.

If the child has been seen, jump to **P5**, which first decreases
l and then undoes this last join. Skipping that decrement would
undo the wrong level; the known correction is necessary. A new
child is explored normally. P2 sets LOC=1 at a solution; a failed
state has no complete continuation and remains available in the
dictionary, even when FIRST=0.

Depth-first reuse is sound without waiting for another traversal.
A previously seen equal state has the same level. It cannot be
an unfinished ancestor, whose level is strictly smaller. Hence
its first exploration has already finished when a different
prefix encounters it. Its entire branch list and terminal status
are therefore known. This closes the printed depth-first versus
breadth-first observation.

## E73-3 — every operation of the conversion procedure

Use0 for rejection and1 for acceptance. For n≥2, the root has
at least one branch: the alternating sequence n−1,0,n−2,1,…
is graceful, with consecutive differences n−1,n−2,…,1, and
satisfies the normalization. The corrected initialization sets
LOC(root)=2, puts its dummy record1 on the stack, and sets z=3,
the next unallocated output index. This prevents collision with
the already reserved root index.

The outer loop pops a state whose LOC index is reserved but whose
instruction list has not yet been written. Its q list contains
exactly its outgoing choices. Save q'=o_q before changing anything.
If its target α has LOC=0 but FIRST≠0, reserve LOC(α)=z, increase
z, and push that target using the current branch record as stack
storage. If LOC=1, it is a solution terminal; if LOC=0 and FIRST=0,
it is a dead terminal. An already reserved target is neither
reallocated nor pushed again.

Changing o_q into a stack link is safe. A branch record belongs
to only one parent list, and that list is being consumed exactly
once. Its former successor q' has already been saved. No later
state needs the old link. Distinct records may target the same
state, but that does not share their parent-list links.

For each choice v_q, write an instruction at the current p. Its
choose branch goes to LOC(α). If q' exists, its reject branch goes
to the newly reserved next alternative z; set p=z and increase z.
Otherwise rejecting goes to0. Then advance q=q'. Thus every
parent's output is exactly a chain that either chooses one legal
v or rejects all of them. Every reserve is unique, every pushed
state is eventually popped, and every stored branch produces one
instruction. Both loops terminate on finite lists. No branch is
lost when the same child state is reused.

The variable at such a node must mean the **choice at the current
difference d**, not the integer v independently of d. One can
use Boolean choice variables(d,v), ordered by descending d and,
within each d, by the reverse candidate order of the linked list.
Choosing one advances to the next d; rejecting advances to the
next v. The represented family consists of exactly one choice
per remaining difference. It may contain duplicate subgraphs,
and nodes with both children0, so the result need not be reduced
or zero-suppressed. This is precisely why the source qualifies
its description as being like a ZDD.

## E73-4 — the exact4A counting reference and conversion cost

At terminal0 the count is0 and at terminal1 it is1. At an
instruction, solutions that reject the current choice and those
that choose it are disjoint, so its count is the sum of the two
child counts. This remains valid for equal child diagrams, dead
nodes, and unreduced sharing: the branch decisions distinguish
the families. Counting a shared child once and reusing its value
does not collapse the different prefixes leading to it.

This is exactly the recurrence in Volume4A answer7.1.4--208,
which removes the three skipped-variable powers of2 from AlgorithmC.
Its stated input requires children before parents in the numbering.
The newly produced F7 indices do not have that property in general;
first take a topological order or traverse children before parents,
as4A p207 explicitly permits. Then the ordinary ascending C1/C2
loop applies, with root last. This representation conversion is
part of applying the referenced algorithm under its actual premise.
Every saved small output has this renumbering and its entire count
table checked against the full72 continuations.

If D distinct states and A branch records are stored, the conversion
has O(D+A) time and space, and at most A nonterminal output nodes.
Each state's nonempty list is traversed once; each record is
consumed once. Counting needs O(A) integer additions, with their
bit cost determined by the counts' length. Building the dictionary
by explicit length-n keys uses O(nD+A) storage and, with expected
constant-probe hashing, O(T+nA) work apart from output, where T
is the number of candidate tests in the merged traversal. This
does not claim constant time for comparing arbitrary full keys
or a worst-case hashing bound.

At n=1, however, the root is already terminal1 and has no branches.

## E74-1 — the maximum length for2m points and the perturbation step

Let m≥1 and sort the distinct coordinates as
y1<…<y_(2m). A path means a visiting order of marked points, with
length the sum of distances of consecutive visits. A longest order
uses all points: an omitted distinct point could be appended to
increase the length. A maximum exists because there are finitely
many orders. If repeated coordinates are allowed as distinct
labels, the final formula extends by continuity: the maximum of
finitely many continuous length functions is continuous, as are
the sorted-coordinate sums. This supplies a precise justification
for the corrected small-perturbation reduction; it does not assume
that a chosen maximizing permutation stays unchanged.

Suppose an internal coordinate x_k lies strictly between its
neighbors. Deleting it from that position leaves the length of
that two-edge portion unchanged, by additivity on a line; appending
it after the former final point adds a positive distance. Thus
such an order was not maximal. Every internal point must therefore
be a strict local extremum, so the signs of consecutive differences
alternate. Reverse the order if needed to arrange

    x1>x2<x3>…<x_(2m−1)>x_(2m).

The exact length is

    2 sum(odd positions) x − 2 sum(even positions) x − x1 + x_(2m).

Its coefficients are m−1 copies of+2, one+1 at the first point,
one−1 at the last point, and m−1 copies of−2. If a larger
coefficient multiplies a smaller coordinate than a smaller
coefficient, swapping them increases the sum by the product of
their positive differences. Repeated swaps prove the rearrangement
maximum: the largest m−1 coordinates occupy internal odd positions,
the smallest m−1 occupy internal even positions, x1=y_(m+1), and
x_(2m)=y_m. This assignment itself alternates, so the unrestricted
coefficient maximum is attainable by a legitimate path. It is
equivalent to every printed inequality

    x_(2k)<x_(2m)<x1<x_(2k+1), 1≤k<m.

Writing S for the m smallest and T for the m largest coordinates,
the answer is

    L_max=2 sum(T)−2 sum(S)−y_(m+1)+y_m.

At m=1 this reduces to the one distance y2−y1; the displayed
chain of inequalities is empty, as it should be.

## E74-2 — maximum cyclic length and the necessary equality condition

Choose t strictly between y_m and y_(m+1). For every cyclic edge
a--b, the triangle inequality gives|a−b|≤|a−t|+|b−t|, with
equality exactly when its ends lie on opposite sides of t. Each
point occurs in two cyclic edges. Therefore the cyclic length is
at most2sum|y−t|=2sum(T)−2sum(S). A cyclic order alternating S,T
attains equality. Since every inequality's deficit is nonnegative,
equality in the total forces **every** edge to alternate S,T.
This necessity is essential to part(b); the maximum value alone
would not prove the conclusion. For two points the cyclic length
counts the outward and return segments, consistent with the formula.

## E74-3 — part(b), with every arithmetic and orientation step

Take a graceful permutation p1,…,p_(2m) of1,…,2m with
p_(2m)=p1+m. The closing segment has length m, so its cyclic
length is

    (1+…+(2m−1))+m=m(2m−1)+m=2m².

Here sum(T)−sum(S) is
m(3m+1)/2−m(m+1)/2=m². Thus this cycle attains the exact maximum
fromE74-2 and must alternate the two halves. The endpoint condition
also gives p1≤m and p_(2m)>m. Starting in the lower half then
forces every even position p_(2k)>m, exactly as required. The
argument includes m=1.

## E74-4 — part(c), with the endpoint coefficients

Conversely, if all m even positions are greater than m, they use
precisely T={m+1,…,2m}; all odd positions use S={1,…,m}.
The path alternates low/high. Every internal high has coefficient+2
and the final high coefficient+1; every internal low has coefficient−2
and the first low coefficient−1. Hence its length is exactly

    2sum(T)−p_(2m)−2sum(S)+p1
      =2m²−p_(2m)+p1.

Gracefulness independently gives length1+…+(2m−1)=2m²−m.
Equating these expressions yields p_(2m)−p1=m. This proves the
converse and verifies the final printed intermediate formula,
without needing any permutation search or asymptotic assumption.
