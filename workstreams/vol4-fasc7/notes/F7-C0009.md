## An exact contradiction under the stated conditions

Use the answer's matrices

    A = [1 1; 1 0],   B = [1 −1; −1 0],   X = diag(1,−1),

and choose m=6, p=(−1,0,1,0,0,−1). Every p_k is at least−1,
p1=pm=−1, and q=Σ(p_k+2)=11. The printed starting expression is

    M=(AXA)A^p1 … (AXA)A^p6 = A B A² B³ A² B²
      = [−2 1; 7 −3],                traceM=−5.

The printed last expression instead becomes

    A B² A² B⁴ = [7 −4; 12 −7],      trace=0.

Thus this particular printed equality asserts −5=0. With
the corrected first exponent, it is

    A B⁴ A² B⁴ = [12 −7; 29 −17],    trace=−5.

These are exact2×2 integer multiplications. `check_reduction55c.py`
also independently evaluates the original six-factor normal
form, including the exact A inverse, and obtains the same M.
All three matrices are saved in `results/reduction55c.json`.

## Why the correction is +3

In the affected paragraph put r=p2+2, s=p3+2 and let R denote
the remaining alternating powers. The two valid preceding
equalities follow from ABA=BAB and cyclic invariance of trace:

    tr(ABA^r B^s R)=tr(BAB A^(r−1) B^s R)
                  =tr(ABA^(r−1) B^s R B).

Repeating this exactly r times gives

    tr(ABA^r B^s R)=tr(A B^(s+1) R B^r).

Hence the first B exponent is s+1=p3+3, and the final B
exponent increases by p2+2, exactly as the source already
prints. The number of switches decreases by two and the
original total length q is preserved. For m=4 the first and
last remaining B runs merge into one; the witness uses m=6
to keep all displayed factors distinct.

The full corrected reduction, its termination, all norm
estimates, all eigenvalue steps and the excluded small
q=3–6 cases are proved in
`notes/hc-boundary-matrix-bounds-055.md`. Therefore this
finding does **not** refute either final inequality55(b–c).
