# F7-C0018 — The concatenation construction need not take polynomial time

Confirmed, low severity, 12 November 2026. Answer 364, final parenthetical
comment, printed 239/file 254. First digital release, March 2025,
ISBN 978-0-13-532824-8. The original page was inspected directly.

The answer replaces the input constraints R_1,...,R_m by their Cartesian
product, a relation R with s_1*...*s_m tuples, and uses a domain D^k where
k is the sum of their arities. Its final comment nevertheless describes
the construction as needing only polynomial time. For the explicit finite
domains and tuple tables used in the construction, this is false when m
is part of the input.

For each j=0,...,m-1, take two variables x_j,y_j, both with domain
{2j,2j+1}, and the binary relation

    R_j = {(2j,2j), (2j+1,2j+1)}.

These are m distinct, nontrivial two-tuple relations. The input has O(m)
domain/table entries, or O(m log m) bits with explicit integer labels.
The printed construction has k=2m, D={0,...,2m-1}, and R contains 2^m
distinct tuples of length 2m. Merely listing R requires exponentially many
entries; explicitly listing the new domain D^(2m) is larger still. No
search or difficult counting theorem is involved in this lower bound.

The mathematical satisfiability equivalence is unaffected, and the exercise
explicitly permits inefficiency. The error is the unqualified time claim.
Delete the parenthetical assertion, or qualify it by fixing the number
and arities of the input relations. Representing products and domains by
succinct symbolic descriptions would be a different input model and is
not the explicit-table construction being described. The witness uses
distinct relation tables, so merely sharing identical relation symbols
does not remove its growth.
