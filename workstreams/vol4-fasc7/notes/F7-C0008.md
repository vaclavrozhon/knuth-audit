# F7-C0008 — a realizable boundary can use another W ordering

Status: **candidate, medium severity**, awaiting independent review.
Target: exercise50(a), p118/file133, the assertion that its six
listed boundary cases are exhaustive; also answer50(a), p169/file184.
This is separate from the shared-inner-line issue in C0007.

## A simple, biconnected and labelable counterexample

Use the clockwise boundary polygon

```
a=(0,0)   b=(0,-1)   c=(2,-1)   d=(2,0)
e=(1,0)   f=(1,1)    g=(-1,1)   h=(-1,0)
```

in that cyclic order, and add just the chord **ac**. Its open
points are `(x,−x/2)` for0<x<2, all inside the polygon's upper
rectangular part. The chord meets no other edge or vertex.
The polygon is simple, with clockwise signed double area8.
Every junction has degree2 or3; all degree2 corners are
noncollinear. There are **no T junctions**, so exercise47's
extra prohibition on two outer T bars is vacuously satisfied.
The polygon itself is a spanning cycle, hence the graph is
biconnected. No repeated boundary point or bridge is involved.

The exact HC network is

```
a = W(h,b,c)       e = V(d,f)
b = V(c,a)         f = V(g,e)
c = W(d,a,b)       g = V(h,f)
d = V(e,c)         h = V(a,g).
```

This is an actual HC picture and therefore a realizable network
under the explicit definition in47. Exercise50 permits the other
endpoint of an inner line to lie on the boundary, so chord ac
does not violate its assumptions.

It even has a complete valid labeling, written in the indicated
directions:

`ab:−, bc:>, cd:>, de:−, ef:<, fg:+, gh:<, ha:+, ac:+`.

The resulting local Table1 rows are, for a through h respectively,

`+−+, O−, O+I, −I, −I, +O, I+, +O`.

Thus the counterexample does not depend on an unsatisfiable
network or on a vacuous interpretation of a free boundary.
The labeling is not standard, which is allowed by the actual
premise “realizable HC network.”

## The missing case occurs at a

At a the previous boundary junction is h, the next is b and the
other neighbor is c. The exercise's only W case would require

`a = W(b,c,h)`.

But the actual ordered rays at a are

`h−a=(−1,0)`, `b−a=(0,−1)`, `c−a=(2,−1)`.

Their three relevant clockwise determinants, using the page's
downward y orientation, are

`[h−a,b−a]=1`, `[b−a,c−a]=2`, `[h−a,c−a]=1`.

Therefore they form **W(h,b,c)**, with b as the middle branch.
The proposed W(b,c,h) is impossible: `[c−a,h−a]=−1`, so c is
not between b and h in the required smaller-than180° sector.
Unlike Y junctions, W arguments cannot be cyclically permuted;
the main text on p12 explicitly distinguishes this ordered
geometry. None of the other five cases has type W.

Geometrically, a is a reflex corner of the outer polygon while
the three incident rays themselves occupy a sector smaller than
180°. The definition of realizability in47 excludes a certain
outer T configuration, but it does not exclude this W geometry.
The complete eight-vertex realization proves that no hidden
global planarity obstruction rules it out.

## Consequence and bounded repair suggestion

The six-case classification is not exhaustive under its stated
input assumptions. The proposed boundary-matrix construction
cannot assign one of those cases to this valid boundary
occurrence without changing the actual junction relation.
The ordering claim in answer50(a) is therefore also unsupported
by the stated boundary-cycle definition.

One needs to include the other boundary placements of a W, or
state an additional assumption that rules them out. For example,
an existing standard labeling would force both outer W branches
to be the extreme branches, because a W middle branch cannot be
an arrow. The exercise, however, assumes realizability, not the
existence of a standard labeling. A full repair of the general
algorithm, including C0007's separate shared-line issue, is not
claimed here.

The six matrices printed in50(b) remain correct for the six
specific configurations they actually represent. The already
audited histoscape and four example boundaries use those
configurations correctly; their exact counts are unaffected.
