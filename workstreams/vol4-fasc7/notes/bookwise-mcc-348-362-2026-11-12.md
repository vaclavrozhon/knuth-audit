# Prime-queen tours, multiple covering and exercises 348–362

Read main printed 75–78 and the MCC continuation at the top of 79,
questions 348–362 and complete answers 234–238. Original answer pages
235–236 and 238 were inspected for the algorithm and displayed patterns.
The five changes from s to s' in F3 and the corresponding changes in 353
are applied: the temporary slack must not overwrite the search stage.

The two knight-tour encodings use board bipartiteness correctly. In 348,
cell and step primary items give bijections, while the colored copies make
successive moves agree at both endpoints. The allowed parity and queen
attack tests are applied to both endpoints. Equal numbers of required odd
labels and attacked red cells justify the extra omission rule; the two
multiplicity items in the remaining case account separately for outgoing
and incoming extra visits. In 349, one option traverses a white cell
between two red cells. The two red-cell primary copies and colored step
copies join those paths; every white cell and odd step is used exactly once.
The second parity choice is covered by the explicit symmetric case.

For MCC, the residual lower bound is BOUND-SLACK. Equation (133) is
SIZE+1-max(BOUND-SLACK,0); for the displayed five-option example it is
three. Equality to one forces all remaining options. Algorithm F saves
both sizes and bounds at nonforced stages, decrements BOUND on commitment,
and covers an item when its upper bound reaches zero. Otherwise it removes
the selected option only from that item's current list. A primary item can
become inactive with no options only after its residual lower bound is
satisfied. The restoration in F18 recovers both quantities, and setting
d_l=1 before purging ensures that a contradiction during that purge backs
up rather than trying the same alternative again. Multiple forced copies
in FORCE correspond to all of an item's required remaining options;
entries left after the item becomes inactive are safely ignored.

The tie-breaking slack and size comparisons in F3 agree with the cited
multiple-covering heuristic. Exercise 353 keeps the integer branching
degree separate from its weighted floating-point score, so a heuristic
does not change whether a move is forced. The benchmark summaries use
the displayed best scores; no solver was run to reproduce them.

For 354, the even-diameter disk is centered at half-integral coordinates.
Its true squared distance is the displayed integer expression plus 1/2;
the strict inequality is equivalent because both the expression and d^2/4
are integers. Moving an outside center to the permitted interior range
does not lose any covered cells. The stated m,n>1 convention is used.
Piling in 355 is read with the preceding MCC definition: solutions are
subsets of the generated placement options, so identical placements are
not selected twice. The twelve-piece example obeys that convention.

In 356, negative clauses are encoded by counting chosen bindings in the
variable-assignment encoding inherited from 216. With one selected binding
per variable, the cap k-1 forbids exactly the all-matching tuple. A double
binding in one option needs the auxiliary count in the printed ternary
gadget; an all-three option is omitted. This argument is scoped to such
assignment encodings, not to arbitrary repeated colored occurrences of
the same binding in unrelated options. The unspecified general gadget is
not expanded into a new construction here.

In 357 the line anchors are exactly those with room for two more primitive
steps but no predecessor in the board. Reflections and coordinate exchange
cover the four relevant slope classes; rows, columns and diagonals already
cannot contain two queens. In particular N(5)=12 and 32+N(16)=2700 agrees
with table (134). The determinant in 358 is the standard collinearity test.
The colored/noncolored items in 360 exclude both kinds of mutual attack
while allowing queens a knight move apart and knights a queen move apart.
Color '0' in an input option is an explicit color, distinct from blank.

The 362 encoding makes each cell pick one color, counts each color in its
row and column, and uses polyomino interiors and halos to enforce maximal
connected components. The displayed 10-by-10 tapestry was checked: each
row and column has counts 1,2,3,4; its ten components of each color have
the required sizes, and all ten size-four components are ell tetrominoes.
For example the first such component is {(0,0),(0,1),(1,0),(2,0)}, matching
the printed option and its boundary zeros. Uniqueness is a separate census.
