# Independent review of the retained geometry candidates

7 November 2026. The main reviewer checked the printed premises, original
pages and the previously prepared witnesses for C0005–C0009. No old verifier
or search was rerun. These are newly accepted conclusions from retained
candidates, not five discoveries made from scratch in this session.

**C0005, confirmed/medium.** Exercise40 asks for sufficient quantitative
conditions for an HC projection. The corrected answer167 still omits triple
crossings away from vertices. The three disjoint tetrahedra in its note have
nonzero volume determinants −42,−40,−84. Near the origin their projected
supporting halfplanes are y≥0, y−x≥0 and y+2x≥0, in successively deeper slabs.
The front edge remains visible in both directions; the two other edges each
retain a different lower ray. Thus four visible rays meet. This contradicts
the explicit degree2/3 requirement. The qualitative rotation-stability premise
on11 would exclude the witness; the proposed quantitative replacement does
not. This is precisely the sufficiency claim requested in the exercise,
not a complaint about an ordinary empty or zero-size object.

**C0006, confirmed/low.** The downward arrow above the lowest inner V of
Fig104(c) is visible in the original crop. Its incident surfaces are the
tetrahedron's face around the triangular hole and the tunnel wall. Their
interiors project to opposite sides of the edge: the hole and tunnel wall
have the same side, while the surrounding face has the opposite side.
The visible edge therefore has two front-facing incident faces, not one.
Its local material sector is convex, so the appropriate label is +. The
original text on10 explicitly says Fig104 labels the objects of Fig101;
this is not merely a different valid labeling of an abstract outline.

**C0007, confirmed/low.** Exercise50 expressly permits an inner line's other
endpoint on the boundary. Its definition of realizable in47 is planar HC
realizability, not an implicit promise of a physical standard labeling.
For the retained eight-vertex polygon with chord ae, the boundary product is
RV³RV³ and its trace is25gg. Indeed (1,1,1,1)V³=(5,5,3,3), which directly
gives that trace. The two g occurrences require opposite directions on the
same chord. They are incompatible as actual line labels. Thus the printed
interpretation of diagonal words needs a repeated-line compatibility check.
This witness does not disprove the final Boolean free-boundary verdict; the
finding remains restricted to the incorrect intermediate interpretation.

**C0008, confirmed/medium.** The other retained polygon, with chord ac, is
simple, biconnected and has no T junction. It satisfies47 and50, and even has
a valid nonstandard labeling. At its reflex boundary corner a the rays to
previous h, next b and inner c have determinants1,2,1 in the chosen downward
y convention. Their order is W(h,b,c), whereas the purported exhaustive list
admits only W(b,c,h). The latter order has the contrary last determinant−1.
Unlike Y, W cannot be cyclically permuted. No hypothesis in50 requires a
standard labeling that would exclude this corner. The six matrices remain
correct for the six configurations actually listed, but the list is incomplete.
