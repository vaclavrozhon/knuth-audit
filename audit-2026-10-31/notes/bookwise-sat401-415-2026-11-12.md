For408(a), the two printed schedules have length11 and the correct durations.
A schedule of length10 would have no idle machine or job, because all six
loads equal10. Its initial positive perfect matching has duration triple
(7,3,2), (7,5,7), (3,2,7), or (3,3,1). Each has a unique earliest finishing
operation, which cannot be followed immediately by another compatible
operation. Thus length10 is impossible. The greedy bound(b) follows because
the final job must be on another machine whenever its last machine is idle.
The disjunctive starting-time constraints, order clauses, reflection symmetry

For409(a), the jobs outside the middle common operation form a partition into
two subsets, and the larger subset gives the stated lower bound. The same
subset also supplies a schedule, with the first common operation at0, the
middle one at floor(A/2), and the last after the A private operations on its

printed one-extra-slot construction and the primary proof were read.
For(c), every diagonal block has length n−2. With a horizon n−2+k, all these
blocks cover the common middle interval, leaving at most2k slots for n−1

slots attains the bound. The printed rounding is floor, not ceiling.
The further open question about a 3s/2 bound is not pursued.
