# D239: Elementary SAT backtracking algorithms

Read main 211–219, questions 119–133 (327–328), and answers 119–133
(572–574). Close PDF pages 231–239, 347, and 592–593; shared pages
348/594 continue into Algorithm L. No new finding in this block.

Conditioning deletes satisfied clauses and removes opposite literals.
On a consistent set of literals the operations commute, and the
recursive procedure (56) partitions the remaining assignments. This
also proves the set expression in 120. For 119, a literal at position
5 occurs in eight arithmetic-progressions clauses, versus four or six
at the other positions up to reflection. Removing those eight and
shortening the eight opposite-polarity clauses gives the listed 24.

Algorithm A maintains nonempty active clauses and puts the smallest
remaining variable at the right end of each clause. Thus A3 can shorten
clauses using SIZE alone. The early-success test C(l)=a satisfies every
active clause with the selected literal; it occurs after setting the
current move code. Unused later variables may be assigned freely.
Pure-literal moves need no opposite branch. In answer 121, an interrupted
A3 restores precisely the prefix of changed sizes. A4 removes all other
cells of each satisfied clause from their literal lists; the final cell
is the chosen literal. A7 reverses the order of clauses. It need not
reverse the order of cells within a clause because their literal lists
are distinct. A8 restores all shortened active clauses. The printed
31-cell example is consistent with these literal/clause conventions.
The move-code interpretation and the branch/contradiction labels in
Fig. 81 were read against the displayed Rivest instance.

The all-solutions variant 122 removes early success and pure-literal
pruning, visits only at depth n+1, and tries both values once at every
level. It consequently no longer needs the occurrence counts. Clause
numbers C(p) on ordinary cells remain necessary.

Algorithm B maintains one nonfalse watched literal per clause. Moving
to an unset value during backtracking cannot falsify a watched literal,
which justifies the absence of undo operations. Answer 124 saves the
next link before transferring a clause. On failure it leaves W at the
unprocessed suffix; on success B4 clears that list. Its parity test
uses the same truth convention as the move codes. Answer 123's initial
watch heads and zero links agree with the first literal of each stored
clause. The all-solutions changes in 125 apply the visiting and branching
logic of 122 while retaining B's watch-list maintenance.

Algorithm D's ring consists exactly of unset variables with a nonempty
watch list. A unit can only occur on such a variable. D3 scans the ring
once or chooses a forced literal; D7 rotates the ring to the predecessor
of the conflicting variable and restores undone variables whose lists
are nonempty. Flipping a genuine branch after these undo operations
is safe for the existing watches: it was not forced when first chosen.
Answer 129 tests every other literal of a watched clause. Answer 130
moves the false watch, adds a newly active unset variable at the ring
head, and handles the empty-ring case explicitly. The scan must find a
replacement because the opposite literal was not a unit. The manually
read traces (59), 126–128 and Fig. 82 agree with the move codes and ring
rotation; the 2016 corrections to the draft are already incorporated.
No separate solver implementation or full trace replay was run.

For 132, the independent equality pairs force the second variable of
each pair after a branch on the first. The contradictory final pair is
reached only after the earlier choices; chronological backtracking
therefore revisits all 2^n choices. The randomized-order growth question
is left as research. For 133, the bottom-up recurrence considers both
values of each possible branching variable in each partial assignment;
min/max choices give optimum/worst trees. The reported 67/471/187 node
counts and the displayed optimum strategy were not exhaustively
regenerated. Runtime/memory benchmark tables on 218–219 and the
heuristic experiment 131 are likewise reported computations, not new
independent measurements.
