## Boundary convention in 86

The reported 80-cell boundary and free inner 8x8 imply a 12x12
predecessor window for a 10x10 target. With that finite convention,
choose a border that leaves all output outside the target dead; the
64 inner cells are then independent of those exterior constraints.
The mean is consequently N*2^64/2^100=N/2^36, as stated. The numerical
BDD count N and its exact boundary implementation are reported data,
not independently regenerated here. On an unrestricted infinite plane
one could add arbitrarily remote dying cells; that is not the finite
window interpretation used for this answer. No new finding is promoted
from the unstated choice of window. Read the author's sat-life-grid
boundary handling as additional context, without treating it as a
reproduction of this BDD calculation.

## Encoding and short counterexamples

87 and (40)–(42): the two players' current states are one-hot. At-most-one
clauses plus the initial state and successor implications suffice; each
command forces some successor. Unscheduled players retain their state,
and variables retain their values outside write commands. Checked all
four command kinds and the complement polarities in the original PDF.
There are 30 transition and 20 next-state exclusion clauses per step,
11 initial and two final units: 13+50r clauses and 11+12r variables.

88's printed six-step counterexample is valid. In 89, Bob reads a=0,
Alice then sets a and enters on l=0, and Bob changes l and enters on
l=1: the printed nine-step trace reaches both critical states. In 90,
strict alternation (43), deadlock (45), and the yielding protocol (46)
all admit the stated fair starvation cycles. Checked the state/flag
changes in those displayed cycles rather than launching new searches.

91 follows from finite states and infinitely many changes of scheduled
player: take repeated states at Alice bumps separated by a Bob bump.
Conversely repeating such a cycle is a fair infinite starvation run.
92's pairwise difference witnesses are correct: one-hot state inequality
has an oriented 1-to-0 witness, and shared bits use XOR witnesses.
However its proposed deletion of a wait clause is wrong. F203 supplies
a fixed five-step path, satisfying every other original clause and all
distinctness constraints, with an illegal final B1-to-B3 jump. Keep the
guard, either by retaining the clause or dropping just its successor.

## Invariants and fairness

The flag invariant (50) gives a=b=0 in program states 0/1 and the
respective flag one in states 2–6. Thus 392 formal states reduce to 98.
It is preserved by every command, and holds initially. The selectors
in 93 express the negation of a conjunctive invariant with the right
signs. In 94, k-induction with the base cases proves the property for
all reachable states. This is the intended safety meaning of invariant;
it need not make the predicate closed under a single step from arbitrary
unreachable states. No semantic failure of the claimed safety proof
is inferred from that terminology shift.

95: a predecessor of simultaneous critical states would need a flag
both zero and one. In 96, the l=0 bad state has only the backward chain
(A5,B3) -> (A5,B4) -> (A5,B5), with a=b=1 throughout. Going back one
more step would require B2 to set l=1, a contradiction; l=1 is symmetric.
Thus the main text's three-step cutoff follows directly. The long
unreachable trace was read, with its persistent flag mismatch; its
claimed longest-simple-path optimum was not recomputed. 97 removes
the two flag-preserving exits from the critical sections, reducing a
hypothetical A6/B6 coexistence to the already excluded A5/B5 case.

98(a): a shortest mixed-player cycle with a repeated state splits into
two shorter cycles; neither can be mixed. Any further repetition could
also be removed, so both are simple and pure. In (b), the excluded
Alice states require a previous maybe/critical state. In (c), g/h are
one-way witnesses that each player has been bumped since time zero;
f selects a repeated state with both witnesses true. The equality
clauses have the correct polarities. The cited longest-path length 15
and solver certificate are accepted reported computations, while the
two pure-loop alternative is ruled out by the A3/A4 and flag transitions.

99: read Dekker's program, the state/flag invariant, the additional
turn and critical-region exclusions, and the fairness reduction. The
extra exclusion of A5/B0/l is needed to prevent apparent starvation
while Bob stays in his maybe state. The claimed length-42 search is
not rerun. The write states A7/B7 cannot coincide because they follow
mutually excluded critical states without dropping their flags.

100: the displayed Bob-starvation cycle is valid. Alice cannot remain
waiting once Bob yields, because his retry requires her flag to clear.
101: read the full four-signal protocol and its two entrance/inner
priority stages. Each player writes only their own two flags; the outer
stage prevents immediate reentry from defeating the inner yielding stage.
The cited Peterson construction is accepted without a new complete
state-graph proof or census. 102 correctly weakens each test guard while
the other player occupies a write state and adds the nondeterministic
successor disjunction, preserving exactly one successor state.

The main text's sequential-consistency qualification is essential and
is explicitly retained. This review makes no claim about deploying the
protocols on a particular hardware memory model.
