169: the function exp(-ax)+exp(-bx)-1 is positive before its unique
root, strictly decreasing and convex. The initial 1/(a+b) is the first
Newton iterate from zero and remains below the root. When K3 orders
the two lower approximations, a nonpositive value of the first function
at the second approximation certifies which root is smaller. Otherwise
both Newton updates remain lower bounds and improve them. Swapping
j and k can cause outer indices to be revisited; this does not itself
supply an incorrect selected minimizer. No extra complexity claim or
exact-real termination claim for tied roots is made; the text explicitly
uses finite-precision arithmetic. The wording of the K2 prefix invariant
should be read with the current comparison history after such swaps.

For the random-clause model, there are N=2^k binomial(n,k) clauses,
and every assignment satisfies (1-2^-k)N of them. The n=5 plots and
reported probabilities, solution counts and stopping-time statistics
were read, without rebuilding their large BDD bases. Tail summation
gives E P=sum q_m and E P^2=sum(2m+1)q_m. Grouping a length-m word by
its t distinct clauses gives the Stirling-number transform (77).
The generating-function identities in 186 give (78)–(79); repeated
clauses can only delay failure, as proved directly in 185.
