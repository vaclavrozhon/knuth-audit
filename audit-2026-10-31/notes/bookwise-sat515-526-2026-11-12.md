## Result

**F254:** Answer 525, p. 654, needs **5n variables**, not 3n. Each of the
5n exact-cover options has one Boolean variable. Its other size counts
are consistent. The original 2015 draft contains the same mistake.

**Existing F064 retained:** The hard-sudoku condition 515(vi) needs the
two-box restriction. HARD_SUDOKU_NOTES.md and its fixed counterexample,
four puzzle checks and complete encoding counts remain the evidence.
The candidate-cell encoding is sound: an actual completion supplies at
least one candidate whenever one is needed; excluding exactly one then
means at least two. Symmetry and conditions (i)–(v) were read with the answer.

## Arguments reviewed

- Main 313–317: parallel portfolios, cube-and-conquer, the resolution
  examples and the historical account read. The standard complexity
  results and cited history are accepted; no bibliography reconstruction
  or historical performance rerun. Known 2023 dot-weight correction on
  p. 313 and replacement exercise 5 on p. 317 are not new findings.
- 516 is an explicitly open strong-exponential-time question. Its answer
  and qualifications were read; no attempt to settle the conjecture.
- 517: the two general reductions and the short-equation gadget agree
  with S517 / HARD_SUDOKU_NOTES.md. The previously checked gadget truth
  tables and equation-splitting checks are reused.
- 518: the printed permanent gadget identity agrees with A002's exact
  polynomial check. The general composition argument was now checked:
  if r input ports and the same r output ports of a clause are selected,
  the corresponding minor has permanent 16/2^r for r>0, and zero for
  r=0 or unmatched port sets. This follows by comparing coefficients
  in (a). A nonzero global selection consequently follows one complete
  literal chain per variable and meets every clause. Its external weight
  is 2^(n+sum r), so the total contribution is 16^m*2^n per satisfying
  assignment. This also covers unsatisfiable formulas, without a new
  numerical permanent computation. The row/column counts in (b) agree.
  Here n naturally counts variables occurring in the formula. C001's
  contrary convention is not promoted to an error. The 46 by 46
  illustration was inspected, but not independently transcribed cell
  by cell; the general construction and gadget, not that transcription,
  establish correctness. Known F6 matrix/parameter errata are accounted for.
- 519–521: the integer-program inequalities in 520 are equivalent to
  the clauses, and the historical comparisons retain their stated scope.
  The F6 correction of 21 June 2022 changes “Forget” to “Keep” in 521;
  the 4B copy still prints “Forget”, but this is a known correction and
  is not counted as new. The 2023 bibliography update for answer 521 is
  also known. No solver competition or tuning experiment was repeated.
- 522–524: SHADOW_PATHS_NOTES.md supplies the two independent complete
  enumerations, all six printed delta sequences, the three encoding
  counts, and the general obstruction for Pm x Pn x P2. These were read
  and reused; the printed encodings and geometric argument agree.
- 525: the Tseytin parity count is 50*8=400 clauses on 100 edges.
  The apparent difficulty at the exceptional odd vertex is resolved by
  the author's archived sat-eulerian-balance.w: complement one incident
  literal there and impose exactly two of four signed literals at every
  vertex. The four positive and four negative triples give 8 ternary
  clauses per vertex, hence 400 clauses and 1200 literals, with odd
  parity only at the exceptional vertex. No program was executed.
  Markström's cited paper, Section 2, supplies the related subdivided-edge
  construction; the author's source makes the omitted implementation
  detail explicit. Historical timings and symmetry-breaking counts
  were not reproduced. The separate matching size error is F254.
- 526: put W+ = sum 2^(-|C|) over clauses containing the chosen literal,
  and W- over those containing its complement. Restriction changes
  w(F) by W- - W+, which is nonpositive by the stated choice. At least
  one clause disappears, validating induction on the number of clauses;
  the all-empty base case is correct. Thus w(F)<=m/2^k gives the claim.
