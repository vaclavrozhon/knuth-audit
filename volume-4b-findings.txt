TAOCP Volume 4B: working corrections

These 274 notes come from an AI-assisted audit. I have checked only some
of them myself; they are suggestions for review, not author-confirmed errata.
The source is the first edition, second printing (2022). Page numbers refer
to printed pages. The notes are grouped by topic, with exercise statements
and answers kept together.
Detailed evidence is in findings/; data/volume-4b-findings-index.json maps
the item numbers below to those files. Updated after the November 17 audit.

Mathematical preliminaries

1. Mathematical Preliminaries Redux, p. 9, sentence after (38)

History-dependent interval endpoints need a fixed upper bound v on the sum
of squared widths; the valid tail bound is then exp(−2x^2/v). For a
counterexample to the unrestricted statement, let (Y_1,Y_2) be (1,0),
(−1,2), (−1,−3) with probabilities 1/2,3/10,1/5, respectively; both
increments have conditional mean zero. Take the first interval as [−1,1],
and the second as [−1/4,1/4] when Y_1=1 and [−3,2] otherwise. Then
P(Y_1+Y_2≥1)=4/5, but even the average proposed bound,
(exp(−8/17)+exp(−2/29))/2, is less than 11106/14167<4/5.

2. Mathematical Preliminaries Redux, answer 11, p. 372

The tie event must be |X_1−alpha|=|X_2−alpha|, not X_1=X_2. For independent
fair signs with alpha=0, the distance comparison has probability 1, but
(1+P(X_1=X_2))/2=3/4. The smallest repair is to change the printed equality
to ≥; alternatively keep equality and use the equal-distance event. The
argument then proves the stated conclusion.

3. Mathematical Preliminaries Redux, answer 26 (p. 374)

The statement that Newton's coefficient inequalities were finally proved by
Sylvester in 1865 should credit Maclaurin's 1729 proof for the same-sign
real-root case needed here. Sylvester's cited paper concerns the stronger
root-counting rule and distinguishes it from the earlier coefficient
criterion. The mathematical argument in the answer is unaffected.

4. Answer MPR 28(b), p. 374

The endpoint choice should be epsilon=-min(p_(n-1),1-p_n), not
delta=-min(p_(n-1),1-p_n). For the two probabilities 1/4 and 3/4,
epsilon=-1/4 changes their product by delta=-3/16. Substituting -1/4 for
delta would make a probability negative.

5. Mathematical Preliminaries Redux, answer 38, p. 376

Remove X_0 from the final conditioning list. The restricted growth string
and the specified prefix start at X_1; an added variable X_0 is undefined
and could, if supplied arbitrarily, change the conditional expectation.

6. Mathematical Preliminaries Redux, answer 67(c), p. 380

For the profit after n trials, sum Y_0 through Y_(n−1), not through Y_n. Two
initial losses make the printed expression at n=1 equal −3, although the
profit after one trial is −1.

7. Mathematical Preliminaries Redux, answer 68(d), p. 380

The tail is asymptotic to sqrt(2/(pi n)), not 1/sqrt(pi n). The preceding
exact expression is binomial(2k,k)/4^k with k=floor(n/2), whose asymptotic
gives the missing factor sqrt(2).

8. Mathematical Preliminaries Redux, answer 74, p. 381

The level-n probability should be A(n+1,r−1)/(n+1)!, with A denoting the
Eulerian numbers, rather than A(n,r−1)/n!. At level 1 the diagram gives
probabilities 1/2,1/2, while the printed formula gives 1,0. The following
permutation explanation also uses n+1 elements.

9. Mathematical Preliminaries Redux, answer 77, p. 382

The parenthetical example of a time-constant auxiliary sequence does not
work for every martingale. Let Z_0=0 and Z_n=xi for n≥1, where xi is a fair
±1 sign; this is a bounded martingale in its own history. If X_n=Y at every
time, adaptedness would make xi determined by Y, giving E(Z_1|Y)=xi rather
than Z_0=0. One can use the martingale’s own history instead of a
time-constant helper.

10. Mathematical Preliminaries Redux, exercise 82(a), p. 19

The requested transition range should end at n=50, not n=51. The transition
at n=51 would require Z_52, whose displayed ratio is 0/0 for every deck.
Alternatively, an explicit constant continuation after the last defined term
would be needed.

11. Mathematical Preliminaries Redux, answer 83, p. 382

The sum should have summand X_k−E(X_k), not X_n−E(X_n). As printed it is
n(X_n−E(X_n)), which need not be a martingale: take X_1 to be a fair 0/1
variable and all later X_n=0. The intended partial sum uses each centered
increment once.

12. Mathematical Preliminaries Redux, answer 84(a), p. 382

Conditioning on the full Z history is not the same as conditioning on the
transformed history. Take f(z)=z^2 and (Z_0,Z_1)=(-1,-1),(1,0),(1,2) with
probabilities 1/2,1/4,1/4, then keep the process constant: E(Z_1^2|Z_0) is 1
or 2, whereas E(Z_1^2|Z_0^2)=3/2. After applying conditional Jensen,
condition both sides once more on the transformed history. This proves the
intended submartingale claim without the false equality.

13. Mathematical Preliminaries Redux, answer 90(e), p. 383

In each factor of the displayed product, change exp(c_k^2 t^2/4) to
exp(c_k^2 t^2/8). Since c=sum(c_k^2)/2, the printed constant terms multiply
to exp(ct^2/2), not the claimed exp(ct^2/4). The final exponent ct^2/4 and
the resulting fixed-width tail bound should remain unchanged.

14. Mathematical Preliminaries Redux, answer 96, p. 384

Center the binomial variable in the window: P(|X−n/2|<sqrt(n))=Theta(1).
Here X counts heads, so the printed P(|X|<sqrt(n)) tends to zero; for n≥16
it is at most exp(−n/8). The final classification does not change.

15. Mathematical Preliminaries Redux, answer 103(b), p. 385

Change g_112 to g_122 in the first transition from state 012: input bit 1
sends that state to 122. For output 2, the word 11 has probability 1/4,
whereas the printed recurrence gives coefficient [z^2]=0. The final
generating function already uses the correct transition.

16. Mathematical Preliminaries Redux, answer 109(c), p. 386, network arcs

The arcs to remove are x_i→y_j, not x_i→x_j. The middle arcs of the stated
network join the x layer to the y layer; deleting the named x-to-x arcs
leaves forbidden couplings available. Even on a two-element chain, the
resulting full crossed flow can violate the required order.

17. Mathematical Preliminaries Redux, answer 109(c), p. 386, cut proof

Use the lower closure A={x: x≤a for some a outside J}, not the upper
closure, and put B=P\A. The accompanying closure statement should be A=A↓ or
B=B↑, so that the lower-set inequality p(A)≥q(A) applies. On the chain
{1<2}, with p=(3/4,1/4), q=(1/4,3/4), I={1,2}, J={1}, the printed choice
makes the asserted chain of inequalities 5/4≥1/2≥1. The corrected closures
give the intended cut argument.

18. Mathematical Preliminaries Redux, answer 111(b), p. 387

The sketch values belong to the image of A∪B under the permutation, not
necessarily to A∪B itself. For A=B={1} and permutation 21, both sketches
equal 2. Replace the union by its image, or apply the inverse permutation to
the sketches; the stated collision probability is unchanged.

19. Mathematical Preliminaries Redux, answer 112(b), p. 388

In each of the first, third, and fourth tableaux, replace entries 3,9,12 by
1032,3210,0321, respectively, using 0 for a blank; the printed entries are
1302,0213,3021. They belong to the other branch and do not preserve the
chosen placements. Completing the first printed branch makes position 2 beat
position 3 in seven of twelve permutations instead of six, so these are not
just harmless reorderings. The second tableau and the general construction
are correct.

20. Mathematical Preliminaries Redux, notes after exercise 113 (p. 388)

The bound attributed to the 1896 paper has the wrong error term. With that
reference, write psi(n)=n+o(n); the later 1899 result gives an error with
exp(-c sqrt(log n)), rather than exp(-C log n). This corrects the statement
of the cited result, without asserting that the stronger formula is false.

21. Mathematical Preliminaries Redux, answer 113(a), p. 388

The auxiliary probability is 1/(n−k+1+t), not 1/(n−k+t), because {k,…,n}
together with t smaller elements has n−k+1+t elements. At n=5, k=4, the
printed inclusion–exclusion sum is 1/4, whereas the event occurs in 6 of the
120 permutations, giving 1/20. Inserting the missing +1 gives that value.

22. Mathematical Preliminaries Redux, answer 116, p. 389

The top coefficient is (−1)^(m+1), not (−1)^m, since the product of the
(1−x_e) terms is subtracted. With five parallel edges over the field of
three elements, the coefficient is 1 rather than 2. It is still nonzero, so
the existence argument is unaffected.

23. MPR exercise 121(d), p. 24

The entropy formula should be E lg(1/y(Y)), not E lg(1/Y). If Y is always 2,
its entropy is zero, while the printed expression gives -1.

24. MPR exercises 121 and 124, pp. 24–25; answer 121(b), p. 390

The expectation identities require y(t)=0 whenever x(t)=0. If X is always 0
and Y is uniform on {0,1}, then E(rho(X) lg rho(X))=-1/2, while the
divergence sum is infinite, and E rho(X)=1/2 rather than 1. The introductory
identity in exercise 124 also fails for f=1: its two sides are 1 and 1/2.
The finite-divergence assumptions in the later theorem already imply the
missing support condition.

25. MPR exercise 123, p. 25

The mixture should be z_k(t)=p_k y(t)+(1-p_k)x(t), because A selects Y and
Pr(A)=p_k. For x=(3/4,1/4), y=(1/4,3/4), and p_k=1/4, the correct mixture is
(5/8,3/8), while the printed formula gives (3/8,5/8). The later Bayes
calculation uses the intended weights.

26. Answer MPR 124, p. 390

The proof needs the triangle inequality with absolute values on all three
terms, followed by expectations; the sample tail bound is not a pointwise
bound. It also needs an expectation before identifying squared error with
variance. For x=y=(1/2,1/2), f=(0,1), and n=2, the squared error is 0 or
1/4, while its expectation is the variance 1/8. These are errors in the
proof; the theorem with the published statement correction remains true.

27. Mathematical Preliminaries Redux, answer 125(e), p. 391

Change the reference from part (c) to part (d). The induction adds two
log-convex sequences, and closure under addition is the result proved in
(d).

28. Answer MPR 127(b), p. 391

The rounding of theta*n leaves a bounded term that cannot be included in
O(1/n). Add -{theta*n} lg((1-theta)/theta), where braces denote the
fractional part, or replace the remainder by O(1). For theta=1/3, the
omitted term tends to -1/3 along n=3k+1 but is zero along n=3k. The final
order estimate is unchanged.

29. Answer MPR 130(e), p. 393

In the joint-density factor, (z-q*y/p)^2 should be ((z-q*y)/p)^2: p divides
the whole difference z-q*y. For p=q=1/2, z=1, y=0, the two expressions are 1
and 4.

30. Answer MPR 130(e), p. 393

The value of the integral should be pi/[p(1+z^2)], not 1/[p(1+z^2)]. With
p=q=1/2 and z=0, the integral is 4 times the integral of (1+y^2)^(-2), hence
2*pi rather than 2. The final Cauchy distribution is correct.

31. Answer MPR 132(a), p. 393

The second upper parameter of the hypergeometric function should be -n
rather than -N. For N=4, K=2, n=1, the printed expression is 1/2+2z+z^2,
whereas the probability generating function is (1+z)/2.

32. Answer MPR 132(b), p. 393

The variance needs a factor K: n*K*(N-n)*(N-K)/(N^3-N^2). With N=4, K=2,
n=1, the number of green balls drawn is 0 or 1 with equal probabilities, so
its variance is 1/4. The printed formula gives 1/8.

33. MPR Exercise 134(b) (pp. 27 and 394)

On p. 27, the bound should be 2 exp(-epsilon^2 m/2), rather than
2 exp(-2 epsilon^2 m). With m=1, epsilon=0.9, and two independent
samples from a fair coin, the empirical frequencies differ with
probability 1/2, but the printed bound is about 0.396.

In the answer on p. 394, the tail threshold should be epsilon*m/2
instead of epsilon*m. With this change, the proof gives the bound
above, which is enough for part (c).

34. Answer MPR 137(d), p. 395

For x<m, the expression should be c-m+2 max(0,x-c). With m=1, c=0, x=-1, the
difference of distances is -1, while the printed expression gives -3. The
following lower bound and the theorem remain correct.

35. Mathematical Preliminaries Redux, answer 140(a), p. 396

For fixed 0<p<1 and q=1−p, the expected number of unmarked records is
2q/p+2q/(p^2 n)+O(n^(-2)), not 2q/p+O(q^n/n). At p=q=1/2, the excess above 2
is at least 1/(4n) for n≥16, already excluding an exponentially small
remainder. The cited Banderier–Beier–Mehlhorn paper also includes the
2q/(p^2 n) term (https://lipn.univ-paris13.fr/~cb/Papers/smoothed.pdf). The
preceding estimate for marked records is unchanged.

36. Mathematical Preliminaries Redux, answer 140(e), p. 396

The comparison P(*)≤P(**) fails for fifty zeros followed by fifty ones,
epsilon=1/4, and k=51, for which m=5 and Delta_k=1. Since the noise delta_k
lies in [−1/4,1/4], event (*) always holds and (**) never holds, giving 1≤0.
Instead bound the events separately: P(*)=min(Delta_k/(2 epsilon),1) and
P(**)≤1/(m+1) for k>m. These bounds give the intended final estimate.

37. Mathematical Preliminaries Redux, answer 145, p. 397

The denominator in the coefficient ratio should be product from j=1 to n of
(2k_j−1)!!, not the product through m. There is one factor per coordinate.
For n=3, m=2, and (k_1,k_2,k_3)=(0,0,2), the printed ratio is 3 while the
actual ratio is 1; including the third factor gives 1.

Backtracking: search and enumeration

38. §7.2.2, p. 36, trie description

The prefix-length range should be 0≤l<n, not 0≤l≤n. Full words are already
represented by the ranks on the final links, so they need no 26-entry nodes.
The stated 23,667 nodes correctly counts proper prefixes; including full
six-letter words would give 39,394.

39. Main text, first paragraph, p. 37

After SLUMS and TOTAL, 62 words in WORDS(5757) satisfy the five printed
letter restrictions, not 221. Intersecting those five restrictions gives the
corrected count.

40. Algorithm C, pp. 45–46

The algorithm visits codes with at least g words, not exactly g words as
stated. With m=2,g=2, it visits fifteen two-word codes and fourteen
three-word codes. Either change the description to "at least g", or visit a
code only when the remaining slack s is zero.

41. Answer 7.2.2–5, p. 398

The recursive loop must run over values in the domain, not over every
integer between its minimum and maximum. With one variable, domain {1,3},
and an always-true predicate, the printed loop visits the forbidden value 2.
The nonrecursive Algorithm B handles this domain correctly.

42. Answer 7.2.2–22, p. 400

The loose-Langford modification needs two more backtracking checks: reject
conflicting symmetry and deadline choices, and also put "if ell=h and k=n',
go to L5" at the start of L4. For n=6, the first missing check makes the
algorithm output 13 representatives rather than 10. For n=10, the reachable
prefix (1,-1,9) forces label 8 into an occupied second slot, and the scan
repeats forever between labels 8 and 10. The added L4 check handles this
failed placement; the existing L5 change handles only a placement that had
succeeded.

43. Answer 7.2.2–27, p. 401

The memory-access count should be about 1.83 Tμ, not 18.3 Tμ. A completed
run of the unmodified author program used 1,828,460,227,444 accesses and
produced the stated 625,415 solutions, profile, and mean domain sizes. Even
a conservative bound from the printed profile is below 6.636 Tμ, so the
factor-of-ten discrepancy does not depend only on that run.

44. Answer 7.2.2–32, p. 402, second Strand citation

Change Strand 56 (1919), 74 to Strand 56 (1918), 74. The volume covers
July–December 1918, and page 74 contains the cited NESTLES word square.

45. Answer 7.2.2–33, p. 402

The two word-length labels are exchanged. The trie with 6347 nodes belongs
to the five-letter words, and the compressed trie with 63060 entries belongs
to the six-letter words. The numerical sizes themselves are correct.

46. Answer 7.2.2–38, p. 404

For n=9, the probability should be 1-3*(r^3-r)/(m^9-m^3), where r=(m^3-m)/3,
not 1-3/binom(m^3-1,3). For m=2, exactly 486 of the 504 aperiodic words
finish in one round, giving 27/28 rather than 32/35. The generating function
immediately above the probability is correct.

47. Answer 7.2.2–44(b), p. 404

The selection should use the class c chosen in part (a): if r=0, set x<- -1;
otherwise set x<-MEM[CLOFF+4c]. The printed expression instead uses the
class of the old x. For m=2,g=3, after choices 0001 and 0011 it returns the
already selected 0011, rather than the sole available word 0111 in the new
class. The BACK-COMMAFREE4 code already uses the selected class correctly.

Backtracking: estimates and random walks

48. §7.2.2, p. 49, binomial-tree example

For n=100, the probability that the termination level l is at least 20 is
1.9519870726687698 × 10^(-8). The printed 2.7 × 10^(-9) belongs to l≥21, so
either the threshold or the probability should change.

49. Answer 7.2.2–49, p. 406

For a single uniform choice among five outcomes, starting without saved
random state, 10/3 fresh fair bits on average is not enough. Just the
initial terms of the bit-count lower bound sum to 27/8, already larger than
10/3. The exact optimum for one choice is 18/5, achieved by recycling
rejected bit patterns within that choice. This does not rule out lower
average costs when saved random state is reused across several choices.

50. Answer 7.2.2–53, p. 406

The second-moment update needs +2*c(x_1...x_(ell-1))*COST[ell] rather than
+2*COST[ell]. A tree consisting of a root and one child, both of cost 2,
always gives estimate 4, but the printed recurrence gives second moment 12
and variance -4 instead of zero. The general recurrence above it is correct.

51. Answer 7.2.2–54, p. 407

In the last factor of the path probability, the conditioning prefix must end
at x_(ell-1), not x_ell. The next choice is made from the parent node; the
printed distribution can be undefined at the resulting leaf. The weighting
rule itself is unchanged.

52. Answer 7.2.2–58, p. 407

Move the return label (*) past “Set u←T, T←S←Λ”, to the following loop over
the neighbors of u. Otherwise returning to (*) overwrites the next vertex
and clears the lists being built. For the star with edges 01,12,13 and
target 0, deleting vertex 1 gives a layer containing vertices 3 and 2; after
processing 3, the return replaces vertex 2 by Λ. The February 2025
correction does not move this label; WALK-RANDOM already has the intended
loop structure.

The subtraction of 1 in h(w,z) belongs outside the whole product:

The sample group has a=2, not a=1, since its top-row queen is in column 2.

64. Answer 7.2.2.1--24 (pp. 420--421)

Both filters at the end of the answer remove all solutions when n=4.
The two solutions are 2413 and 3142, where the digits give the columns
of the queens in successive rows. Both are unchanged by a 90-degree
rotation, but neither survives the printed filters.

For the 180-degree case, discard an option only if its actual queen
in the first row is in a column k>n/2. The row and column items in a
merged option can come from different queens, so their presence alone
is not enough to make this test. For the 90-degree case, keep only
one copy of each set of items instead of using the printed filter.

65. Exercise 7.2.2.1–32(d), p. 127; answer p. 422

The hypothesis should say that every option has the same positive number of
primary items. Equal total size is insufficient: with primary a,b and
secondary x,y, the options ab, ax, by all have size 2, but their two
solutions are 100 and 011, at Hamming distance 3. The answer already uses
the stronger primary-item condition, under which every solution has the same
number of options.

66. Answer 7.2.2.1–37(a), p. 423

The relation should be a[q_i,i]=1, with the indices exchanged. Already
q_2=3, while the displayed recurrence gives a[2,3]=5 and a[3,2]=1. The
assertion that the queen sequence is a permutation is unchanged.

67. Answer 7.2.2.1–39(b), p. 424; exercise p. 127

Since sampling is with replacement, repeated options give separate indexed
solutions. For n=kr, put N=binomial(n,r), P=n!/(k!(r!)^k), and let
(m)_k=m(m−1)…(m−k+1); the expected number of solutions is P(m)_k/N^k, not
the probability of seeing a partition multiplied by P. With n=2, r=1, m=3,
the correct mean is 3/2 rather than 3/4: the particular input a,a,b has two
indexed solutions. The change in the large numerical examples is small.

72. Answer 7.2.2.1–57, p. 427, normalization count

After fixing the first box and the first row 1472AB3CD as stated, there are
432 solutions, not 864. The 864 count comes from a weaker condition that
allows 2 and 3 to exchange the two right stacks; swapping those stacks pairs
the solutions, with exactly one member meeting the printed first-row
condition. Thus the count should be halved, or the normalization should be
weakened.

73. Answer 7.2.2.1–57, p. 427, isomorphism claim

The claim that all these solutions are equivalent by row and column
permutations also needs to allow a renaming of the digits. Without that
operation there are six classes: each has 144 members under the weaker
normalization, or 72 under the stated one. For example, one solution has the
digit transition (1 2 3)(4 6 5)(7 8 9) between two rows, a transition absent

270. Answer 7.2.2.2–509 (p. 650)

Fresh random solver seeds do not rule out overfitting to the chosen problem
instances. For example, one parameter setting may cost 1+U on the training
problems and 100+U elsewhere, while another costs 2+U everywhere, with a
fresh uniform random U in [0,1] on every run. Rerandomizing U cannot reveal
the first setting's poor performance on new problems. The justification
should distinguish seed noise from performance on held-out instances; this
is not evidence that the reported measurements actually overfit.
