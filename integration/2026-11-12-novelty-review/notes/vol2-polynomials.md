# Volume 2: polynomial arithmetic and factorization

Publication review, 12 November 2026. Scope: the 21 baseline-confirmed cards below. C0192 was separately withdrawn after a fresh render disproved its transcription; see `vol2-c0192-dismissal.md`.

The first-printing originals were read together with their exercises and definitions: pp. 421–426, 428–429, 433–438, 456–460, 672–678, 680–681, and 686–689. Algorithm M's optional zero-digit test was checked on p. 268. The rendered August 2001 amendment on historical errata p. 43 was also inspected. Existing proof notes, draft reports and small counterexamples were checked; successful large experiments were not repeated.

## Decisions

All 21 cards below are retained, with the indicated limits. No matching prior public report was found in this review. This is a search result, not a claim of priority.

| V2-C0209 | Exercise 31 explicitly permits d=1, but its two counts differ by one in that case: (p−1)/2 versus (p−3)/2. For p=3 the counts are one and zero. The fractional-linear proof works for d>=2; the d=1 exception needs exclusion or separate treatment. |
| V2-C0210 | Answer 37 calls an asymptotic expression exact. The exact count uses multisets: p^(−n) times the product of binomial(a_j+k_j−1,k_j). For p=2 and two linear factors it is 3/4, not 1/2. The limiting permutation-cycle law is unaffected. |
| V2-C0211 | Choosing t of degree below rd makes CRT coordinates independent, but the resulting split bits have probability (p^d−1)/(2p^d), not 1/2. | V2-C0212 | The second modulus in the first line of p. 687 must be q2. Over F3, q1=x²+1, q2=x²+x+2 and t=(x+2)q1 give t1=0,t2=x, but t2⁴ is 1 modulo q1 and −1 modulo q2. The printed test predicts a split where the actual gcd is 1. |

## Algorithm C's time bound

For C0202 the existing exact proof was read, including intermediate coefficients inside a pseudodivision, not merely the final subresultants. Put y=x^D, W=1+...+x^(D−1), A=y⁴+y³+y²+1 and B=y³−y²−y+1. Inputs W(A+B), WA are monic of degree 5D−1 and coefficient bound 2. Their normalized sequence contains W(4y²+y−1) followed by 4^(D−1)W(−7y+11). In the next pseudodivision, a=−7·4^(D−1) is repeatedly multiplied into low coefficients −a^(D−k). A rectangle of Theta(D²) such products has operand lengths Theta(D) and Theta(D²), hence Omega(D⁵) digit-pair operations. The same rectangle survives R2's stated low-index optimization. C3 removes the enormous common factor only afterwards. The assertion is specifically for dense classical multiplication, permitted by p. 268's explicit optionality of M2. Skipping zero digits or retaining powers symbolically can invalidate this lower bound and is not ruled out.

[Collins's 1969 analysis](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/19710009710/downloads/19710009710.pdf), printed pp. 209–213, explicitly assumes weak normality in Theorems 3.13/3.14 and Corollary 3.17. [Brown's 1978 paper](https://people.eecs.berkeley.edu/~fateman/282/readings/brown.pdf), pp. 245–249, explicitly calls its model qualitative, uses operand-size products, and distinguishes normal fourth-degree behavior from the general fifth-degree bound. Neither was treated as a newly discovered phenomenon or as a located public report of this particular book exercise. These sources were accessed again; the local full texts support the citations.

## Errata and public-report search

All four applicable third-edition layers, `all2-prepre`, `all2-pre`, `all2`, and `err2`, were compared using the current November 12 source refresh. URLs and hashes are in `../sources/manifest.json`. The 2022 answer-16 change fixes an index in an example, not C0194's denominator. The 2002 p. 680 change fixes a closing parenthesis, not the exponent. The 2001 exercise-29 change corrects the lower probability bound, not C0211's final fair-bit claim or C0212's modulus. The 2022 answer-12 rewrite, 1999 answer-31 alpha correction, and other adjacent corrections do not repair the cards above. C0208 is specifically a defect in the replacement text itself.

Searches combined Knuth/TAOCP with section 4.6.1/4.6.2, page numbers 673–689, irreducible constants, U/V gcd, Brown equal degrees, subresultant time bounds, abnormal degree drops, quadratic-factor probabilities, negative generating-function exponents, square roots and the 2001 erratum, Cantor–Zassenhaus, discriminants, and exact factorization probabilities. No exact prior report was located. Search coverage is necessarily incomplete.

Three relevant leads were followed rather than counted automatically as prior reports. [Rote's generalized Nullstellensatz survey](https://page.mi.fu-berlin.de/rote/Papers/pdf/The%2BGeneralized%2BCombinatorial%2BLason-Alon-Zippel-Schwartz%2BNullstellensatz%2BLemma.pdf), especially Theorem 2 and its discussion of Knuth's answer 16, concerns the sound root bound; it does not identify the printed denominator typo. [Divasón, Joosten, Thiemann and Yamada](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10817-019-09526-y) report a degree-bound bug in their own earlier oracle implementation, not these book formulas. [Brent's 2021 error survey](https://maths-people.anu.edu.au/~brent/pd/rpb279-final.pdf), section 4.1, concerns an old binary-gcd constant settled before the third edition, not this polynomial block. The PMC full-text endpoint presented a CAPTCHA; the publisher and indexed author article supplied the relevant context.

No author has been contacted. The latest physical printing was not independently inspected, and absolute novelty is not established.
