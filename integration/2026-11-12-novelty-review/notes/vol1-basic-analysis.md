# Publication review: Volume 1, elementary analysis findings

Reviewed on 12 November 2026 against the May 1997 first-printing scan and the freshly downloaded official correction layers. The new official files are byte-identical to the previously inspected Volume 1 archive; see ../sources/manifest.json. The checked layers are all1-prepre, all1-pre, all1, err1 and err1.textxt. Results below concern correctness and the absence of a matching report in the searched sources, not proof of priority or author acceptance.

- **V1-C0002 — retain.** The section allows positive bases, whereas corrected Algorithm E still fails for b=1/2, epsilon=a=1/4: its exact first table entry is -1 and the working residual increases indefinitely. The 2002/2011 initial-value repairs do not add b>1. Sean Werkema's public account (2021) describes that initial-value error, not this base restriction. The original exercise and answer were reread; the failure is independent of numerical rounding.
- **V1-C0003 — retain, errata only.** Exercise 26 does not restrict the factors to positive numbers. In the 2020 replacement, n=1, a_0=-1, a_1=1 gives P1=P2=-1 and their product's principal real square root +1. The final product identity is correct, as is the original answer; only the added proof step loses a sign.
- **V1-C0006 — retain.** The printed q-binomial extension explicitly allows real n. For q=1/2, n=-1, x=3/4 the summands are (-3/2)^k while the product telescopes to 2/5. The 2003 and 2023 amendments do not repair the convergence condition. The positive, nonsingular parameters rule out a branch-choice objection.
- **V1-C0007 — retain with a source limitation.** The displayed lower-index-first iterated limit gives binom(-1,-1)=0. With all four parameters r,s,alpha,beta equal to -1/2, the bilateral sum has just the nonzero k=0 term, equal to 1. Its convergence is absolute. The source page and the stated limiting convention were reread. No matching correction/report was located. Ramshaw's cited 1977 paper could not be obtained during this review; its own precise hypotheses and any discussion there remain unchecked. This finding concerns the explicit statement in TAOCP, not an allegation about Ramshaw's paper.
- **V1-C0008 — retain, combined errata.** The 2005 proof and the 2015 endpoint amendment were reread together. With n=2, y=z=1, x=2, its Y is zero and the allegedly strict ratios are both zero. Weak inequalities at those two steps still give the desired strict derivative comparison. This is a local defect in the proof, not a false theorem.
- **V1-C0011 — retain, errata only.** The 2024 addition asserts existence for Re(s)>0. With coefficients a_k=2^k and s=log(2), the integral to integer N equals (N-1+2^(-N))/log(2), which diverges. The original exercise did not contain this existence assertion. Positive real part together with exp(-Re(s)) inside the generating function's radius is a sufficient correction.
- **V1-C0012 — retain as a small omitted qualification.** The original answer displays a family with arbitrary C, then attributes the asymptotic series to that stated function without fixing C. On z=-1/u, u tending to positive infinity, its extra term is C*u*exp(u). Only C=0 gives even the leading term 1. This is a normalization omission, not an error in the differential equation or its solution. The correct intended choice is readily recoverable; severity remains low.
- **V1-C0013 — retain.** The original bound expressly quantifies over all n and x. At n=2 the standardized absolute deviation is always 1, so x=1/2 contradicts exp(-a*x) for every a>0. This is not just an asymptotic-small-n objection. A multiplicative constant repairs the statement; the cited Flajolet–Soria definition already includes one. The only located correction to this paragraph deletes a closing bracket.
- **V1-C0014 — retain as a minor proof-wording error.** General probability generating functions may have a nonzero constant coefficient and infinite support. For f(z)=(1+z)/2, the constant coefficient of g(f(z)) depends on all coefficients q_j of g through sum q_j*2^(-j). It is not a polynomial in finitely many q_j; two sufficiently high monomials of g distinguish it. The probabilistic conclusion remains true. Calling this an algorithmic failure would overstate the finding.
- **V1-C0015 — retain, errata only.** The fresh 2023 replacement really omits n from the first exponent and says the strengthened estimate holds for all p. Both issues survive reading the preceding p>=1/2 qualification, which applied to the earlier estimate. Even after inserting n, n=8,p=1/4,epsilon=1/2 gives the exact upper tail 277/65536, larger than 2^(-8)=256/65536. The weaker Hoeffding estimate is correct; the stronger series needs restricted p. The source TeX and original typesetting agree.
- **V1-C0016 — retain as a typo.** In the original answer, v=u-log(1+u) tends to infinity with positive u. Thus 1+1/u tends to 1, not 0. Its boundedness, and consequently the conclusion, are unaffected.
