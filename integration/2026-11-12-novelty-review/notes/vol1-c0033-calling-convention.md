# V1-C0033: downgrade to candidate after checking the calling convention

Publication review, 12 November 2026. The execution observation is correct: the added CMPA in answer 1.4.4-12 changes the comparison indicator, whereas original CONTROL does not. The saved two-card reproduction needs no rerun. That establishes a behavioral difference, but does not by itself establish an error under the book's calling convention.

Page 188 explicitly says that changes to register J and the comparison indicator are customarily omitted from subroutine specifications. The same paragraph distinguishes these from ordinary registers, which must be mentioned if affected. Thus V1-C0031's omitted I1 change and this case have different contractual status. Pages 197–198 describe each coroutine as being written as though the other were its subroutine, and require the two entry/exit conditions to fit, with saving/restoring added when needed. These passages were read in their original page images during this review.

Pages 222–223 recommend interspersing JRED CONTROL calls at intervals and give the current code; they do not explicitly promise preservation of the comparison indicator across an amended coroutine. A call between CMPA and JL is therefore not clearly permitted by the stated general convention. The fact that the original implementation happens to preserve the indicator is insufficient to make that a stable promise. Restoring A in the amendment is consistent with the book's distinction between ordinary registers and the indicator.

There is still a possible clarification issue: a reader might understand the buffering calls as transparently interrupting an otherwise unchanged COMPUTE. However, proving the stronger error claim requires an explicit preservation requirement or a legal caller that fails without relying on an unpromised indicator value. Neither has been established. Following the audit instruction to prefer natural readings and not confirm interpretation-dependent objections, change status from confirmed to candidate. This does not claim that the trace is wrong, or that every use of the printed amendment is safe without respecting calling conventions.
