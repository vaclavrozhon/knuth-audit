# Publication review: Volume 1, final eleven findings

Reviewed 12 November 2026. All eleven are retained, with the qualifications below. Original typesetting was compared with the evidence and all four current/historical official correction layers, refreshed for this review. No matching earlier report of these particular TAOCP discrepancies was located. This is a bounded search, not proof of priority or inspection of later physical printings.

- **V1-C0075.** The AMM glossary entry explicitly assigns publication by the Mathematical Association of America to 1894. Cairns's contemporary January 1916 report, p.5, explicitly records the transfer to the newly formed association for the journal's twenty-third year. Both pages were reread. This is a minor conflation of journal foundation and publisher dates. Primary source: [1916 issue](https://archive.org/download/sim_american-mathematical-monthly_1916-01_23_1/sim_american-mathematical-monthly_1916-01_23_1.pdf).
- **V1-C0079.** The book says Logic Design; McCluskey's 1964 account on p.22 says logical design for the same symposium. The recorded participant memoir and institutional history agree. This remains a minor historical-title correction. An original proceedings title page was not newly obtained. Primary source: [McCluskey's contemporary survey](https://ralphgomory.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/02/21_networks.pdf).
- **V1-C0081.** The original book entry ties foundation to the title including Other. The January 1943 issue cover has no Other; October has it. Those two originals were reread, and the earlier recorded April/July cover checks retained. The collective volume title page must not be mistaken for the January issue cover. No claim against the later 1960/1965 dates. Primary sources: [January issue](https://archive.org/details/sim_mathematics-of-computation_1943-01_1_1), [October issue](https://archive.org/details/sim_mathematics-of-computation_1943-10_1_4).
- **V1-C0083.** The 2018 addition permits numerically nonnegative rA. Negative zero meets that condition; with a zero source word each shift and zero addition preserves its minus sign. Exercise 23 explicitly requires the plus sign. Reread the added listing and p.143; the MIX semantics and existing small trace support the invariant. Require a plus sign, not merely a nonnegative value. This concerns only the added answer; no new optimality claim or large rerun.
- **V1-C0085.** Reread the added Levy index line and Volume 1 pp.105/363. The former contains the continuity theorem; the latter contains free-tree definitions. Neither later changes nor the fuller name supply the missing target. The recorded comparison to Volume 2 explains the matching 363, but a copying mechanism remains inference. Correct 363 to 105 in the public erratum.
- **V1-C0086.** Reread the public erratum, earlier correct inequality targets, and Volume 1 pp.377/388. Neither of those two pages mentions the result or its author. The recorded Volume 4B comparison identifies the matching targets there. This is one repeated index erratum, not two findings or a claim that later printed indices necessarily contain it.
- **V1-C0088.** Reread the book's 1868 sentence and original Monatsberichte title (1867), meeting page 597 (15 October 1866), and delta definition on p.601. This establishes an earlier surviving publication without establishing the exact release date of the issue or absolute first use. The historical date is already discussed publicly, including [a 2023 bibliographic answer](https://math.stackexchange.com/questions/4666317/understanding-reference-from-book) and an encyclopedia entry; neither located source identifies the TAOCP discrepancy. This is not presented as a newly discovered historical fact. Primary scan: [Monatsberichte 1866](https://archive.org/download/monatsberichtede1866knig/monatsberichtede1866knig.pdf).
- **V1-C0091.** Reread the original rule on p.616, Robson's actual rule on p.418, and the existing general proof/review. The book tests only the requested length at a lower-power alignment; Robson tests a whole upper-power slot. The legal recursive family has fixed maximum length 32, bounded live volume N, and endpoint/N lower limit at least 4709347/783360 > 6. The induction retains blocking slots, controls boundary contributions by O(log N) for fixed maximum size, and extends from N=576h+32 by monotonicity. Its previously independent finite trace is corroboration, not an asymptotic argument. Neither the optimal bound N(32)<=6 nor Robson's actual algorithm is refuted. The successful large validation was not repeated.
- **V1-C0093.** The triple-order recurrence printed immediately before the faulty sentence gives (P,P,P) for a leaf. Consecutive visits may thus be equal, as well as parent/child. The distance bound and traversal remain correct. Retain as a low-severity proof sentence correction; this occurs at leaves of larger trees too.
- **V1-C0094.** A2 copies Q's old coefficient/variable and downward link into its new exponent-zero child. For P=x, Q=1, that child represents 1, not the zero polynomial asserted in the explanation. The assignments and result x+1 are correct. Retain as a minor explanation error, not an algorithm failure.
- **V1-C0096.** Reread exercise 7 on p.422, the explicit edit list on p.603, and E6 on p.419. The replacement tests are expressly equalities, while the assignment ATOM(Q)<-0 remains untouched. A root with a left leaf executes that assignment although the requested representation has no ATOM field. Deleting it repairs the edit list. The intended algorithm and earlier implementation already incorporate that deletion; retain low severity.

Official searches covered the relevant page numbers, the 2011 index shift and index names in all1-prepre, all1-pre, all1, err1 and err1.textxt. The 2006 wording change for triple traversal and 2023 Krogdahl page-range correction do not repair C0093 or C0091. Index deletions are not exhaustively recorded by the author, so persistence in later printed indices is specifically unverified.
