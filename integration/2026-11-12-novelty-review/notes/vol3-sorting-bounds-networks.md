# Volume 3: sorting bounds, selection and networks

Reviewed on 2026-11-12 against the second edition, first printing (March 1998), file-page offset +11, and the later erratum explicitly identified below. The original typeset targets, detailed arguments, and corresponding errata were checked again. All 21 cards are retained, with the qualifications in this table. Notation in each row is local; the linked card's original note supplies the full definitions and proof.

| Card | Decisive reason and scope |
| --- | --- |
| V3-C0028 | On p. 636 the regularized Gaussian sum needs the upper bound t<m. Its printed sum over all positive t has a negative harmonic tail and diverges, although the left side is finite. The main-text truncation makes the intended operation clear. The subsequent final asymptotic formula is not refuted. |
| V3-C0029 | In the mean Batcher-sort exchange count on p. 630, the linear coefficient contains -(gamma+2)/(4 ln 2), not the printed plus sign. Summing the stated per-index asymptotic proves the negative sign. The discrepancy exceeds 1 in the linear coefficient and cannot be absorbed in the stated oscillation of amplitude below .001. The 1977 first-edition replacement repeats the wrong sign; Sedgewick's original 1978 analysis gives the correct one. |
| V3-C0030 | In the deliberately modified Quicksort with non-strict scans, equal keys cause an out-of-range read already in the second partition phase, not the third as p. 631 says. For N=2 and cutoff M=1, two zero keys with sentinels -1 and 1 suffice: the second Q4 reaches index -1. This does not criticize the unmodified algorithm. |
| V3-C0032 | The Quicksort probability bound on p. 137 is false for N=63, cutoff M=1, and c=.05. The least possible comparison count is 320, while the printed threshold (c+1)(N+1)H_N is about 317.739. Thus every input exceeds the threshold, contradicting the claimed probability below exp(-.05). The minimum follows from the split recurrence with cost n+1 and is attained by balanced splits. The 1996 first-edition addition has the same defective coefficient. |
| V3-C0033 | In the binomial-ratio expansion on p. 637, the term multiplying -1/(6n) has x^2+x^4+..., not alternating signs. Stirling's 1/n correction gives x^2/(1-x^2); terms of order n^-3 cannot change it. The final divisor-sum approximation to its stated precision is unaffected. |
| V3-C0034 | The leftist-tree answer on p. 645 mixes counts by internal nodes and by leaves. Its L starts with z, so G=zL starts with z^2 and cannot satisfy G=z+G(zG). The node-count leading constant is about .68837122, obtained by shifting the leaf count; .25036 belongs to the other size convention. The exponential constant remains about 2.7494879. The 1995 replacement already contains the inconsistency. |
| V3-C0035 | The per-output tournament-merging bound on p. 646 needs ceil(lg k), not floor(lg k), in general. With five streams, replacement of a winner on a deepest leaf leaves four mutually unordered possible winners, requiring three comparisons; floor(lg 5)=2. This concerns the comparison bound, not failure to sort. |
| V3-C0036 | The radix-sort program on pp. 174-175 tests bins 1 through M-1 for emptiness, excluding bin 0. Its answer on p. 651 uses the distribution for all M bins instead. With N=2, M=3 and digit columns 111 and 222, the actual tested-empty count is zero although the proposed distribution requires at least three. The time expression is consistent when its empty-bin variable excludes bin 0. |
| V3-C0037 | On p. 176, floor(N/8) allocated bins do not suffice for the assigned index floor(KN/8), for keys K in [0,1). For N=17 and K=33/34 the index is 2, outside the two bins 0 and 1. A ceiling in the allocation, or consistent rescaling of the index, repairs this. |
| V3-C0038 | On p. 652, the sum of the binwise density suprema is cN+o(N), not N, when there are cN bins. Constant density 1 and c=1/8 already contradict the printed statement. The eventual linear expected-time conclusion still follows. |
| V3-C0039 | The weak-ordering argument on pp. 194 and 655 applies a binary information bound to comparisons with three possible outcomes. For two possible distinct values, Q=2^n-1 weak orderings give ceil(lg Q)=n, but the preceding construction uses n-1 comparisons. The claimed binary lower bound is false; this does not refute the separately stated sharp fixed-m theorem. |
| V3-C0040 | The average comparison lower bound on p. 196 cannot include the ceiling: three distinct elements can be sorted in an average of 8/3 comparisons, below ceil(lg(3!))=3. The non-rounded information lower bound remains valid. |
| V3-C0041 | The six-key decision tree on p. 196 has leaf levels 9 and 10, not 10 and 11, with the root at level zero. A full binary tree whose leaves all had depth at least 10 would need at least 1024 leaves, but this tree has 720. The corresponding main-text and answer counts are consistent with 9 and 10. |
| V3-C0042 | The selection-tail estimate on pp. 664-665 is too strong. Take a sample of n from 2n elements and target t=n/2. For its lower pivot with sample rank r=floor(n/2-sqrt((n/2)ln n)), the single failure point s=floor(n-sqrt(n ln(2n)))+1 has probability binom(s-1,r-1)binom(2n-s,n-r)/binom(2n,n). Stirling expansion gives n^(-1/2-(sqrt(2)-1)^2+o(1)), about n^(-.671573+o(1)), already larger than either claimed tail bound. The final complexity conclusion is not itself disproved. The 1996 first-edition addition already has this defective estimate. |
| V3-C0043 | On p. 663 the maximum defining the new subchampion begins at index k-t+j+1, not k-t+j-1. The preceding comparisons examine only the former range. For k=t=3, j=1, champion 8 with subchampions 7,6,4 and opposing champion 5, the prescribed comparison returns 5 whereas the printed maximum is 7. |
| V3-C0044 | On p. 672 the output-wire indices in the explanatory clause omit a factor n: they need 2mn+2n+j and 2mn+2n+k. For m=3, n=5 the affected output wires are 41,42,43, not 17,18,19. The displayed comparator construction already uses the correct indices. |
| V3-C0045 | In the block-count statement on p. 673, the full lower blocks have count m^2, not zero. For m=3, the already sorted input consisting of one zero and 26 ones gives counts (8,9,9). It directly contradicts the printed zero counts while satisfying the sorting premises. |
| V3-C0046 | The three mask exponents in the shuffle answer on p. 667 use an undefined r where the defined word-size parameter is t. Replacing r by t makes the masks agree with their stated bit lengths and the construction. |
| V3-C0047 | The assertion on pp. 246 and 675 equating uniform sorting with Algorithm Q does not hold for Algorithm Q as printed, which reorders the records. On (4,2,1,3), cutoff M=1, Q makes six nonredundant pair comparisons; uniform sorting in original-index order makes five, using transitivity to skip the pair (1,3). Both sort correctly. A different pivot convention could support a related statement but is not the printed algorithm. |
| V3-C0048 | The number of monotone truth tables on t inputs on p. 669 is D_t, not the printed D_(2t). There are 2^t truth-table positions but only t Boolean inputs; these are different parameters. The nearby historical corrections to another function symbol and traversal order do not correct this subscript. |
| V3-C0049 | The 2005 erratum replacing the Dedekind-number answer on p. 669 has half the necessary leading binomial coefficient in its exponential. Arbitrary subsets of the middle layer already give at least 2^binom(2m,m)-1 relevant monotone functions; the replacement's leading logarithm is binom(2m-1,m)ln 2, exactly half as large. The smaller displayed terms cannot repair this. The original 1998 limiting statement is sound. This card concerns the replacement, and does not endorse all its other terms. |

The five official layers [all3-prepre](https://www-cs-faculty.stanford.edu/~knuth/all3-prepre.ps.gz), [all3-pre](https://www-cs-faculty.stanford.edu/~knuth/all3-pre.ps.gz), [all3](https://www-cs-faculty.stanford.edu/~knuth/all3.ps.gz), [err3](https://www-cs-faculty.stanford.edu/~knuth/err3.ps.gz) and [first-edition errata](https://www-cs-faculty.stanford.edu/~knuth/err3-1e.ps.gz) were compared using the refreshed sources and the existing first-edition page mappings. Relevant neighboring corrections concern punctuation, references, other formulas, or other exercises. None repairs the retained defect. In particular, an erratum containing a defective replacement is not evidence that the defect in that replacement has been reported.

Public searches combined Knuth/TAOCP with Batcher mean sign, regularized sum, equal-key partition trace, Quicksort probability, binomial/Stirling expansion, leftist-tree constant, merging floor logarithm, radix empty piles, Tamminen bins, Riemann sum, weak orderings, average sorting ceiling, six-key tree levels, selection tail, subchampions, uniform sorting, shuffle masks, network wire indices, full blocks, truth tables and the 2005 Dedekind replacement. Exact-page queries often returned irrelevant code-line matches; broader subject queries were also inspected.

Relevant public leads were followed:

- [Sedgewick's 1978 Batcher analysis](https://sedgewick.io/wp-content/themes/sedgewick/papers/1978Batcher.pdf) supports the corrected sign. It predates the audited printing and does not identify this transcription as a book erratum.
- The [2018 Quicksort probability discussion](https://cs.stackexchange.com/questions/86149/how-to-bound-the-probability-that-quicksort-takes-greater-than-n-lg-n-time) quotes the disputed bound and discusses weaker high-probability estimates. It does not identify the missing coefficient or the all-input counterexample. Its remark about an error being too large refers to the probability estimate's strength, rather than reporting the false formula.
- [OEIS A351145](https://oeis.org/A351145) and [A351146](https://oeis.org/A351146) discuss the divisor sum and its final asymptotic. They do not report the intermediate expansion error in C0033.
- [Nogueira's leftist-tree paper](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0166-218X(00)00250-X) gives the node-count constant and distinguishes sizes. The accessible text was checked for its Knuth references and discussion; no explicit report of the book's size-convention inconsistency was found. The mathematical asymptotic itself is previously known.
- [Kiwiel's selection paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/cs/0402005), including its discussion of Knuth, criticizes the precision and practical cost of related analyses. It does not identify the specific false tail exponent retained here. The card is limited to that estimate, not a claim to be the first criticism of this selection analysis.
- The Writ repository's ERRATA page concerns its own software, not these book assertions. The RAIRO weak-ordering search result concerns a ranking-optimization model rather than a correction to the comparison lower bound.

No matching prior public report of these specific defects was found. This finite search does not establish priority or exclude unpublished correspondence. Full original arguments and source locations remain attached to each card.
