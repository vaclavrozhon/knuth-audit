# Publication review: Volume 1, lists and program contracts

Reviewed 12 November 2026 against the supplied 1997 typesetting, the existing proofs and execution records, and all refreshed official Volume 1 correction layers. No matching prior public report was located for the eleven retained findings below. This establishes neither absolute priority nor the contents of later physical printings. V1-C0033 was separately downgraded; see vol1-c0033-calling-convention.md.

- **V1-C0029 — retain.** Reread Programs A and B, pp.168–169 and 174–175. Their output areas allow only 1200 words, although a valid product of two cycles with 3t+4 input words can have t transpositions, requiring 4t output words. At t=320 the input fits in 61 cards and the output has 1280 words. Singleton suppression does not help. The existing literal execution demonstrates code corruption in A and overwriting the output area in B even after the published ENT3 correction. The input is valid, so the erratum about omitted input-error checks is irrelevant. The proposed larger answer area works for A; the B repair explicitly reduces the supported input limit and must not be presented as preserving the original 75-card limit.
- **V1-C0030 — retain.** The exercise on p.193 explicitly asks for nonpositive I1. Following Program M with initial I1=q<=0 yields A=X[q], I2=q and I3=q-1, with the comparison indicator unchanged. The answer's substitution n=1 predicts different results. At q=0 and X[0]=5,X[1]=7 there is a direct discrepancy. The input-address range and representability conditions in the detailed argument remain necessary.
- **V1-C0031 — retain as a contract omission.** SMAX1 executes ENT1 1, but its listed entrance and exit conditions do not account for I1. The general subroutine convention on p.188 preserves ordinary registers unless mentioned; its explicit exceptions for the comparison indicator and J do not cover I1. For n=1 and initial I1=2, I1 exits as 1. This distinguishes the finding from the disputed CONTROL-indicator card C0033.
- **V1-C0032 — retain as a missing integrability qualification.** The text permits any function of the cycle counts and states an expectation identity. For w=1/2, let the function be 2^n n! on the identity permutation of size n>=2 and -2^n n on a single n-cycle, and zero otherwise. Each fixed-size conditional mean is zero, but after geometric mixing the positive and negative contributions are each 1/2 for every n. Both parts have infinite expectation, so the unconditional expectation is undefined. Pointwise finiteness does not repair the formula; integrability or an appropriate one-sided hypothesis does. The result is correct when the necessary existence assumptions are supplied.
- **V1-C0034 — retain.** Reread the exercise and answer, pp.254 and 544. With four stacks and four cells, the insertion/deletion sequence I3,D3,I2,D2,I1,D1,I3,D3,I2 causes five overflows from capacities (0,0,0,4), versus zero from (1,1,1,1), while at most one item is live. The successive first-layout capacities are (0,0,1,3),(0,1,0,3),(1,0,0,3),(1,0,1,2),(1,1,0,2). Thus s0<=s1+M fails. The published changes to the prose proof and the left-shift copy do not affect this right-shift witness.
- **V1-C0036 — retain, erratum only.** The 2018 added answer encloses the Brightwell–Tetali multiplicative upper bound in an extra exponential. The primary paper's Theorem 1.3 was compared directly with the erratum's original display. The outer exponential destroys the claimed improvement; the resulting inequality can still be an upper bound. This is a transcription/claimed-strength error, not a disproof of the source theorem. The original 1997 answer does not contain this added display.
- **V1-C0037 — retain.** The answer on p.552 states the P=Q behavior without an exception. Set P=Q=1 and let the separate multiplier M be -1. The coefficient cancels; A4 frees the node still pointed to by P and then follows its overwritten link. With initially empty AVAIL this becomes a null pointer. The 2022 correction to the Q update does not restore P's old link. The answer already discusses a similar exception for M=Q, but not this aliasing case.
- **V1-C0038 — retain as algebra in the timing explanation.** Substituting m'=0, p'=alpha*p, m''=(1-alpha)*p and the arithmetic progression for q' into the exact displayed time gives 4*alpha*p*r^2+39*p*r+5*alpha*p*r+8*q0*r+38*r+29. The printed approximation instead has 40*p*r+4*alpha*p*r. For p=2,r=1,alpha=1/2,q0=0 these give 154 and 155. The underlying exact formula remains correct.
- **V1-C0040 — retain as a wrong worked output.** Recomputed the predecessor array from the input on p.264 and the descending loop in the code on pp.548–549, including the two added arcs. It is (9,9,1,7,7,4,3,2,6). Starting at the first nonzero entry prints 1,9,6,4,7,3,1. The printed 9,6,8,5,9 corresponds to the opposite processing order. Both are graph cycles, so the objection concerns the claimed execution, not cycle detection itself.
- **V1-C0041 — retain.** The pseudocode on p.580 sets a chosen vertex's key to infinity but subsequently updates every j<n, restoring keys of earlier chosen vertices. With costs c14=1,c12=2,c24=3,c34=10,c23=20,c13=30 it chooses 1, then 2, then 1 again, leaving 3 disconnected. No tie or unspecified diagonal value is involved. The later unchosen-list alternative supplies the missing restriction; the preceding displayed routine does not.
- **V1-C0042 — retain.** Reread the constant representation on p.338 and the MULT tests on p.345. For byte radix b, write B=b^5. A constant c=-(B-1) fits in a word, as does the correct derivative of c*x. Only the auxiliary equality test c-1 overflows to negative zero; JANZ then accepts it as one. The routine returns 1 instead of c. Comparing with CMPA avoids this overflow in both operand tests. No magnitude restriction excluding the witness appears in the stated representation. The accepted executions were reused, not repeated across every radix.

## Source and public-report checks

The official layers all1-prepre, all1-pre, all1, err1 and err1.textxt were refreshed from the [author's site](https://www-cs-faculty.stanford.edu/~knuth/taocp.html); their hashes match the archived versions. The relevant page entries and related corrections were inspected. Original images, detailed mathematical arguments, and execution records remain linked from the individual ledger entries.

Targeted searches included `TAOCP ANS MAXWDS`, `Knuth MAXIMUM nonpositive`, `SMAX1 error`, `Knuth 1.3.3 22 integrability`, `Knuth 2.2.2 17 overflow`, `Knuth Brightwell Tetali error`, `Knuth Brightwell exp errata`, `Knuth 2.2.4 9 -1`, `Knuth 2.2.4 aliasing`, `Knuth 2.2.4 16 time error`, `Knuth 2.2.3 24 cycle output`, `Knuth 2.3.4.1 12 error`, `Knuth 2.3.4.1 Prim mistake`, `TAOCP minimum spanning error`, and `Knuth MULT DECA overflow`.

The [2009 sequential-allocation discussion](https://stackoverflow.com/questions/1157230/taocp-sequential-allocation-questions) was read in full. It explains the first free block and the meaning of BASE, and does not report the false overflow-count bound. General descriptions of correct minimum-spanning-tree algorithms likewise do not establish a prior report of the particular faulty loop. Search snippets were used for discovery only; no claim of exhaustive public coverage is made.
