# V2-C0066 — The initial bound can remain zero or one forever

**Confirmed · low severity.** 3rd edition · 1st printing · 1997.

Location: Volume 2 · §3.5 · Exercise 35

The auxiliary sequence can stay at 0 or 1 forever, producing an empty selection. Starting at least at 2 fixes the proof.

The proposed initial boundary a_0=s_0 need not start an increasing
sequence of boundaries. For the admissible computable injection s_j=j,
the recurrence

a_(m+1) = max{s_j : 0<=j<2^(a_m)}

gives a_m=0 for every m. All selection intervals are empty, and the
last displayed quotient nu(a_m)/a_m is undefined. There is also a
positive fixed-point example: s=(1,0,2,3,...) gives a_m=1 throughout.

Replacing a_0=s_0 by a_0=max(2,s_0), or simply a_0=2, avoids the issue.
Indeed the maximum of 2^a distinct nonnegative integers is at least
2^a-1>a whenever a>=2.

The theorem itself is correct. With the repaired start, the selected
positions below a_(m+1) form a subset of {s_j:j<2^(a_m)} missing at
most a_m+1 elements. R5 therefore implies that the proportion of ones
among the first 2^(a_m) terms of the rearranged sequence tends to 1/2,
which proves both inequalities in the exercise.

## Publication review, 12 November 2026

For the legal computable injection `s_j=j`, the proposed initial boundary remains zero forever; `(1,0,2,3,...)` also gives a positive fixed boundary of one. Starting at at least two forces growth because `2^a-1>a`. The selection theorem survives.
