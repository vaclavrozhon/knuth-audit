# V2-C0237 — The final proof sentence needs even n

**Confirmed · medium severity.** 3rd edition · 1st printing · 1997.

Location: Volume 2 · See attached source pages

In the third edition, first printing, the final sentence of the proof asserts that at least one proposed shift makes a_(n-1) nonzero unless q is identically zero. This assertion needs the qualification that n is even.

In the third edition, first printing, the final sentence of the proof
asserts that at least one proposed shift makes a_(n-1) nonzero unless
q is identically zero. This assertion needs the qualification that n
is even.

For u(x)=x^3+x, the roots are 0,i,-i. Every proposed extremal-root
choice gives c=0, so a2=0, but q(W)=W+1 is not identically zero.
The same issue occurs for higher odd degrees with all roots on the
imaginary axis.

The theorem remains valid. When n is odd, the leading coefficient of
q is a_n=u_n, which is already nonzero; a_(n-1) is irrelevant to its
degree. In the example the scheme simply evaluates x(x^2+1).
When n is even, the printed assertion about a_(n-1) is the needed one.

This is separate from the known 1999 correction m=ceil(n/2)-1 on p. 493.

## Publication review, 12 November 2026

The final nonvanishing-coefficient claim in Theorem E's proof, p. 494, needs even degree. For u=x³+x all permitted choices leave the x² coefficient zero although q=W+1 is nonzero. In odd degree the leading coefficient itself proves the needed nonvanishing, so the theorem survives.
