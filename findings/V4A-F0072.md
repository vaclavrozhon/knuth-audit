# V4A-F0072 — The Lovasz-bound proof equates two different counts

**Confirmed · low severity.** 1st edition · 1st printing · 2011.

Location: 4A / Answer 7.2.1.3–80, printed 739: N=N_0+kappa_t(N_0)

Use N=N_0+N_1 and N_1>=kappa_t(N_0), with the stated separate N_0=0 case; the nonempty-family bound remains valid.

For t=2,N=4 the defined split has N_0=1,N_1=3, but kappa_2(N_0)=2. Thus the displayed equality gives 4=3.

## Publication review, 12 November 2026

Answer 7.2.1.3-80, p. 739: t=2,N=4 gives N0=1,N1=3 but kappa_2(N0)=2. The asserted equality N=N0+kappa_t(N0) fails. Use N=N0+N1 and N1>=kappa_t(N0); the Lovasz bound is unaffected.

Decision: `retained`. No matching prior report was found in the checked sources; absolute priority is not established.
