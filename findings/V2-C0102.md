# V2-C0102 — Weighted convolution leaves modular halving implicit

**Candidate · medium severity.** 3rd edition · 1st printing · 1997.

Location: Volume 2 · §4.3.2 · Exercise 14

Ordinary real halving can return a noninteger instead of the required residue. Modular halving makes the algorithm correct.

Candidate only: the surrounding modular-arithmetic context may supply the intended interpretation.

After the published 2020 correction, take q=3, n=2 and U=V=6. The chunks are (0,3), the weights are (1,1/sqrt(2)), and the cyclic convolution is (9/2,0). The corrected integer coefficients are (9,0), giving the displayed final sum 9/2. Ordinary real reduction modulo7 returns9/2, but36 mod7=1.

For S=sum(t_k*2^floor(k*q/n)), the proved identity is S≡2UV (mod2^q−1). Reduce R=S mod(2^q−1), then compute (R+(2^q−1)*(R mod2))/2. This gives the required integer residue. Exercise11 already explains modular division, so the omission is not classified as a confirmed error. The known missing-factor correction is excluded from our findings.
