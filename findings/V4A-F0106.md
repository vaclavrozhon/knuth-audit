# V4A-F0106 — The near-diagonal saddle expansion drops r+1 to r

**Confirmed · low severity.** 1st edition · 1st printing · 2011.

Location: 4A / Answer 7.2.1.5–61, first two sentences, printed 777

Use alpha=1+(r+1)/n+O(n^(2*epsilon-2)), beta=1-(r+1)/n+O(n^(2*epsilon-2)), and N=r+1+O(n^(2*epsilon-1)).

The expansions of alpha, beta and N omit the +1 coming from alpha=(n+1)/m. Their printed remainders are too small to absorb it.

## Publication review, 12 November 2026

Answer 7.2.1.5-61, p. 777: the saddle parameter (n+1)/(n-r) has first correction (r+1)/n. For r=1 and epsilon=1/4, substitution gives alpha=1+2/n+O(n^-2), beta=1-2/n+O(n^-2), and the intermediate normalizing quantity N=2+O(1/n), rather than 1. The claimed n^-3/2 error does not cover this. The final leading estimate with its larger O(1/r) allowance is not disproved.

Decision: `retained`. No matching prior report was found in the checked sources; absolute priority is not established.
