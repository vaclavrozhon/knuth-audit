# V2-C0034 — The descending-triple formula misses the period-two case

**Confirmed · low severity.** 3rd edition · 1st printing · 1997.

Location: Volume 2 · §3.3.3 · Answer 20 · Printed 574

The period-two boundary case gives a positive probability for an impossible event.

With a=1,c=1,m=2 the full-period sequence alternates 0,1, so three strictly descending values never occur. The printed formula gives 1/8. Its delta-product calculation assumes three distinct zero-state positions; the first and third coincide when m=2. Subtract [m=2]/(4m), or handle that period separately. The general expansion and archived 2133 exact models agree. Rechecked on 6 November 2026; latest physical printing and priority remain unverified.

## Publication review, 12 November 2026

The period-two model merges two zero-state positions and needs a separate term; retain as a minor boundary omission.

Decision: `retained`. No matching prior report was found in the checked sources; absolute priority is not established.
