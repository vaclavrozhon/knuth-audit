# V2-C0179 — The zero sum must take real parts before the limit

**Confirmed · medium severity.** 3rd edition · 1st printing · 1997.

Location: Volume2 · printed 414

The displayed unpaired sum diverges. Take real parts of finite partial sums, or sum both half-planes symmetrically.

The exercise sums only the upper-half-plane integrals. At x=p^k, its imaginary partial sum grows as log(T)/(2*pi*k), by the recorded Landau-formula argument.

## Publication review, 12 November 2026

The exercise sums complex integrals only over zeros in the upper half-plane and omits their real parts. The reread proof derives divergence i*log(T)/(2*pi*k)+constant+o(1) at x=p^k from Landau's unconditional formula and integration by parts. Thus an O(1) term cannot absorb the error. Take real parts before the limit or sum conjugate zeros symmetrically. The classical convergence theorem remains an explicitly cited external input.

Decision: `retained`. No matching prior report was found in the checked sources; absolute priority is not established.
