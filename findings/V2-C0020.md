# V2-C0020 — Rounding the number of cells invalidates the concentration argument

**Confirmed · medium severity.** 3rd edition · 1st printing · 1997.

Location: Volume 2 · §3.3.2 · Answer 20 · p. 564

The auxiliary proof is invalid; an independent argument preserves the stated variance asymptotic.

Answer 20 sets m=ceil(1/delta), but centers its bounds at n*delta instead of the actual n/m. Along explicit sample sizes with fixed alpha=1, the proposed lower threshold approaches the mean in standard-deviation units. One cell then violates the bound with probability tending to 1/2, contradicting the stated polynomially small failure probability. The desired variance asymptotic follows from direct concentration of the sample means of U and U squared. Rechecked on November 6, 2026; no matching correction found in the four errata layers. Latest physical printing and priority remain unverified.

## Publication review, 12 November 2026

Rounding the number of cells shifts the claimed concentration threshold to within o(standard deviation) of the mean along a fixed-parameter sequence.

Decision: `retained`. No matching prior report was found in the checked sources; absolute priority is not established.
