# V2-C0231 — Both determinant exponents should be n(n-1)/2

**Confirmed · high severity.** 3rd edition · 1st printing · 1997.

Location: Volume 2 · §4.7 · Exercise 29(a)

The exercise added to page 537 on 1 April 2024 defines A_n using row and column indices 0,...,n-1. In its determinant formula, both occurrences of n(n+1)/2 should be n(n-1)/2.

The exercise added to page 537 on 1 April 2024 defines A_n using
row and column indices 0,...,n-1. In its determinant formula, both
occurrences of n(n+1)/2 should be n(n-1)/2.

For example, take n=1, A(w,z)=1, p=q=1, and r=s=2. Then T(w,z)=1,
so det(T_1)=det(A_1)=1, whereas the displayed right side is 4.

In general, let
  P(m,i) = [w^m] p(w) (w r(w))^i,
  Q(k,j) = [z^k] q(z) (z s(z))^j,
with all indices between 0 and n-1. Then T_n=P A_n Q^t. These two
triangular matrices have diagonal entries p(0)r(0)^i and q(0)s(0)^j,
respectively. Their determinant exponents are therefore
0+1+...+(n-1)=n(n-1)/2. This also proves the formula when some
constant terms vanish, without the normalization in the answer.

Part (b), whose answer is det(A_n)=1, is unaffected. The cited
Gessel-Xin paper, EJC 13 (2006), R53, page 14, also gives the correct
constant scaling factor with exponent binomial(n,2).

Checked in all2.pdf (file page 9), err2.pdf (file page 8), and the
original err2.textxt, freshly retrieved on 4 November 2026. Both
plus signs are present in the typeset formula and in the TeX.
No later correction was found in the six archived errata layers.

Audit ID: V2-C0231. Unsent draft. The new exercise is absent from
the 1997 first printing; no later physical printing was inspected.

## Publication review, 12 November 2026

The determinant exercise added in the 2024 errata uses n(n+1)/2 instead of n(n−1)/2. At n=1 and constant factors p=q=1, r=s=2 it gives 1=4. Triangular substitution matrices have diagonal powers indexed 0 through n−1. Gessel–Xin already give the correct scaling identity; the finding is the erroneous book exercise.

Decision: `retained`. No matching prior report was found in the checked sources; absolute priority is not established.
