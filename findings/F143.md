# F143 — The toy matching example is not bipartite

**Confirmed · low severity.** 1st edition · 2nd printing · 2022.

Location: 4B / main text 7.2.2.1, p. 104, before (90)

The example belongs to general matching, not the bipartite subclass defined on the preceding page.

The introduction calls example (90) a 2DM problem. But options “1 4”, “1 5” and “4 5” form a triangle, which cannot be bipartite. The natural correction is X2C. The impossibility claim, the complete 19-node tree and the formula for independent copies remain valid. Both the book and author draft have the same wording; six errata layers contain no matching correction. A physical copy and priority remain unchecked.

## Publication review, 12 November 2026

Main text page 104: the example called 2DM is a general exact 2-cover instance, not bipartite matching. Its edges 14, 15, 45 form a triangle, contradicting the immediately preceding definition of 2DM. The unsatisfiability and tree size do not change.

Decision: `retained`. No matching prior report was found in the checked sources; absolute priority is not established.
