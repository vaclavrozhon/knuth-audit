# V2-C0228 — The reduced dual's label list has an extra power

**Confirmed · low severity.** 3rd edition · 1st printing · 1997.

Location: Volume 2 · §4.6.3

The list of labels of the dual reduced graph includes one extra power of two. For n >= 2 it should end at 2^(e_0-1), followed by n, rather than at 2^e_0 followed by n. The case n = 1 is a single vertex.

The list of labels of the dual reduced graph includes one extra power
of two. For n >= 2 it should end at 2^(e_0-1), followed by n, rather
than at 2^e_0 followed by n. The case n = 1 is a single vertex.

For example, n = 3 gives the chain 1, 2, 3 in both directions.
The vertex labeled 2 has out-degree one and is suppressed. The reduced
graph and its dual both consist of vertices 1 and 3 joined by three
parallel arcs. There is no vertex labeled 2 in either reduced graph.

More generally, the original reduced vertices are floor(n/2^k),
for k = e_0,...,0. Except at the original source, the number of paths
from such a vertex to the sink is 2^k. Reversal therefore gives labels
1, 2, 4, ..., 2^(e_0-1), n. The duality assertion itself is correct.

A recent physical printing and priority have not been verified.
Draft only; not sent.

## Publication review, 12 November 2026

The reduced dual graph in answer 34, p. 696, ends with labels 2^(e0−1), n, not 2^e0, n. For n=3 its two surviving nodes have labels 1 and 3, with three parallel arcs.

Decision: `retained`. No matching prior report was found in the checked sources; absolute priority is not established.
