# V2-C0214 — The normalized Gräffe limit is missing its sign or phase

**Confirmed · high severity.** 3rd edition · 1st printing · 1997.

Location: Volume 2 · §4.6.2 · Exercise 20,683

The normalized Graeffe iterates need not converge to x^m with coefficient +1. For u(x)=2x-1, we have m=1, and the t-th unnormalized iterate is 1-2^(2^t)x for t>=1. Dividing by its positive Euclidean norm gives a limit of -x.

The normalized Graeffe iterates need not converge to x^m with
coefficient +1. For u(x)=2x-1, we have m=1, and the t-th unnormalized
iterate is 1-2^(2^t)x for t>=1. Dividing by its positive Euclidean norm
gives a limit of -x.

For real coefficients the limit is (-1)^m*x^m. For complex coefficients,
a unit scalar phase must be removed; it need not itself converge.
The estimates for M(u) are unaffected.

## Publication review, 12 November 2026

The Graeffe limit on p. 683 lacks a leading sign or phase. Starting with 2x−1 gives 1−2^(2^t)x, whose normalization tends to −x. The root and Mahler-measure bounds are unaffected.
