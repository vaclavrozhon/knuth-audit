# V3-C0042 — The selection proof uses a false tail bound

**Confirmed · medium severity.** 2nd edition · 1st printing · 1998.

Location: Answer 5.3.3-24, printed664–665: the claimed failure-probability decay is false

Invalidates the printed probabilistic induction; the final selection-complexity theorem is not refuted.

The exact hypergeometric distribution contradicts the printed estimate already at one admissible rank.

## Publication review, 12 November 2026

The selection-tail estimate on pp. 664-665 is too strong. Take a sample of n from 2n elements and target t=n/2. For its lower pivot with sample rank r=floor(n/2-sqrt((n/2)ln n)), the single failure point s=floor(n-sqrt(n ln(2n)))+1 has probability binom(s-1,r-1)binom(2n-s,n-r)/binom(2n,n). Stirling expansion gives n^(-1/2-(sqrt(2)-1)^2+o(1)), about n^(-.671573+o(1)), already larger than either claimed tail bound. The final complexity conclusion is not itself disproved. The 1996 first-edition addition already has this defective estimate.

Decision: `retained`. No matching prior report was found in the checked sources; absolute priority is not established.
