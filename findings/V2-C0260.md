# V2-C0260 — Wrong exponent in the elementary iterate formula

**Confirmed · low severity.** 3rd edition · 1st printing · 1997.

Location: Volume 2 · Answer 4.7-15 · Printed page 722

Replace the exponent inside the parentheses by k-1. The preceding differential equation and integrated identity are correct.

The formula uses z^(1-k) where z^(k-1) is required. For U(z)=z/(1+z), it gives z²/(1+z) even for the first iterate.

## Publication review, 12 November 2026

Answer 4.7–15 on p. 722 reverses an exponent: the parenthesis needs z^(k−1). With U=z/(1+z), even the first printed iterate is z²/(1+z), rather than U. The integrated identity immediately preceding it is correct.

Decision: `retained`. No matching prior report was found in the checked sources; absolute priority is not established.
