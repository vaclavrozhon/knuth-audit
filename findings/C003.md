# C003 — The strict probability bound can be attained

**Confirmed · medium severity.** 1st edition · 2nd printing · 2022.

Location: 4B / answer 7.2.2.1–200(b), p. 471

An intermediate inequality is false, although the algorithm’s final error guarantee is valid.

For n=M=2, take only the two diagonal options and choose p=5. Then S=xy, and exactly 9 of the 25 assignments give zero. Thus the probability equals 1−(1−1/p)^M; the first bound must be non-strict. The sentence also reverses the conditioning. The correct one-sided error remains below 1/2 per trial. Both the book and author draft agree, with no matching correction found in six errata layers. This is the original C003 card, now supported by an exact counterexample; a physical copy and priority remain unchecked.

## Publication review, 12 November 2026

Answer 7.2.2.1-200(b), p. 471: for the polynomial xy over the field with five elements, the zero probability is 9/25, attaining 1-(1-1/5)^2. The first strict inequality must be non-strict. For one variable the next inequality is also attained. The conditional guarantee concerns a random evaluation of a fixed nonzero polynomial; the final error bound below 1/2 survives.

Decision: `retained`. No matching prior report was found in the checked sources; absolute priority is not established.
