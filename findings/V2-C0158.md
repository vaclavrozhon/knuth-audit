# V2-C0158 — The exact norm identity needs the full convergent numerator

**Confirmed · low severity.** 3rd edition · 1st printing · 1997.

Location: Volume 2 · §4.5.4 · E3, pp. 398 and 400; answer 14, p. 660

A supporting integer identity uses a reduced residue in place of the full numerator; the algorithm’s modular invariant remains valid.

The table gives N=197209, P=159316, V=720 and S=0. The asserted integer identity would require Q²=128704, between 358² and 359². The full convergent is 750943/1691 and satisfies 750943²−197209·1691²=720. Use the full numerator in the exact identity and the later proof modulo factor-base primes. This is a notation and proof defect, not a counterexample to Algorithm E. The known initialization correction does not remove it.

## Publication review, 12 November 2026

Algorithm E stores a convergent numerator reduced modulo N; that value cannot be substituted into the exact integer norm identity. In the printed N=197209 row, P=159316 and V=720 would force an impossible integer denominator square, whereas the full numerator 750943 and denominator 1691 satisfy the identity. The congruence and algorithm remain correct.

Decision: `retained`. No matching prior report was found in the checked sources; absolute priority is not established.
