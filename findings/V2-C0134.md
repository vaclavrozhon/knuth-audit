# V2-C0134 — The quotient formula has the scale of an average, not a total

**Confirmed · low severity.** 3rd edition · 1st printing · 1997.

Location: Volume 2 · Section 4.5.3 · Printed page 654

The additional empirical expression is called the total sum of partial quotients.

The additional empirical expression is called the total sum of partial quotients. For prime p that sum is at least p, while the displayed expression is O(log²p); the stated remainder cannot repair the mismatch. The main exercise is correct. The exact normalization and constants of the intended conjecture remain unresolved.

## Publication review, 12 November 2026

The prose on pp. 653–654 calls the expression a total, and the exercise defines the total over all numerators. For prime p that total is at least p, whereas the expression is O((log p)^2). This contradicts its O(p^-1/2) residual for infinitely many p. An average was presumably intended, but the inaccessible Pitteway–Castle paper and its constants are not claimed verified.

Decision: `retained`. No matching prior report was found in the checked sources; absolute priority is not established.
