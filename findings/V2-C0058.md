# V2-C0058 — Positive rotation sine creates strong block-sum correlations

**Confirmed · high severity.** 3rd edition · 1st printing · 1997.

Location: Volume 2 · §3.4.1 · Exercise 31(c) · page 590

The stated recipe creates substantial dependence between output-block sums, even after the recommended thinning and scale correction.

Exercise 23 chooses positive U and V, so the printed sine 2UV/(U^2+V^2) is always positive. With one angle per pool, uniform affine offsets and the recommended angle rejection, E(cos theta)=0 and beta=E(sin theta)=3(sqrt(3)-1)/pi.

Take the first 682 X entries from each 2048-entry pool and divide their sum by sqrt(682). This uses the suggested one-third of the pool. The expected update matrix has off-diagonal blocks beta*J and -beta*J, where J averages 1024 coordinates. Two independent passes therefore give covariance -(682/1024)*beta^2 = -0.325469... .

The printed positive scale is a=abs(Y_1023+sqrt(4095))/sqrt(2*S). Exact Gaussian and spherical moments bound the change in covariance by less than 0.1, proving scaled covariance below -0.22. The proof includes rare negative values inside the absolute value and does not replace S by its mean.

An independent random sign on the sine removes this particular mean-matrix mechanism. It does not prove independence or eliminate every finite-pool defect. The conclusion concerns the explicit common-angle recipe and output schedule; Brent’s implementation changes matrices within a pass.

The complete proof, code, four archived result blocks, original page and all errata layers were reviewed again on November 6, 2026 without rerunning the experiments. Brent’s later paper already discusses the general need for random signs. No corresponding correction to this answer was found in the available errata. The latest physical printing and novelty remain unverified.

## Publication review, 12 November 2026

Exercise 23 supplies positive coordinates in a quarter disk, so the prescribed sine in answer 31(c) always has the same sign. For the stated pool size and 682 returned X entries, the expected two-pass transformation gives covariance `-(682/1024)[3(sqrt(3)-1)/pi]^2=-0.325469...` between normalized block sums. The exact printed scale changes it by less than 0.1, so it remains below -0.22. The argument includes rare sign reversals in the scale numerator. Retain for this specific recipe and output ordering. Brent had already published the general nonzero-mean-transform problem and random-sign remedy; neither that idea nor a defect in every Wallace implementation is claimed as new.

Decision: `retained`. No matching prior report was found in the checked sources; absolute priority is not established.
