# V4A-F0044 — The multiplier is chosen after the variable order

**Confirmed · medium severity.** 1st edition · 1st printing · 2011.

Location: 4A / TheoremX on printed247 and answer7.1.4-176(d) on656

The fixed-multiplier theorem is not established by this proof. The cited primary theorem has the weaker quantifier order.

The proof fixes the variable order before choosing the multiplier via its two prefix sets. It proves an order-dependent choice, not the claimed single multiplier with a lower bound over every order.

## Publication review, 12 November 2026

Theorem X and answer 176(d), pp. 247 and 656: the proof fixes a variable order, forms P,Q from it, and then chooses the multiplier a. It does not prove that one a works for every order, as B_min requires. Woelfel's original Theorem 11 explicitly has the former quantifier order. This is a proof gap, not a refutation of the numerical assertion.

Decision: `retained`. No matching prior report was found in the checked sources; absolute priority is not established.
