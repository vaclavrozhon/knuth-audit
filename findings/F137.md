# F137 — A supposedly impossible triangle strip can be tiled

**Confirmed · medium severity.** 1st edition · 2nd printing · 2022.

Location: 4B / answer 7.2.2.1–143(b,c), p. 455, the 12 × 1 Langford strip

The 12 × 1 strip can use all 24 Langford triangles. Four of the six convex shapes are tileable, not three.

An explicit two-row vertex coloring gives a complete counterexample: all shared vertices agree and every rotational tile type occurs once. A short standard-library Python verifier needs no solver or data files. Two completed independent censuses also have exactly the same 8,603 color classes, representing 206,472 labeled arrangements; every saved model was validated. The other two disputed shapes have zero solutions in both censuses. Our Volume 4B PDF prints the correct (12,12,1,0) tuple followed by the false impossibility claim. The 2019 draft has the same sentence but an old tuple error, corrected publicly in 2021 as E071; it is not an exact same-case corroboration. No correction to the impossibility claim was found in six errata layers checked against the official files on November 3, 2026. Physical-copy verification, the complete latest digital edition and priority remain separate.

## Publication review, 12 November 2026

Answer 143(b,c): the 12-by-1 strip can also be tiled by all 24 Langford triangles. The embedded two-row color certificate verifies directly, so four of the six shapes are possible, not three. The old correction of the strip parameters to (12,12,1,0) is already incorporated and does not remove this counterexample.

Decision: `retained`. No matching prior report was found in the checked sources; absolute priority is not established.
