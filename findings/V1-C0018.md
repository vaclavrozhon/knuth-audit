# V1-C0018 — The expansion for Q_x fails for negative x

**Confirmed · high severity.** 3rd edition · 1st printing · 1997.

Location: Volume 1 · §1.2.11.3 · Exercise 11

The expansion for Q_x(n) in the branch x<1 fails for some
negative x. For x=-4 it predicts Q_(-4)(n)=1/5+O(1/n), whereas
the exact identity printed on page 505 gives

-4 Q_(-4)(n)+R_(-1/4)(n)=n!(-4/n)^n exp(-n/4).

The alternating series for R_(-1/4)(n) lies strictly between
3/4 and 1. But n! >= (n/e)^n, so the absolute value of the
right-hand side is at least (4 exp(-5/4))^n, whose base exceeds 1.
Thus Q_(-4)(n) tends to minus infinity along even n and plus
infinity along odd n.

For every fixed x<0, the expansion should include the term

n! x^(n-1) exp(n/x)/n^n,

just as in the branch x>1. This follows either from the exact
identity or by splitting

Q_x(n)=integral_0^infinity exp(-t)(1+xt/n)^(n-1) dt

at t=-n/x. The part beyond that point equals the missing term;
the first part equals -R_(1/x)(n)/x and has the printed
algebraic expansion. The displayed expansion for R_x(n), x<1,
does not need this correction.
