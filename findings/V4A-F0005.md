# V4A-F0005 — The Cartesian-product color formula needs nonempty factors

**Confirmed · low severity.** 1st edition · 1st printing · 2011.

Location: 4A / answer 7–128, p. 530, chromatic number of a Cartesian product

The answer gives the wrong value when one factor has no vertices; the usual nonempty-factor theorem remains correct.

The book permits the null graph K0, and exercise 7–128 imposes no nonempty-factor condition. But K0 square K2 is K0 and can be colored with at most one color, whereas max(χ(K0),χ(K2)) is 2. This counterexample works whether χ(K0) is defined as 0 or 1. Requiring both vertex sets to be nonempty restores the factor-copy lower bound and the printed modular coloring proof. The original definitions, exercise and answer were checked, and all five relevant 4A/F0 errata layers contain no corresponding correction.

## Publication review, 12 November 2026

Answer 7-128, p. 530: the Cartesian-product chromatic-number equality needs nonempty factors. The book allows K0 explicitly. K0 times K2 has at most one color, whereas the maximum of the two factor chromatic numbers is two. This is a minor boundary omission, retained from the baseline rather than a new boundary search.

Decision: `retained`. No matching prior report was found in the checked sources; absolute priority is not established.
