# V2-C0039 — The equality case needs a closedness assumption

**Confirmed · low severity.** 3rd edition · 1st printing · 1997.

Location: Volume 2 · §3.3.4 · Note after answer 9 · p. 579

The stated version of Minkowski’s theorem misses a boundary condition. The resulting spectral upper bound remains valid.

The open cube (-1, 1)^t has volume 2^t and contains no nonzero integer point. Use strictly greater volume, or require a closed convex symmetric set in the equality case. Applying the strict statement to larger balls and taking a limit still proves mu_t <= 2^t. The Hermite proof is also valid. Original p. 579 and all errata layers rechecked on 6 November 2026; the 2012 bibliographic correction is unrelated. Priority unverified.

## Publication review, 12 November 2026

The stated version of Minkowski's theorem includes an arbitrary convex symmetric set of volume equal to `2^t`. The open cube `(-1,1)^t` disproves that formulation. Require closedness at equality or strict volume. The subsequent application to balls can be repaired by a limiting argument, so this is a qualification of the theorem statement, not failure of the final spectral bound.

Decision: `retained`. No matching prior report was found in the checked sources; absolute priority is not established.
