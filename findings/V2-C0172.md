# V2-C0172 — The minus case in the modulo-eight example has a solution

**Confirmed · low severity.** 3rd edition · 1st printing · 1997.

Location: Volume 2 · §4.5.4 · printed 670

Remove the minus alternative. The odd-modulus restriction still has a valid motivating example.

The text claims x^2 plus or minus y^2 cannot be3 modulo8. For the minus sign, x=2,y=1 is a solution.

## Publication review, 12 November 2026

The no-solution example modulo 8 is correct for x squared plus y squared, but false for the printed plus-or-minus: 2 squared minus 1 squared equals 3. Replacing the sign by plus preserves the intended illustration of the odd-modulus restriction.

Decision: `retained`. No matching prior report was found in the checked sources; absolute priority is not established.
