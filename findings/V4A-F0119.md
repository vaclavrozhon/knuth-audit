# V4A-F0119 — The derivative limit has an exponent off by one

**Confirmed · low severity.** 1st edition · 1st printing · 2011.

Location: 4A / Answer 7.2.1.6–33(e), final displayed limit, printed 789

Replace the last exponent n-1 by n-2; this gives the stated factorization count n^(n-2).

The limit of ((x+y)^(n-1)-y^(n-1))/x is (n-1)y^(n-2), not the printed (n-1)y^(n-1).

## Publication review, 12 November 2026

Answer 7.2.1.6-33(e), p. 789: the limit of ((x+y)^(n-1)-y^(n-1))/x as x tends to zero is (n-1)*y^(n-2). At n=2,y=2, the true value is one and the printed value is two. The corrected recurrence still gives q_n=n^(n-2).

Decision: `retained`. No matching prior report was found in the checked sources; absolute priority is not established.
