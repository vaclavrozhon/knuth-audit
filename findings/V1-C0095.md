# V1-C0095 — Few input terms do not guarantee that large subtrees are skipped

**Candidate · low severity.** 3rd edition · 1st printing · 1997.

Location: Volume 1 · §2.3.3 · performance discussion · printed 359

A possible overstatement of the circumstances that produce a speed advantage.

The informal speed claim needs a scope check. For P=x and Q=x+x²+⋯+xⁿ, A4 follows n leftward links through all coefficients of Q. The earlier list algorithm also visits all n nonzero terms; both take Θ(n) on this family. Tree representation can save work on suitably branched inputs, but the ratio of term counts alone does not establish that saving. Because “much faster” is informal, this remains a low-severity candidate, not a proved output defect or a measured machine-time ratio. The original text and small traces through n=64 were checked; no qualification appears in the four refreshed errata layers.
