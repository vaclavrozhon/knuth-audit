# F110 — Repeated options change the expected solution count

**Confirmed · medium severity.** 1st edition · 2nd printing · 2022.

Location: 4B / answer 7.2.2.1–39(b), p. 424; question p. 127

The formula counts distinct set partitions instead of the row selections enumerated by Algorithm X.

The exercise explicitly permits repeated options. With two items and three independent singleton options, the true expectation is 3/2, while the printed occupancy formula gives 3/4. The concrete options a,a,b have two indexed solutions. An example with the book parameters n=100, r=5, m=500 has 481 solutions but one geometric partition; the original DLX1 confirms the count. The corrected expectation is P times the falling factorial (m)_k divided by N^k. The numerical effect in the book large examples is below 0.02%. Exact enumeration, a general proof, PDF and author PS support the finding; checked errata have no matching correction. Physical copy not yet checked.

## Publication review, 12 November 2026

DLX 39(b): sampling with replacement counts solutions by option indices. With three singleton options independently chosen from a,b, the mean number of solutions is 3/2; the printed formula gives 3/4 by counting distinct partitions. For n=kr, the corrected mean is P·(m)_k/N^k, where P=n!/(k!(r!)^k), N=binomial(n,r), and (m)_k is the falling factorial. The numerical effect in the book's large examples is small.

Decision: `retained`. No matching prior report was found in the checked sources; absolute priority is not established.
