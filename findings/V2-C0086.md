# V2-C0086 — The quadratic-monomial count is 4n+2, not 4n

**Confirmed · low severity.** 3rd edition · 1st printing · 1997.

Location: Volume 2 · §4.2.2 · Exercise 19

The local count should be 4n + 2 for n >= 2; the principal error bound and recurrence remain valid.

On page 615, answer 19 says that f_n contains "4n terms of 2nd order".
Using the error variables and the correct recurrence printed immediately
above this statement, the quadratic part of f_2 is

-eta_1 gamma_1 - gamma_1 delta_1 - gamma_1 sigma_1 - delta_1 sigma_1
 -gamma_2 sigma_2 - delta_2 sigma_2
 +gamma_2 gamma_1 + gamma_2 sigma_1 - eta_2 gamma_1 - eta_2 sigma_1.

These are ten distinct monomials, whereas 4n is eight. In general the
count is 4n+2 for n >= 2, and four for n = 1. The two extra terms arise
because g_1 has linear part gamma_1+sigma_1, while g_k has linear part
sigma_k for k >= 2.

This is only a minor counting slip. It does not affect the O(n eps^2)
conclusion, the corrected target s_n-c_n, or Kahan's summation method.
Replacing "4n terms" by "O(n) terms" would suffice.

The displayed recurrences have also been checked by direct substitution
into the original operations, independently of this term count.

## Publication review, 12 November 2026

Expansion of the corrected compensated-sum recurrence gives ten quadratic monomials at n=2, not eight. In general the count is 4n+2 for n>=2, with four at n=1. The 2021 correction to the returned value is included. The asymptotic O(n*epsilon^2) bound is unaffected.

Decision: `retained`. No matching prior report was found in the checked sources; absolute priority is not established.
