# V2-C0206 — A constant v permits k=1

**Confirmed · medium severity.** 3rd edition · 1st printing · 1997.

Location: Volume 2 · §4.6.2 · Exercise 14

The assertion 2<=k<=r needs the additional hypothesis that v is nonconstant. The exercise permits any solution of (8). Over F_2, take u=x(x+1) and v=1. Then r=2, but w(T)=T+1 and the first dependence occurs at k=1.

In general, k is the number of distinct residues of v on the irreducible
factors of u. Thus 1<=k<=min(p,r); k=1 precisely when v is constant.
Algorithm N itself returns the correct result in this case.

## Publication review, 12 November 2026

Exercise 14 allows a constant v. For u=x(x+1) over F2 and v=1, the first dependence is at k=1, contradicting 2<=k<=r. Use 1<=k<=min(p,r); the matrix algorithm itself works.
