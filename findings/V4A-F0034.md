# V4A-F0034 — Global uniqueness does not supply the local response used in Theorem M

**Confirmed · medium severity.** 1st edition · 1st printing · 2011.

Location: 4A / Theorem M proof, printed pages 214–215: existence of A(b) in (31)

The printed proof needs an additional premise or a different argument. The counterexample refutes its local-response step, not the numerical bound in Theorem M.

The hypothesis only requires a unique consistent assignment to the whole network. A three-module network can satisfy it while the left part has two possible responses to the same backward boundary signal, invalidating the asserted function A(b).

## Publication review, 12 November 2026

Theorem M, pp. 214–215: uniqueness of a complete network assignment does not make each cut's local response a function. With M1: u=v, M2: v=u XOR b and a=u, M3: b=a XOR x3 and f=a, the full network uniquely forces b=0 and a=u=v=x3, but the left side has both a=0 and a=1 when b=0. The asserted local A(b) is undefined. This is a proof gap, not a counterexample to the theorem.

Decision: `retained`. No matching prior report was found in the checked sources; absolute priority is not established.
