# V2-C0174 — Dropping the floor invalidates the refined time calculation

**Confirmed · medium severity.** 3rd edition · 1st printing · 1997.

Location: Volume 2 · §4.5.4 · Answer 36, p. 665

The intermediate parameter expansion and claimed remainder fail; the final optimum is recoverable by tuning an integer parameter.

The displayed expansion applies to rho=ln(2√N)/ln(p_m), while r is floor(rho). The saved parametric family has fractional part tending to 1/2, although the stated error tends to zero. Removing this floor can change the logarithm of the time by order √(ln N ln ln N), beyond the claimed O(ln ln N) remainder. The note supplies a corrected integer optimization that retains the intended final bound. This concerns the algebra within the stated heuristic model.

## Publication review, 12 November 2026

Page 399 defines r as a floor, while the page 665 expansion has a vanishing remainder and silently drops its fractional part. The stored family with rho=k+1/2-o(1) and c tending to one disproves that precision. The resulting logarithmic timing error is t*(ln(m)-ln(rho))+O(1/rho), too large for the stated O(ln ln N) remainder. The own proof recovers the final optimized expression by choosing integer r first and tuning m; this remains an estimate within the book's heuristic model.

Decision: `retained`. No matching prior report was found in the checked sources; absolute priority is not established.
