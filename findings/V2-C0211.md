# V2-C0211 — The proposed sampling does not give exact fair bits

**Confirmed · high severity.** 3rd edition · 1st printing · 1997.

Location: Volume 2 · §4.6.2 · Exercise 29,687

Choosing t uniformly of degree < r*d makes the CRT residues independent, but their square-character bits have probability (q-1)/(2q), q=p^d, not exactly 1/2. Thus the entire stated fair-bit assumption does not become rigorously true merely by enlarging the…

Choosing t uniformly of degree < r*d makes the CRT residues independent,
but their square-character bits have probability (q-1)/(2q), q=p^d,
not exactly 1/2. Thus the entire stated fair-bit assumption does not
become rigorously true merely by enlarging the degree range.
For p=3,d=1,r=2 the splitting probability is 4/9, with mean 9/4 trials.

The same asymptotic cost bound holds with the biased probabilities.
Indeed, counting surviving pairs proves it even for degree < 2d.
This is a correction to an exact auxiliary assertion, not an algorithm failure.

## Publication review, 12 November 2026

Choosing t of degree below rd makes CRT coordinates independent, but the resulting split bits have probability (p^d−1)/(2p^d), not 1/2. Thus the final claim that the fair-trie assumption becomes rigorous is false. For p=3,d=1,r=2 the split probability is 4/9, with mean 9/4. The stated asymptotic bound can still be proved by the recorded pair-separation argument.

Decision: `retained`. No matching prior report was found in the checked sources; absolute priority is not established.
