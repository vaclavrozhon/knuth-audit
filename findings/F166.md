# F166 — The Friedman-urn probabilities use the preceding Eulerian row

**Confirmed · medium severity.** 1st edition · 2nd printing · 2022.

Location: 4B / Mathematical Preliminaries Redux, answer74, p.381

Use A(n+1,r−1)/(n+1)!. A complete insertion bijection and the probability recurrence prove this correction for every level. The diagram and the neighboring martingale remain valid.

Answer74 prints A(n,r−1)/n! for level n. At the first level this gives probabilities1 and0, although the original diagram correctly gives1/2 to each node. The following permutation explanation also uses n+1 elements.

## Publication review, 12 November 2026

Answer MPR74: the distribution is A(n+1,r-1)/(n+1)!, where A is the Eulerian number, rather than A(n,r-1)/n!. At the explicitly labelled level 1, the diagram gives 1/2,1/2 while the printed formula gives 1,0.
