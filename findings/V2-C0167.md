# V2-C0167 — The padding rule leaves its cube-root boundary unprotected

**Confirmed · low severity.** 3rd edition · 1st printing · 1997.

Location: Volume 2 · §4.5.4 · Answer 32, p. 664

One boundary message is sent as an ordinary integer cube and can be read by taking its cube root.

With M=floor(cuberoot(N)), the branch x≥M leaves x=M unpadded, although M³<N for the RSA modulus. For N=55, M=3 and x=3, the transmitted value is 27; x is inside the permitted message range. The known erratum changing the upper bound on random y does not fix this branch. The note gives a complete randomized repair using ceil(cuberoot(N)), separated representative ranges and an explicit decoder. Its scope is this small-message leakage, not general encryption security.

## Publication review, 12 November 2026

With N=55 and M=floor(cuberoot(N))=3, the branch x>=M leaves x=3 unpadded, and ciphertext 27 reveals its integer cube root. The 2004 correction to the padding range's upper endpoint is separate. The stored ceiling-and-disjoint-ranges repair addresses this particular low-message defect and is not a claim of modern cryptographic security.

Decision: `retained`. No matching prior report was found in the checked sources; absolute priority is not established.
