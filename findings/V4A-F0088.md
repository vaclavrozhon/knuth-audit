# V4A-F0088 — The number of occupied diagonals need not decrease

**Confirmed · low severity.** 1st edition · 1st printing · 2011.

Location: 4A / Answer 7.2.1.4–70, continuation on page 761, printed 761

Replace the claimed decrease in the number of diagonals by a decrease in the sum of their indices over all occupied cells.

The sorting move in the Bulgarian-solitaire proof can leave the number of occupied diagonals unchanged. A sum of cell diagonal indices gives the intended strict decrease.

## Publication review, 12 November 2026

Answer 7.2.1.4-70, p. 761: sorting the displaced top row need not decrease the number of occupied diagonals. The transition 43 through (2,3,2) to 322 has four occupied diagonals throughout. The sum of diagonal indices over occupied cells does decrease, from 19 to 18 here, and repairs the potential argument.

Decision: `retained`. No matching prior report was found in the checked sources; absolute priority is not established.
