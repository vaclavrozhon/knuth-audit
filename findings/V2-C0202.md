The unrestricted fourth-degree time bound appears to fail for literal
Algorithm R with dense classical integer multiplication: Algorithm M
with its optional zero-digit test M2 omitted. This model qualification
is essential; the example is not a lower bound for all implementations.

For any integer D >= 2 set y=x^D, W=1+x+...+x^(D-1), and
  A=y^4+y^3+y^2+1, B=y^3-y^2-y+1,
  U=W(A+B), V=WA.
Both inputs are monic of degree n=5D-1 with coefficients bounded by N=2.
After the initial equal-degree step, Algorithm C has successive
nonzero remainders
  WB, W(4y^2+y-1), 4^(D-1)W(-7y+11), 32(-7)^(D-1)W.
These follow from
  A=(y+2)B+(4y^2+y-1),
  16B=(4y-5)(4y^2+y-1)+(-7y+11),
  49(4y^2+y-1)=(-28y-51)(-7y+11)+512.

During pseudodivision of C=W(4y^2+y-1) by E=4^(D-1)W(-7y+11),
put a=lc(E)=-7*4^(D-1). The degree difference is D. After the first
iteration k=D, the whole highest coefficient block cancels. For
D>k>=1 all subsequent quotient coefficients are zero. Before step k,
the low coefficients r_j equal -a^(D-k) for 0<=j<D. With the optimization
in the parenthesis of R2, the same identity holds whenever k<=j<D.

Restrict to 1<=k<=floor(D/4), ceil(D/2)<=j<D. Both variants perform
Theta(D^2) products a*r_j with operand lengths Theta(D) and Theta(D^2).
Dense schoolbook multiplication therefore requires Omega(D^5) digit-pair
iterations, contradicting O(D^4 log^2 D). The raw remainder is
512(-7)^(D-1)4^(D^2-1)W; C3 removes the large factor only afterwards.

The fourth-degree upper bound does hold if all degree differences are
bounded by a fixed constant. Without that restriction, direct classical
analysis gives O(n^5(log(Nn))^2). Collins's cited 1969 treatment assumes
a weakly normal sequence in Theorems 3.13/3.14 and Corollary 3.17.
Brown, ACM TOMS 4 (1978), pp. 245-247 and 249, likewise distinguishes
the normal and abnormal degree cases in his qualitative dense model.

Possible repairs are to restrict the exercise to bounded degree drops,
or to weaken its general upper bound. Optimizations that retain powers
separately, skip zero digits, or use faster multiplication are outside
this particular lower bound; the example does not rule out a different
implementation attaining the stated time.

The scan and all six available errata layers were checked. No matching
correction was found. The latest physical printing and priority have
not been checked. No report has been sent.

## Publication review, 12 November 2026

The unrestricted fourth-degree time bound is contradicted by the recorded family under literal Algorithm R and dense schoolbook integer multiplication with optional M2 omitted. This is a model-specific lower bound, not a lower bound for every implementation. The known distinction between normal and abnormal degree drops is not a new discovery. Details below.

Decision: `retained`. No matching prior report was found in the checked sources; absolute priority is not established.
