# V3-C0013 — The Mellin integral is missing a nonzero remainder

**Confirmed · medium severity.** 2nd edition · 1st printing · 1998.

Location: Volume 3 / answer 5.2.2-46, p. 636

A periodic asymptotic expression is presented as the exact value of an integral.

The integral equals J_s(n)=sum over j>=1 of (n/2^j)^s exp(-n/2^j), so J_s(2n)-J_s(n)=n^s exp(-n)>0. The printed constant plus delta_s(n) is unchanged by doubling n. An exponentially small tail is missing. Related published corrections fix the main text and answer 48; no correction to this value in answer 46 was found. Original typesetting and all relevant archived errata layers checked; no matching correction found.

## Publication review, 12 November 2026

The integral in answer 46 on p. 636 is J_s(n)=sum over j>=1 of (n/2^j)^s exp(-n/2^j). Its difference J_s(2n)-J_s(n)=n^s exp(-n) is nonzero. The printed constant plus periodic term cannot be an exact equality. This local missing remainder is separate from the already published remainder corrections to the main text and answer 48.

Decision: `retained`. No matching prior report was found in the checked sources; absolute priority is not established.
