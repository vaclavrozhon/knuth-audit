# V4A-F0004 — The five-color torus claim includes a graph that needs six colors

**Confirmed · medium severity.** 1st edition · 1st printing · 2011.

Location: 4A / answer 7–126, p. 530, remaining five-color case

The stated optimum and construction fail for the omitted family with one cycle of length 3.

Exercise 7–126 explicitly allows m,n ≥ 3. After handling 3 by 3, 3 by 4 and 3 by 5, the answer claims five colors suffice otherwise. But C3 strong C6 contains a K6: take all three rows in two adjacent columns. It therefore needs at least six colors, and alternating two disjoint three-color palettes achieves exactly six. A period-3 sequence with steps ±1 modulo 5 cannot exist either. The original exercise, product definition and answer were checked. All five relevant 4A/F0 errata layers contain only a bibliographic year correction for answer 126, which does not fix this error.

## Publication review, 12 November 2026

Answer 7-126, p. 530: the asserted five-color rule misses further three-row tori. The 3 by 6 kingwise torus contains K6 and has a six-coloring. The cited paper restricts its five-color theorem to odd cycles longer than three.

Decision: `retained`. No matching prior report was found in the checked sources; absolute priority is not established.
