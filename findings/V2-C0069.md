# V2-C0069 — The argument confuses prediction with a success test

**Confirmed · medium severity.** 3rd edition · 1st printing · 1997.

Location: Volume 2 · §3.5

The printed construction can predict the wrong bit with probability 1. The lemma is true after separating the predictor from its success test.

In the proof of P2, A_k^P applied to the padded string gives the
complement of the desired prediction. Equation (39) defines A_k^P
as the success indicator F_k xor B_(k+1) xor B'_(k+1); the predicted
bit itself is A_k = F_k xor B'_(k+1) xor 1.

Take N=1, S={1}, and A(B)=B. This is a shift-symmetric source with
P(A,S)-P(A,U_1)=1/2. Necessarily k=0, and F_0=C, a fresh fair bit.
The printed construction yields A'=A_0^P(0)=C xor C=0, so it predicts
the actual bit 1 incorrectly with probability one. Using A_0 instead
gives C xor C xor 1=1, as required.

In both branches of the proof of P2, replace A_k^P by A_k. The final
probability 1/2 should refer to a correct prediction on a uniform
input, not to the probability that the predictor outputs 1.
Likewise, the final sentence of answer 41 should describe the
probability of a correct prediction. For example, with N=1, S={0},
and A(B)=1-B, the repaired predictor always outputs 0 but is always
correct; the displayed sum in answer 41 is 1.

The statements of P2 and exercise 41 remain valid. The hybrid identity
and the downstream reductions have been proved with these two roles
kept separate, and the smallest example has been checked exactly.

The original pages were visually inspected. All four historical and
current errata layers, plus the current err2 TeX, were searched on
1 November 2026. The 30 June 2024 addition to P4 concerns running-time
notation and does not repair this construction. No matching correction
was found. Priority and a later physical printing remain unverified.

## Publication review, 12 November 2026

The construction substitutes a prediction-success indicator for a predicted bit. For `N=1,S={1},A(B)=B`, the printed construction predicts zero with certainty. Using the predictor itself returns one. The answer to exercise 41 has the same distinction between output probability and success probability. The reduction statements remain true.

Decision: `retained`. No matching prior report was found in the checked sources; absolute priority is not established.
