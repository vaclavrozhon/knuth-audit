The audit dismissed this concern as a book error.

The counterexamples to applying the later first-fit bounds to the
particular Algorithm A are valid. Algorithm A reserves the upper end
of the first adequate hole; the later bounds require lowest-address
placement. A seven-cell pool with maximum live volume five and maximum
request two already distinguishes the conventions. A 43-cell pool,
live volume thirteen, and maximum request five also disproves the
harmonic bound for this particular implementation.

However, exercise 2.5-43 explicitly asks the reader to find a defensive
strategy. The first-fit remark in answer 38 says "a first-fit strategy".
Both formulations permit choosing the lower end of the selected hole.
They do not assert the same guarantee for every first-fit variant or
specifically for Algorithm A. The proposed mathematical finding is
therefore withdrawn after direct review of pages 456 and 616.

An explicit reminder about the placement convention might improve the
exposition, but no false theorem has been established. The traces are
retained as evidence about the difference between variants, not as a
counterexample to Robson's theorem or to the stated existence claim.
