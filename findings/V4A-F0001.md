# V4A-F0001 — Contracting an arbitrary triangulation edge can remove four edges

**Confirmed · medium severity.** 1st edition · 1st printing · 2011.

Location: 4A / answer 7–46(b), p.521

The induction step can leave the class of simple maximal planar graphs; the formula e=3n−6 remains correct.

Take the triangular bipyramid with edges ab,bc,ca,ad,bd,cd,ae,be,ce. It is a maximal planar graph with 5 vertices and 9 edges. Contracting ab and merging parallel edges leaves zc,zd,ze,cd,ce: 4 vertices and 5 edges, so four edges disappear. Following the printed instruction to delete only three edges instead leaves a multigraph. Thus the proof’s arbitrary-edge contraction step is false. Original typesetting and five relevant 4A/F0 errata layers were checked; no matching correction was found.

## Publication review, 12 November 2026

Answer 7-46(b), p. 521: contracting an arbitrary edge of a maximal planar graph need not remove exactly three edges and preserve maximality. An equatorial edge of the triangular bipyramid has three common neighbors; simplifying after contraction leaves five edges on four vertices. The Euler-formula conclusion remains correct.

Decision: `retained`. No matching prior report was found in the checked sources; absolute priority is not established.
