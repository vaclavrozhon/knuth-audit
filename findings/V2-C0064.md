# V2-C0064 — The sequence of points is not unique

**Confirmed · medium severity.** 3rd edition · 1st printing · 1997.

Location: Volume 2 · §3.5 · Exercise 21(b)

Reflection gives a different point sequence with the same gaps. Uniqueness holds for gap lengths, not for the points themselves.

The stated uniqueness of the sequence of points is false. Reflection
preserves every gap length. Put

W_n = lg(2n+1) mod 1,
  Z_0 = 0, and Z_n = 1-W_n for n >= 1.

For n >= 1, W_n is strictly between 0 and 1, since the odd integer
2n+1 is not a power of two. Thus Z is also an admissible [0..1)
sequence starting at zero. For every n, the first n points of Z have
exactly the same multiset of gaps as the first n points of W. Both
therefore satisfy all the stated inequalities, with equality.
Nevertheless W_1=lg(3/2), whereas Z_1=lg(4/3).

A possible correction is to assert that every sequence satisfying the
inequalities has the same sorted gap lengths as W, for every n. The
unique object is the sequence of gap multisets, not the point sequence.
This agrees with Theorem 2 in Ramshaw's original Stanford report
CS-TR-77-637 (1977), whose strategies are defined in terms of lengths.
The minimax conclusion in part (c) is unaffected.

The original page, all2-prepre, all2-pre, all2, err2, and current err2
TeX have been checked; no corresponding correction was found.
The newest physical printing and priority have not been checked.
Nothing has been sent.

## Publication review, 12 November 2026

Reflection about 1/2, with the initial zero retained, gives a different admissible point sequence with exactly the same multiset of gaps at every stage. Its second point is `log_2(4/3)` rather than `log_2(3/2)`. The uniqueness claim can concern gap multisets, not the point sequence. The minimax conclusion is unaffected.

Decision: `retained`. No matching prior report was found in the checked sources; absolute priority is not established.
