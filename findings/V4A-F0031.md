# V4A-F0031 — Double precision can be one bit too short for the subset-sum gap

**Confirmed · medium severity.** 1st edition · 1st printing · 2011.

Location: 4A / Answer 7.1.3-216, printed page 620: sufficient arithmetic width

The stated precision guarantee permits overflow that changes the computed answer; the O(m) algorithm with sufficient precision is unaffected.

Under n>=floor(log2 m), two n-bit words do not always hold the bucket sums or the first missing subset sum. With n=2 and inputs (1,2,3,3,3,3,3), the answer is 19.

## Publication review, 12 November 2026

Answer 216, p. 620: under the printed floor-logarithm premise, two n-bit words can be too short. With n=2 and inputs 1,2,3,3,3,3,3, the bucket sum is 17 and the first missing subset sum is 19, both above 15. This concerns the precision claim, not the linear-time algorithm with sufficient width.

Decision: `retained`. No matching prior report was found in the checked sources; absolute priority is not established.
