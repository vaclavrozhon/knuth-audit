# V2-C0238 — The exceptional-set union does not cover R

**Confirmed · medium severity.** 3rd edition · 1st printing · 1997.

Location: Volume 2 · See attached source pages

In the third edition, first printing, the final paragraph of the proof defines R_i by A_(2i-1)=0 and R' by requiring all original alpha's to be nonzero, and asserts that R is their union. This union can miss an output.

In the third edition, first printing, the final paragraph of the proof
defines R_i by A_(2i-1)=0 and R' by requiring all original alpha's to be
nonzero, and asserts that R is their union. This union can miss an output.

Consider the permitted chain

lambda_0=x, lambda_1=lambda_0+lambda_0, lambda_2=a*lambda_1.

Here q=1. At the only addition, T1=T2=x, hence A1=A2=1. Consequently
R1 is empty. R' consists of the nonzero multiples of x, whereas R also
contains the zero polynomial, obtained when a=0.

One correction is to define R' by the nonvanishing of the actual
normalizing denominators A_(2i-1), rather than all original alpha's.
Alternatively, no exceptional-case decomposition is needed: the
polynomial relations proved for nonzero alpha's extend to all alpha's
by continuity, since coefficient functions of the original chain are
polynomials. Theorem A itself is unaffected.

The 2019 wording change concerns the preceding paragraph and does not
address this union.

## Publication review, 12 November 2026

The asserted equality of result sets on p. 496 excludes valid zero intermediates even when they are never denominators. The chain x, x+x, a(x+x), with a=0, witnesses the failure. Restrict exclusions to actual denominators, or use the polynomial-identity continuation argument; the theorem survives.

Decision: `retained`. No matching prior report was found in the checked sources; absolute priority is not established.
