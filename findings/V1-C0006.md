# V1-C0006 — The generalized q-binomial series needs a stronger convergence condition

**Confirmed · high severity.** 3rd edition · 1st printing · 1997.

Location: Volume 1 · §1.2.6 · Exercise 58

The extension of the q-nomial theorem in the last paragraph needs a stronger convergence condition when n is negative. The stated conditions |q|<1 and |x|<1 do not suffice for arbitrary real n.

The extension of the q-nomial theorem in the last paragraph needs a
stronger convergence condition when n is negative. The stated conditions
|q|<1 and |x|<1 do not suffice for arbitrary real n.

Take q=1/2, n=-1, and x=3/4. From the product definition in (40),

binom(-1,k)_q = (-1)^k q^(-k(k+1)/2),   k >= 0.

Consequently the kth term of the proposed series is

binom(-1,k)_q q^(k(k-1)/2) x^k = (-x/q)^k = (-3/2)^k.

The terms do not tend to zero. Meanwhile the finite products telescope:

product_{j=0}^N (1+q^j x)/(1+q^(j-1) x)
    = (1+q^N x)/(1+q^(-1) x) -> 2/5.

All factors in this example are finite and nonzero, so neither singular
parameters nor a choice of branch is involved.

A sufficient repair is to add |q^n x|<1. More precisely, for
0<|q|<1 and a fixed value of q^n, the product is analytic on
|q^n x|<1. Its functional equation

(1+q^n x) F(x) = (1+x) F(qx),   F(0)=1,

gives exactly the coefficients in the exercise. For nonterminating
parameters the ratio of consecutive series terms tends to -q^n x.
Thus one may replace the stated |x|<1 by |q^n x|<1, or retain it and
add the latter condition. Nonnegative integral n gives a finite sum.

## Publication review, 12 November 2026

The permitted negative parameter makes the summands fail to tend to zero despite a convergent product.
