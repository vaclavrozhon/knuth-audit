# V2-C0145 — The concentration estimate needs a restriction on epsilon

**Confirmed · low severity.** 3rd edition · 1st printing · 1997.

Location: Volume 2 · Section 4.5.3 · Printed page 372

For epsilon=4, the printed upper bound on the number of exceptions tends to zero, but (1,1) is always exceptional.

For epsilon=4, the printed upper bound on the number of exceptions tends to zero, but (1,1) is always exceptional. This contradicts the stated quantifier over all positive epsilon. It does not refute the concentration result for small epsilon.

## Publication review, 12 November 2026

The explicit range of all positive epsilon permits epsilon=4. Its stated exception bound then tends to zero, while (1,1) remains an exception for every N. This also survives a fixed multiplicative constant. Dixon's own 1971 recap repeats the same wording, so this is an inherited missing-range qualification, not a demonstrated misquotation or a refutation of small-epsilon concentration.

Decision: `retained`. No matching prior report was found in the checked sources; absolute priority is not established.
