# V4A-F0010 — The generalized bent construction permutes the wrong variable half

**Confirmed · medium severity.** 1st edition · 1st printing · 2011.

Location: 4A / answer7.1.1–132(b), p.560, final parenthesis

The proposed extension can produce a function that is not bent, even after the inconsistent dimension notation is repaired.

Let u=(x1,x3,x5), v=(x2,x4,x6), pi(u)=(u1,u2,u3 XOR u1u2), and h(v)=v1v2. The printed pi(u) dot v XOR h(v) agrees with affine x5 on40 of64 inputs, but a six-variable bent function allows at most36. Its Walsh coefficient there is16 instead of magnitude8. The correct generalization is u dot pi(v) XOR h(v), or pi(u) dot v XOR h(u). The original identity-permutation exercise is valid. The parenthesis also needs consistent m-bit/2m-variable notation. Original typography, relevant errata and public reports were checked; no matching correction was located.

## Publication review, 12 November 2026

Answer 7.1.1-132(b), p. 560: the generalized bent construction uses the permutation on the wrong half of the variables relative to the arbitrary added function. The six-variable example in the detailed note has Walsh coefficient 16, hence an affine agreement count of 40 instead of the bent maximum 36. The standard construction uses u dot g(v)+h(v), or g(u) dot v+h(u).

Decision: `retained`. No matching prior report was found in the checked sources; absolute priority is not established.
