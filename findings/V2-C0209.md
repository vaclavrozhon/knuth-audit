The condition d>=1 should be d>=2. For p=3,d=1, part (i) with t=0
has one polynomial, x-1, whereas part (ii) with t1=0,t2=1 has none.
In general the counts for d=1 are (p-1)/2 and (p-3)/2.
The fractional-linear bijection in the answer works for d>1.
This also removes the exception to "exactly half" for odd d.

## Publication review, 12 November 2026

Exercise 31 explicitly permits d=1, but its two counts differ by one in that case: (p−1)/2 versus (p−3)/2. For p=3 the counts are one and zero. The fractional-linear proof works for d>=2; the d=1 exception needs exclusion or separate treatment.

Decision: `retained`. No matching prior report was found in the checked sources; absolute priority is not established.
