# V1-C0025 — Medieval arithmetic was not confined to computing Easter

**Confirmed · low severity.** 3rd edition · 1st printing · 1997.

Location: Volume 1 · §1.3.2 · Exercise 14 · Printed page 518

Fibonacci’s documented compound-interest calculation contradicts the broad claim that Easter was the sole nontrivial application of arithmetic in medieval Europe.

The original page image has been inspected; no physical copy checked.

The statement about the "sole nontrivial application of arithmetic in
Europe during the Middle Ages" is too broad.

A concrete counterexample is Fibonacci's Liber abaci, chapter 12,
the eighteen-year investment problem. It computes 100*(5/4)^18 using
repeated squaring and rational arithmetic. See Laurence Sigler's
translation, Fibonacci's Liber Abaci (Springer, 2002), p.439; the
corresponding Boncompagni page marker is 313. This is a medieval
European application to compound interest, independent of Easter.

Suggested replacement: "The calculation of Easter was an important
application of arithmetic in medieval Europe, hence such algorithms
are historically significant."

The four historical/current Volume 1 errata layers were searched on
2026-11-01, by page and subject. The nearby changes concern brackets,
optimization, Aquitaine, and bibliographical updates; no correction
of this assertion was found. This is a historical wording correction,
not a defect in Algorithm E or its MIX implementation. No priority
or author acceptance is claimed; nothing has been sent.
