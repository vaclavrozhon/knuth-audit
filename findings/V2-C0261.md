# V2-C0261 — Induced-series proof omits the constant-term normalization

**Confirmed · low severity.** 3rd edition · 1st printing · 1997.

Location: Volume 2 · Answer 4.7-22(c) · Printed page 723

The coefficient identity remains valid. Divide out the common factor U0^(x+n*alpha) before applying the polynomial-identity argument.

For U(z)=2+z, the induced series has linear coefficient 2^alpha. The proof incorrectly calls these coefficients polynomials in alpha under the assumption U0≠0.

## Publication review, 12 November 2026

The polynomial-in-alpha argument on p. 723 needs normalization by the nonunit constant term. For U=2+z the induced series has linear coefficient 2^alpha. Divide out the common factor U0^(x+n alpha) before using the polynomial identity principle. The desired coefficient identity is correct.

Decision: `retained`. No matching prior report was found in the checked sources; absolute priority is not established.
