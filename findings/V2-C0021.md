# V2-C0021 — Floating-point rounding can pass the final collision-test threshold

**Confirmed · medium severity.** 3rd edition · 1st printing · 1997.

Location: Volume 2 · §3.3.2 I · Algorithm S3 · p. 71

The floating-point algorithm needs an explicit bound check and probability clipping. The table and exact recurrence are correct.

For n=2 and m=10 or 10018, evaluating the printed operations in binary64 produces a cumulative sum of 1+2^(-52). Step S3 reports a negative probability and its literal inner loop can run beyond the final threshold. An immediate global stop could prevent the overrun, but the negative output occurs first. Exact arithmetic does not have this counterexample. Rechecked against the original page and all errata layers on November 6, 2026. Latest physical printing and priority remain unverified.

## Publication review, 12 November 2026

The stated floating-point operations can pass the final probability sentinel and emit a negative probability; exact arithmetic does not have this defect.

Decision: `retained`. No matching prior report was found in the checked sources; absolute priority is not established.
