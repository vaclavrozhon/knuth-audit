# V1-C0045 — The infinite-tree example has one distinct cardinal level

**Candidate · medium severity.** 3rd edition · 1st printing · 1997.

Location: Volume 1 · §2.3 · Exercise 11(c), answer p.562

The example satisfies the degree and ancestor conditions, but its wording does not clearly distinguish chain length from the number of distinct cardinal levels.

Candidate: the answer defines a node’s level as the cardinality of its proper ancestors. Every node of the proposed construction has continuum many ancestors, so there is only one distinct cardinal level. The construction does have an uncountable ancestor chain. The issue remains open because “uncountably many levels” may refer informally to that chain.
