# V1-C0013 — Missing prefactor in the exponential tail bound

**Confirmed · high severity.** 3rd edition · 1st printing · 1997.

Location: Volume 1 · §1.2.10 · Exercise 13

The exponential-tail bound requires a multiplicative constant. For n=2, X_n is uniform on {0,1}, and mu_n=sigma_n=1/2. Thus |(X_n-mu_n)/sigma_n|=1 surely. At x=1/2 the claimed inequality would say 1 < exp(-a/2), impossible for any a>0.

The exponential-tail bound requires a multiplicative constant.
For n=2, X_n is uniform on {0,1}, and mu_n=sigma_n=1/2.
Thus |(X_n-mu_n)/sigma_n|=1 surely. At x=1/2 the claimed
inequality would say 1 < exp(-a/2), impossible for any a>0.

A correction is C exp(-a x), for some positive constants C and a,
with n>=2 and x>=0. Definition 4.1 in the cited Flajolet-Soria
paper (1993), page 171, includes just such a constant C.
For this particular distribution one can take a=1/2 and
C=2 exp(1/4), using its representation as a sum of independent
Bernoulli variables with success probabilities 1/2,...,1/n.
