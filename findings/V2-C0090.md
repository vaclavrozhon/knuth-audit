# V2-C0090 — The priority claim for round-to-even is too late

**Confirmed · low severity.** 3rd edition · 1st printing · 1997.

Location: Volume 2 · §4.2.2

Primary sources from 1882 and 1906 predate the suggested 1930 origin; no absolute priority is established.

The historical paragraph suggests that round-to-even was first
mentioned in Scarborough's Numerical Mathematical Analysis (1930).
There are substantially earlier explicit descriptions.

Robert S. Woodward, Probability and Theory of Errors, fourth edition
(New York: Wiley, 1906), p. 42, describes the rule that makes the last
rounded figure even when exactly half a unit is discarded. The title
page explicitly dates this edition 1906.

Simon Newcomb, Logarithmic and Other Mathematical Tables (Henry Holt;
copyright 1882), pp. 14-15, also recommends the nearest even result
for exact halves, with examples such as .26181/2 -> .13090 and
.26183/2 -> .13092. The scanned title page is undated, but its copyright
page clearly says 1882. I do not infer the particular impression's
printing date solely from that copyright notice.

Scans:
https://archive.org/details/probabilitytheor00wooduoft/page/42
https://archive.org/details/logarithmicother00newcrich/page/n24

I suggest replacing the tentative priority statement with these earlier
references, without claiming that either is the first occurrence.

Checked against the third edition, first printing (1997), and all four
available historical/current errata layers. I found no correction of
this paragraph. A recent physical printing has not been checked.

## Publication review, 12 November 2026

The tentative attribution of the first half-to-even rule to Scarborough (1930) is contradicted by Woodward's 1906 title page and explicit rule on p. 42, and by Newcomb's rule on pp. 14–15 in a volume with 1882 copyright. The Newcomb copyright is not an independently established printing date. The earlier rule is already discussed publicly; retain only the correction to the book's historical statement, not priority for finding that rule.

Decision: `retained`. No matching prior report was found in the checked sources; absolute priority is not established.
