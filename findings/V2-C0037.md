# V2-C0037 — The largest-quotient lower bound fails

**Confirmed · medium severity.** 3rd edition · 1st printing · 1997.

Location: Volume 2 · §3.3.4 · Exercise 6 and answer · pp. 115, 578

Exercise 6 overstates the guaranteed spectral accuracy; the algorithm itself remains correct.

Take prime m=131 and primitive multiplier a=97. The partial quotients are 1, 2, 1, 5, 1, 4, so A=5. The vector (5, 4) is admissible and has squared length 41; hence mu2 <= 41*pi/131 < 2*pi/(5+1+1/5). The proof incorrectly bounds 131/20 by 5+1+1/5. Replacing the denominator by A+2 gives a valid weaker bound, proved using adjacent convergent ratios and AM–GM. Original pages 115/578 and all errata layers rechecked on 6 November 2026; no applicable correction found. Priority and later physical printing unverified.

## Publication review, 12 November 2026

For `m=131,a=97`, the largest continued-fraction quotient is 5, while the dual vector `(5,4)` has squared norm 41. Hence the asserted strict bound would require `41/131>10/31`, which is false. The multiplier is primitive: its powers at exponents 65, 26 and 10 are 130, 89 and 80 modulo 131. The proposed weaker bound with denominator `A+2` is consistent with the proof.

Decision: `retained`. No matching prior report was found in the checked sources; absolute priority is not established.
