# V2-C0217 — Incorrect power of pi in the Gaussian integral

**Confirmed · medium severity.** 3rd edition · 1st printing · 1997.

Location: Volume 2 · §4.6.2 · Exercise 21(c),684

The denominator should be n! * pi^t, not n! * pi^n: n is the homogeneous degree and t is the number of complex variables.

The denominator should be n! * pi^t, not n! * pi^n: n is the
homogeneous degree and t is the number of complex variables.

For u(z)=z^2 in one variable, the squared Bombieri norm is 1 and
the integral is 2*pi. The printed expression therefore equals 1/pi.
More generally, integrating each complex monomial gives pi^t * j!,
which yields the corrected normalization directly.

The product inequality and the coefficient bound are unaffected.
The same formula occurs in the second-edition supplement on p. 627.

## Publication review, 12 November 2026

The Gaussian integral in answer 21(c), p. 684, needs pi to the number of variables, not to the degree. For one variable and u=z², the integral is 2pi and the printed normalization yields 1/pi instead of 1.

Decision: `retained`. No matching prior report was found in the checked sources; absolute priority is not established.
