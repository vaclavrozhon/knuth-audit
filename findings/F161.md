# F161 — The Azuma proof uses /4 where the individual factors need /8

**Confirmed · low severity.** 1st edition · 2nd printing · 2022.

Location: 4B / Mathematical Preliminaries Redux, answer 90(e), p. 383

Replace /4 by /8 only in the individual product factors. The final exp(ct²/4), the optimization and the fixed-width theorem remain correct. This is separate from the predictable-width extension on p. 9.

In answer 90(e), the individual factors exp(c_k²t²/4) multiply to exp(ct²/2), since c=Σc_k²/2. The answer instead identifies their constant term with exp(ct²/4). Already for one fair ±1/2 increment and t=c1=1, the printed product has expectation exp(1/4), not exp(1/8).

## Publication review, 12 November 2026

Answer MPR90(e): each individual exponential needs denominator 8, not 4. With c=sum(c_k^2)/2, the printed product's constant is exp(c*t^2/2), not exp(c*t^2/4). This is separate from the predictable-width error.
