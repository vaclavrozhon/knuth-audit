# V4A-F0006 — The random-Horn satisfiability limit is too large

**Confirmed · medium severity.** 1st edition · 1st printing · 2011.

Location: 4A / exercise 7.1.1–50, p. 86, limiting satisfiability probability

The probability formula in exercise 50 is false for the stated uniform sampling model; the Horn-clause count and core algorithm remain valid.

At c=10, the printed infinite-product formula is at least exp(-10). An independent least-model argument instead proves limsup P_n(10) <= exp(-18) + exp(-12)/(1-exp(-6)) < exp(-10). The proof bounds disjoint supporting-clause events, uses a rigorous one-sided Poisson comparison with the fixed sample size, and controls every model size. It uses no simulation and does not assert an unproved replacement limit. The original exercise and all five relevant 4A/F0 errata layers were checked.

## Publication review, 12 November 2026

Exercise 7.1.1-50, p. 86: the displayed random-Horn limit fails for the specified uniform clause model. At c=10 the printed expression is at least exp(-10), but the least-model union bound and Poisson coupling in the detailed note give limsup at most exp(-18)+exp(-12)/(1-exp(-6)), which is smaller. The argument checks the actual normalized clause count and does not assume a replacement limiting law.
