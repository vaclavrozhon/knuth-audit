# V2-C0166 — The RSA root-density bound multiplies two conditions incorrectly

**Confirmed · medium severity.** 3rd edition · 1st printing · 1997.

Location: Volume 2 · §4.5.4 · p. 405

The stated probability bound fails when m is divisible by one of the two large prime factors.

The book claims that either p1·q1 divides m or the density of x^m=1 modulo pq is below 1/(p1·q1). For p=23, q=47, p1=11, q1=23 and m=22, exactly 44 of 1081 residues satisfy the congruence, exceeding 1/253, although 253 does not divide 22. The product bound requires both p1 and q1 to be absent from m. If only one is absent, the corresponding single-factor bound is available. This corrects the mathematical estimate; it is not a general attack on RSA.

## Publication review, 12 November 2026

For safe primes p=23,q=47 and exponent m=22, the root fraction is 44/1081, exceeding 1/253 although 253 does not divide m. The exact count is gcd(m,p-1)*gcd(m,q-1). The small product bound requires both individual large factors to be absent; their product merely failing to divide m is insufficient.

Decision: `retained`. No matching prior report was found in the checked sources; absolute priority is not established.
