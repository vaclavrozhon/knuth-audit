# V2-C0004 — The inverse formula requires a specially lifted inverse

**Confirmed · high severity.** 3rd edition · 1st printing · 1997.

Location: Volume 2 · §3.2.1, Answer 5 · p. 543

A valid modular inverse can give an incorrect predecessor.

For m=8, a=3, c=1 and the valid inverse a′=3, the second inverse formula gives predecessor 4 instead of 0. The 2003 addition b=a−1 does not fix this. The first formula is correct; the second requires a′ to be an inverse modulo m(a−1). Rechecked against the original and current errata on November 6, 2026.

## Publication review, 12 November 2026

An ordinary modular inverse satisfies the stated condition but gives an incorrect previous state; a lifted inverse is required.

Decision: `retained`. No matching prior report was found in the checked sources; absolute priority is not established.
