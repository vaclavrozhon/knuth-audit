# V2-C0045 — A scalar product has the opposite sign

**Confirmed · low severity.** 3rd edition · 1st printing · 1997.

Location: Volume 2 · §3.3.4 · Answer 18 · p. 581

The displayed off-diagonal product has the wrong sign; the reduction example remains valid.

For V=mI+((1-m)/3)J, the product of distinct rows is (1-m²)/3. The book prints (m²-1)/3. At m=64 the correct value is -1365. Step S5 uses its absolute value, so the failure of pairwise reduction and the following improved basis are still correct. Original pages and all errata layers rechecked on 6 November 2026. Later physical printing and priority remain unverified.

## Publication review, 12 November 2026

The off-diagonal scalar product for `V=mI+yJ`, `y=(1-m)/3`, is `(1-m^2)/3`, not its negative. At `m=64` it is -1365. The test uses its absolute value, leaving the example's conclusion intact.

Decision: `retained`. No matching prior report was found in the checked sources; absolute priority is not established.
