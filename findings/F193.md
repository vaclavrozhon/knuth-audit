# F193 — Finite density bounds omit boundary terms

**Confirmed · low severity.** 1st edition · 2nd printing · 2022.

Location: 4B / DLX answer416, printed537/file557: Finite density bounds omit boundary terms

Use an O(n) boundary allowance for3-clue counts and n²−1 in the edge-deletion argument; limiting densities are unchanged.

A valid3×3 puzzle has six3-clues, and a valid2×2 zero-clue puzzle removes only four edges. These contradict both finite estimates.

## Publication review, 12 November 2026

Answer 416's finite bound for 3-clue puzzles fails on a 3x3 puzzle with six 3s and its unique 16-edge loop. It needs a boundary term for odd n. The zero-clue argument also overstates required edge deletions: a 2x2 puzzle with one corner 0 deletes four edges and leaves one cycle. The valid general lower bound uses n^2-1, not n^2+2n. Both asymptotic density conclusions survive.

Decision: `retained`. No matching prior report was found in the checked sources; absolute priority is not established.
