# V2-C0230 — Newman's construction chooses too few primes

**Confirmed · high severity.** 3rd edition · 1st printing · 1997.

Location: Volume 2 · §4.6.3

The displayed choice of r in Newman's construction does not give the stated upper bound. The bound itself remains valid after changing r.

The displayed choice of r in Newman's construction does not give the
stated upper bound. The bound itself remains valid after changing r.

Write t=ln(m), u=ln(t), and a=(ln(2))^2/2. The printed choice

r=floor((1+ln(2)/(2*lg(lg(m))))*ln(m)/ln(ln(m)))

satisfies r=(t/u)(1+a/u+O(1/u^2))+O(1). Using the expansion for p_r
cited in the answer and k=p_1*...*p_r gives

ln(k)=t+(a-1)*t/u+O(t*ln(u)/u^2).

Thus the number of multiples of k that the construction explicitly
computes is

floor(m^2/k)=m*exp((1-a+o(1))*ln(m)/ln(ln(m))).

Since a<1, this is much larger than m, whereas the claimed chain has
length m+o(m). This is an asymptotic obstruction, not a choice of the
implicit constant. Merely changing lg lg m to ln ln m is insufficient.

One valid replacement is

r=floor((1+(1+ln(2)/2)/ln(ln(m)))*ln(m)/ln(ln(m))).

Then ln(k)=t+(ln(2)/2)*t/u+O(t*ln(u)/u^2). Both m^2/k and the
precomputation bound 2^(-r)*k*ln(k) have logarithm

t-(ln(2)/2)*t/u+O(t*ln(u)/u^2),

which proves the stated upper bound for every fixed epsilon>0.

For a finite illustration, m=2^1024 gives printed r=111 and p_r=607.
The exact primorial has 831 bits; floor(m^2/k) has 1218 bits.

I checked all six available errata layers, including the older
second-edition amendment dated 1997-06-28, p. 638, answer 33, line 8,
which changes (1+ln(2)/2) to ln(2)/2. That amendment does not supply
the above correction to the formula printed in the third edition.
Without the original second-edition page I am not identifying which
occurrence it changed. No matching third-edition correction was found.

## Publication review, 12 November 2026

The Newman parameter in answer 38, p. 697, is too small to deliver the claimed m+o(m) bound. Writing t=ln(m), u=ln(t), the printed expression gives r=(t/u)(1+a/u+O(1/u²)) with a=(ln 2)²/2<1, hence m²/K=m exp((1−a+o(1))t/u), not o(m). The theorem remains true with the repaired parameter in the original report.

Decision: `retained`. No matching prior report was found in the checked sources; absolute priority is not established.
