The modified initialization can leave the coefficient domain when the two input degrees are equal. For primitive integer polynomials
  u=2x^3+x+1, v=2x^3+x^2+1,
the leading-coefficient gcd is 2, delta=0, and the first pseudo-remainder is -2x^2+2x. The special C3 branch gives v=-x^2+x but also h=2^0/2=1/2.

Thus the optimization is not an algorithm over the original UFD in this case. It remains meaningful over the fraction field; this example does not claim an incorrect final gcd.

One repair is to require strictly unequal input degrees. For equal degrees, first perform one ordinary primitive-remainder step E, handle a zero or constant remainder immediately, and otherwise restart the optimization on the new primitive pair, retaining the original common content.

## Publication review, 12 November 2026

Brown's modification permits equal input degrees but then sets h=1/c. For primitive inputs 2x³+x+1 and 2x³+x²+1, c=2, the first remainder is nonconstant and C3 produces h=1/2 outside Z. Page 429 explicitly promises h in the original coefficient domain. Restrict initial degrees to be different or perform one primitive remainder step and restart. No incorrect gcd over the fraction field is claimed.

Decision: `retained`. No matching prior report was found in the checked sources; absolute priority is not established.
