# V2-C0077 — Two middle bits do not determine high parameter bits

**Confirmed · medium severity.** 3rd edition · 1st printing · 1997.

Location: Volume 2 · §3.6 · Exercise 7

The claim that floor(X_n/2^16) mod 4 suffices to recover the 32-bit multiplier and first difference loses information about their high bits.

The claim that floor(X_n/2^16) mod 4 suffices to recover the 32-bit
multiplier and first difference loses information about their high bits.

These observations depend only on X_n modulo L=2^18. Reducing the
recurrence modulo L shows that replacing a or c by any congruent
value modulo L leaves every such observation unchanged, for all n.

For example, with X_0=0, all 2^28 pairs

a=5+u*2^18, c=1+v*2^18, 0<=u,v<2^14

have full period 2^32, satisfy a mod 8=5, and generate the same infinite
two-bit observation sequence. Both the multiplier and the first
difference (which equals c) vary across this family. The full
16-bit observations do distinguish them: a=5 and a=262149, with
c=1, give X_2=6 and 262150, whose top 16-bit values are 0 and 4.

The two bits could at most determine these parameters modulo 2^18;
the higher observed bits are necessary to determine higher parameter
bits. This issue persists with arbitrarily many observations.

The known 2009 correction only removes the Blum-Blum-Shub citation.
Original typography and all four errata layers plus saved current
TeX checked. Later physical printings and priority unverified; not sent.

## Publication review, 12 November 2026

The two middle bits depend only on the state modulo 2^18. The full-period families a=5+u*2^18 and c=1+v*2^18, with 0<=u,v<2^14 and the same initial state, therefore give identical observations forever. Both a and the first difference vary. The claim about all sixteen retained bits is separate.

Decision: `retained`. No matching prior report was found in the checked sources; absolute priority is not established.
