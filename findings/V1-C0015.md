# V1-C0015 — The added exponential estimate is too strong

**Confirmed · high severity.** 3rd edition · 1st printing · 1997.

Location: Volume 1 · 2023 errata addition to §1.2.10 · Exercise 21

The added bound omits n in its exponent and claims a stronger inequality outside its valid parameter range.

Write S(epsilon)=sum_{k>=1} 2^(2k-1) epsilon^(2k)/(2k^2-k).
The new bound has two problems.

First, n is missing from exp(-S(epsilon)). For n=2, epsilon=1/4,
S(epsilon)<=1/6<1/4=2 epsilon^2 n, contradicting the displayed
inequality exp(-S(epsilon))<=exp(-2 epsilon^2 n).

Second, even exp(-n S(epsilon)) is not an upper bound for all p.
At epsilon=1/2 the convergent series has sum log 2. If X is
binomial with n=8 and p=1/4, then

Pr(X>=6) = 277/65536 > 256/65536 = exp(-8 log 2).

For epsilon>1/2 the series even diverges, although such epsilon
is allowed when p<1/2.

A safe repair is to keep only exp(-2 epsilon^2 n), valid for all
p. Alternatively, the stronger series bound is valid after
inserting n and restricting to p>=1/2, 0<=epsilon<=1-p.
Indeed, the usual binomial rate function D_p has

D_p''(epsilon)=1/[(p+epsilon)(1-p-epsilon)]
                >=1/(1/4-epsilon^2)=S''(epsilon)

on that restricted interval. Both functions and their first
derivatives vanish at zero, so D_p>=S>=2 epsilon^2.

The amendment was inspected in all1, err1, and the source TeX.
All five available historical/current errata sources were checked
on November 1 and rechecked on November 5, 2026; no later correction was found.
Priority and a physical copy are unverified.

## Publication review, 12 November 2026

The replacement omits n and its stronger all-p estimate fails even after n is inserted.

Decision: `retained`. No matching prior report was found in the checked sources; absolute priority is not established.
