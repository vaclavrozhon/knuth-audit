# V2-C0128 — An exact logarithm is confused with its ceiling

**Confirmed · low severity.** 3rd edition · 1st printing · 1997.

Location: Volume 2 · §4.5.2 · Exercise 40

The auxiliary equality is wrong; the step counts and main upper bound are correct.

For u=1 and v=3·2^k−1, the definition gives m=log2(3·2^k−1), while the note says m=k+2. It is ceil(m) that equals k+2. For k=2, m=log2(11), not4. The recurrence-family counts are separately proved; the later empirical limit remains open.

## Publication review, 12 November 2026

The original definition is the exact logarithm m=lg(max(abs(u),abs(v))). For the example u=1,v=3*2^k-1, it is not k+2; k=2 already gives lg(11), not four. The ceiling equals k+2. The operation counts and bound are not disputed.
