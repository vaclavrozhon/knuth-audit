For n = 7, lambda(n) = 2 and the minimum signed binary weight is 2,
since 7 = 8 - 1. The stated count lambda(n) + weight(n) - 1 is 3.
However, even with both x and x^(-1) initially available, three
multiplications cannot produce x^7 for a formal indeterminate x.

After two multiplications every available exponent has absolute
value at most 4. Reaching absolute value 4 requires both operations
to create absolute values 2 and 4, so absolute value 3 is absent.
Without 4, the third product has absolute exponent at most 6;
with 4, the required other exponent 3 is unavailable.
Four multiplications suffice: x^2, x^4, x^8, x^8*x^(-1).

The signed binary representation alpha_n can have its leading digit
one position above lambda(n). The usual left-to-right method uses
m + weight(n) - 1 multiplications, where m is the index of that
leading digit; lambda(n) + weight(n) is a valid uniform upper bound.

The same count appears in the second-edition errata dated
24 August 1996, page 638, answer 30. No later correction was found
in the six available historical/current errata layers. Checked in
the scanned first printing; a newer physical printing has not been
checked. Draft only; not sent.

## Publication review, 12 November 2026

The signed binary weight count in answer 30, p. 696, undercounts n=7=8−1: it predicts three multiplications, but four are needed even when x and its inverse are available. The top signed-digit position can exceed the ordinary binary logarithm index by one.

Decision: `retained`. No matching prior report was found in the checked sources; absolute priority is not established.
