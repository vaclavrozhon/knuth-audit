The discriminant criterion needs the restriction p does not divide
the leading coefficient of pp(u). For example, 2x^2+2x+1 has
discriminant -4, but its reduction modulo 2 is the squarefree constant 1.
For a nonconstant reduction, 2x^3+2x^2+x+1 has discriminant -72
and reduces to x+1 modulo 2. Both inputs are primitive and squarefree.

For nonprimitive u, primes dividing its content must also be excluded.
The claimed finiteness and the displayed monic example remain valid.

## Publication review, 12 November 2026

The discriminant criterion needs exceptions for primes dividing the leading coefficient or content. For primitive 2x²+2x+1, the discriminant is −4, yet reduction modulo 2 is the squarefree constant 1; 2x³+2x²+x+1 similarly reduces to x+1. The exercise's finiteness conclusion remains true.
