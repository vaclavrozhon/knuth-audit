# V2-C0234 — The final saving is two divisions, not a multiplication plus division

**Confirmed · medium severity.** 3rd edition · 1st printing · 1997.

Location: Volume 2 · §4.6.4 · Exercise 6

The endpoint optimization of J1--J4 saves two divisions, rather than one multiplication and one division (for n >= 2).

The endpoint optimization of J1--J4 saves two divisions, rather than
one multiplication and one division (for n >= 2).

Put m=floor(n/2), h=ceil(n/2). The stated unoptimized counts,
n+h-1 multiplications and n divisions, are correct. At the upper
endpoint, restore u_n instead of dividing the scaled v_n by x_0^h.
At the lower endpoint, replace

x_0^m * (u_0/x_0^m + w)

by u_0 + x_0^m*w, where w is v_1 just before the j=0 operation.
This removes the initial division but leaves the multiplication.
Thus the improved counts are n+h-1 multiplications and n-2 divisions.

The printed allocation is impossible in general already for n=2:
it would give one multiplication and one division. The required
constant coefficient u_0+u_1*x_0+u_2*x_0^2 has total degree three
in the input coefficients and x_0. A straight-line program with
one multiplication, one division, and arbitrarily many additions
has growth at most quadratic under generic simultaneous scaling
of all inputs. If multiplication comes first, the division has
polynomial operands of degree at most two. If division comes first,
its affine operands give a quotient growing at most linearly,
and the subsequent multiplication gives at most quadratic growth.

Two multiplications and no divisions do suffice for n=2:

b = u_2*x_0;
    w = u_1+b;
    v_0 = u_0 + x_0*w;
    v_1 = w+b;
    v_2 = u_2.

Suggested replacement: For n >= 2, two divisions can be saved by
treating v_n and v_0 as special cases. For n=1, only one division
can be saved.

The total saving of two multiplication/division operations remains
valid for n >= 2; the algorithm's computed values are correct.

## Publication review, 12 November 2026

The endpoint simplification in answer 6, p. 698, saves two divisions, not one multiplication and one division. Restoring the top coefficient removes one division; rewriting x^m(u0/x^m+w) as u0+x^m w removes the other while retaining the multiplication. The total operation saving is still two.

Decision: `retained`. No matching prior report was found in the checked sources; absolute priority is not established.
