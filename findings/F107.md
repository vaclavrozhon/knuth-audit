# F107 — The rotational filters delete both four-queen solutions

**Confirmed · high severity.** 1st edition · 2nd printing · 2022.

Location: 4B / answer 7.2.2.1–24, pp. 420–421, final symmetry-reduction rules

Applying the printed symmetry reduction can remove every valid solution.

For n=4, both printed filters remove the two solutions 2413 and 3142, although each claimed reduction should retain one. This happens under both literal item-membership and natural geometric readings. A correct half-turn reduction tests the actual queen in row 1 and removes its orbit only when its column exceeds n/2. A correct quarter-turn reduction removes duplicate option sets. These repairs have general proofs and reproduce all tested counts through n=17, plus all 1,589 reduced n=32 models. The wording is present in both PDF and author PS; no matching erratum was found. Physical copy not yet checked.

## Publication review, 12 November 2026

DLX 24: at n=4, the stated half-turn filter rejects both 2413 and 3142, although it should retain one. The quarter-turn filter similarly removes both options. The defect survives the natural geometric reading. Filtering the actual top-row queen by k>n/2 repairs the first; deduplicating identical item sets repairs the second.

Decision: `retained`. No matching prior report was found in the checked sources; absolute priority is not established.
