# F090 — 3⅓ random bits are not enough

**Confirmed · high severity.** 1st edition · 2nd printing · 2022.

Location: 4B / answer 7.2.2–49, p. 406

The optimal expected cost of a standalone uniform choice among five options is 18/5.

An expected 3⅓ bits cannot suffice for a standalone uniform choice among five options: even a lower bound is 27/8. The exact optimum 18/5 was proved and attained by recycling. Confirmed in the PDF and PostScript, with general tests through q=40.

## Publication review, 12 November 2026

Answer 49: a separate uniform choice among five possibilities needs at least 27/8 fresh fair bits on average, already exceeding the stated 10/3. The exact optimal expectation is 18/5, obtained by summing the residual binary probabilities and attained by recycling rejections. This finding uses the single-choice model of the surrounding comparison; amortized generation of multiple outputs has different bounds.

Decision: `retained`. No matching prior report was found in the checked sources; absolute priority is not established.
