# V2-C0051 — The norm condition is missing a square

**Confirmed · low severity.** 3rd edition · 1st printing · 1997.

Location: Volume 2 · §3.3.4 · Answer 29 · p. 583

A premise in the auxiliary argument is false as printed; the final inequality has a valid independent proof.

With k nonzero centered coordinates of absolute value at least 1, the correct condition is v²≥k≥2. For m=2,a=1 and vector (1,1), v=sqrt(2)<2. The product inequality prod(x_j²)≥sum(x_j²)-k+1 proves the final bound without needing the relaxed optimizer to lie inside the coordinate box. Original pages and all errata layers rechecked on 6 November 2026. Later physical printing and priority remain unverified.

## Publication review, 12 November 2026

The premise is `v^2>=k>=2`, not `v>=k>=2`, because `v` is Euclidean length and `k` counts nonzero integer coordinates. The vector `(1,1)` already distinguishes them. The final product inequality follows from the corrected premise; an attained coordinate-box optimizer is unnecessary.

Decision: `retained`. No matching prior report was found in the checked sources; absolute priority is not established.
