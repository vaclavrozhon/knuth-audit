# V2-C0083 — Implication (36) fails for normalized zero

**Confirmed · low severity.** 3rd edition · 1st printing · 1997.

Location: Volume 2 · §4.2.2

The implication needs nonzero arguments or a smaller tolerance; it fails for normalized zero with the permitted tolerance.

The implication (36) needs an exception for zero. Page 215 explicitly
includes zero, with the minimum exponent, among normalized numbers.

For example, take radix 10, precision 8, excess 0, and exponent range
0..99. The normalized numbers

u = (0, .00000000) = 0,
    v = (0, .10000000) = 1/10

are essentially equal with epsilon = 1/10 < 1: definition (24) gives
|u-v| <= epsilon min(10^0,10^0). But the first conclusion of (36),
|u-v| <= 10 epsilon |u|, would say 1/10 <= 0. No floating point
operation or range exception is involved.

One repair is to require u and v to be nonzero in (36). Alternatively,
replace epsilon < 1 by epsilon < 1/b. This sharper threshold excludes
a zero/nonzero pair from (24) and is sharp at epsilon = 1/b.

I checked the first printing of the third edition against all2-prepre,
all2-pre, all2, and err2. The May 15, 2026 correction affects the two
lines after (37), not (36). I have not checked a later physical printing.

## Publication review, 12 November 2026

The book's normalized representation includes zero with its minimum exponent. For base 10, eight digits, excess zero and epsilon=0.1, u=0 and v=0.1 satisfy the exponent-based essential-equality definition but violate the claimed relative bound with right side zero. Excluding zero, or using epsilon<1/base, repairs this endpoint. Lomont's 2005 discussion concerns symmetry and strict comparison at two zero operands, not this implication with one nonzero operand.

Decision: `retained`. No matching prior report was found in the checked sources; absolute priority is not established.
