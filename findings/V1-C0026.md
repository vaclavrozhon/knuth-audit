# V1-C0026 — The priority claim for Victorius needs qualification

**Confirmed · low severity.** 3rd edition · 1st printing · 1997.

Location: Volume 1 · §1.3.2 · Exercise 14 · Printed page 518

Earlier repeating Easter tables qualify as systematic procedures, so the unqualified priority claim for Victorius is too broad under that interpretation.

"The first systematic algorithm for calculating the date of Easter"
appears too strong. The earlier canon traditionally attributed to
Hippolytus contains a table of Easter Sundays in seven columns of
sixteen years. The inscription and tables, with Latin translations,
are printed in Patrologia Graeca 10 (1857), cols. 875-884.
Eusebius, Ecclesiastical History VI.22.1, already describes the
sixteen-year paschal canon; see also PG 10, cols. 571-572.

Victorius himself discusses earlier cycles of 84, 95, and 112 years
and their repetition in his letter to Hilarius, section 3 (Mommsen,
Chronica minora I, MGH Auctores antiquissimi 9, 1892, pp. 678-679).
His own 532-year construction is described in section 10, pp. 683-684.

Reading a fixed table by year with cyclic repetition is a finite,
systematic procedure. This interpretation includes tabular canons
of the same general kind as the work attributed to Victorius.
It does not require their lunar models to be astronomically exact.
The transcription notes a damaged character in the earlier table;
the observation concerns the existence and structure of the earlier
procedure, not a new reconstruction of every inscription character.

A minimal revision would replace "The first systematic algorithm"
with "An important early systematic algorithm". This does not
assign a new absolute priority to Hippolytus or resolve the disputed
identity of all works traditionally attributed to him. A narrower
priority claim about a particular western 532-year cycle would need
separate qualification.

All four public errata layers were checked. The 2014 change from
Aquitania to Aquitaine does not address the priority claim; no
corresponding correction was found. The scan of the first printing
was directly inspected; later physical printings were not checked.
This report has not been sent or published.

## Publication review, 12 November 2026

Earlier tabular canons are systematic finite procedures of the kind used for Victorius; the absolute priority needs qualification.

Decision: `retained`. No matching prior report was found in the checked sources; absolute priority is not established.
