The phrase saying that (16) can be used only when (18) is nonzero seems
too strong if it refers to the evaluation scheme itself, rather than
the specific parameter formula (17).

Take u(x)=x^6-x^4, set alpha_0 through alpha_5 to zero and alpha_6=1.
Then z=x^2, w=x^2+x, and (16) gives

((z-x)w)z = x^6-x^4,

although (18) vanishes. More generally, for monic u define a=u5/3,

D=u3-2*a*u4+5*a^3,
  N=u1-a*u2+a^2*u3-a^3*u4+2*a^5.

With arbitrary alpha_1 and the remaining parameters computed by (19),
the residual of (16) minus u(x) is exactly (D*alpha_1-N)*x.
If D=N=0, the scheme works with any alpha_1, even though (17) is undefined.
For example, this applies to every even monic polynomial of degree six.

A possible clarification is to restrict the nonvanishing requirement
to determining alpha_1 by (17). The stated rational prescription itself
does require a nonzero denominator.
