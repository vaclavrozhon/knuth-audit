# V2-C0255 — Powers-tensor rank formula fails for arbitrary matrices

**Confirmed · medium severity.** 3rd edition · 1st printing · 1997.

Location: Volume 2 · Section 4.6.4 · Exercise and answer 55 · Printed pages 521 and 711

The requested general answer is false. The companion-matrix theorem remains valid; restricting the exercise to cyclic matrices repairs that special case.

For P=I2, the answer’s formula gives rank 1, but the tensor with slices I2,I2 has rank 2. A non-scalar example, P=diag(0,0,1,1), gives rank 4 rather than the stated 2. The minimum polynomial alone loses repeated invariant blocks.

## Publication review, 12 November 2026

The answer for arbitrary P on p. 711 confuses the degree of its minimum polynomial with the dimension of its representation. For P=I2 the printed tensor rank is 1, while its identity slice forces rank 2 and a two-term diagonal decomposition attains it. For diag(0,0,1,1) the discrepancy is 2 versus 4. The cyclic/companion-matrix theorem remains valid.

Decision: `retained`. No matching prior report was found in the checked sources; absolute priority is not established.
