# V2-C0157 — The Proth equivalence needs the prime-three exception

**Confirmed · low severity.** 3rd edition · 1st printing · 1997.

Location: Volume2 · printed 663

A trivial boundary is missing from the generalization. The main5*2^n+1 exercise is proved.

The allowed k=1,n=1 gives prime3, but3^1 is0 modulo3, not-1. Handle3 separately or require N>3.

## Publication review, 12 November 2026

The stated generalized criterion permits k=1,n=1,N=3, but its base-three residue is zero, not minus one. Add N>3. The actual exercise's family 5*2^n+1 is unaffected; this is a boundary exception in the more general answer.

Decision: `retained`. No matching prior report was found in the checked sources; absolute priority is not established.
