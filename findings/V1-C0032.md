# V1-C0032 — The expectation claim needs integrability

**Confirmed · high severity.** 3rd edition · 1st printing · 1997.

Location: Volume 1 · §1.3.3 · Exercise 22(c) · Printed pages 183 and 524

Every fixed-size average can be zero while the overall expectation is undefined; the claim needs an integrability or nonnegativity assumption.

The words "any function" need an integrability qualification. For w=1/2,
let I_n be the cycle structure of the identity and J_n that of a single
n-cycle. For n>=2, put phi(I_n)=2^n n! and phi(J_n)=-2^n n, and set phi=0
elsewhere (including sequences with infinite total size). This is a
finite-valued measurable function on the whole stated domain.

Under the uniform distribution on permutations of n objects, I_n has
probability 1/n! and J_n has probability 1/n. Hence phi_n=0 for every n,
and the displayed series converges absolutely to zero. However,
P(N=n)=2^(-n-1), so each n>=2 contributes 1/2 to each of E(phi^+) and
E(phi^-). Both are infinite, and E(phi) is undefined.

One could require E|phi|<infinity; alternatively, nonnegative functions
are allowed if infinite expectations are admitted. Convergence of the
displayed series alone is insufficient.

The original page images were inspected. The earlier expectation
conventions in section 1.2.10 do not supply a general integrability
assumption. All four errata layers (all1-prepre, all1-pre, all1, err1)
were checked on 2026-11-01; the known n>=1 correction to the example
in part (c) is a different issue. The author may have intended existence
of the expectation implicitly; this report concerns a missing hypothesis.

## Publication review, 12 November 2026

Finite conditional means need not define the stated unconditional expectation without integrability.

Decision: `retained`. No matching prior report was found in the checked sources; absolute priority is not established.
