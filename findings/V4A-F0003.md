# V4A-F0003 — Odd-degree construction loses the antipodal neighbor when n=d+1

**Confirmed · medium severity.** 1st edition · 1st printing · 2011.

Location: 4A / answer 7–110, p. 528, Case 1

The construction can produce the wrong degree sequence, although the requested graph exists.

For the admissible sequence 3333, take n=4, d=3, k=1 and p=4. Both printed ranges of cyclic offsets reduce to the same offset 2. They create edges 13 and 24; the added matching creates 12 and 34. The result is C4, with degree 2 at every vertex, rather than the required K4 of degree 3. The issue occurs when the antipodal offset equals its own negative. The original typesetting and all five relevant 4A/F0 errata layers were checked. The known correction d=2k>0 applies to the even-degree branch and does not fix this case.

## Publication review, 12 November 2026

Answer 7-110, p. 528: the odd-degree construction overlaps its two circulant ranges when n=d+1. For n=4,d=3,p=4, the printed construction has degree two, not three. The separate complete-graph construction in the detailed note repairs this boundary.

Decision: `retained`. No matching prior report was found in the checked sources; absolute priority is not established.
