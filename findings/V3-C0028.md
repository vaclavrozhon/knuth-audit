# V3-C0028 — A regularized Gaussian sum is missing its finite upper limit

**Confirmed · low severity.** 2nd edition · 1st printing · 1998.

Location: Volume3 / §5.2.2 answer44, p.636, exact r_-1(m) identity with sum_(t>0) f_-1(t)

The displayed auxiliary identity has a divergent right side; the final asymptotic expansion remains correct.

In answer5.2.2-44, p.636, the exact identity for r_-1(m) uses a sum over all t>0 of f_-1(t). Its normalized terms are(exp(-t²/(2m))-1)/t and have a negative harmonic tail. For m=2 the right side diverges while the left side is exp(-1/4). Restoring0<t<m gives the exact identity already described on p.130. Original typesetting and all five errata layers were checked. Known2021 endpoint/delta corrections are separate; the complete W expansion, including49/36, has been independently derived.

## Publication review, 12 November 2026

On p. 636 the regularized Gaussian sum needs the upper bound t<m. Its printed sum over all positive t has a negative harmonic tail and diverges, although the left side is finite. The main-text truncation makes the intended operation clear. The subsequent final asymptotic formula is not refuted.
