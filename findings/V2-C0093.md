# V2-C0093 — The finite leading-digit formula has incorrect endpoints

**Confirmed · low severity.** 3rd edition · 1st printing · 1997.

Location: Volume 2 · §4.2.4

Equation (6) uses the strict inequality 10f < r. At r=s=2 and n=1, P_1(20)=11/20: the integers counted are 1 and 10 through 19. The first equality in (8), stated for s<=r, instead gives 12/20.

Equation (6) uses the strict inequality 10f < r. At r=s=2 and n=1,
P_1(20)=11/20: the integers counted are 1 and 10 through 19.
The first equality in (8), stated for s<=r, instead gives 12/20.

For 1<r<10 and 1<=s<10, the first branch should use s<r; the second
branch applies to r<=s<10, with ceil(10^n r) in place of floor(10^n s)+1.
The endpoint s=10 should instead be treated as s=1 in the next decade.
Otherwise the second branch omits 10^(n+1): for r=2,s=10,n=1 it gives
11/100 instead of 12/100. The limiting formulas and Lemma Q are unaffected.

There are two related endpoint qualifications. The discussion explicitly
allows 1<=r<=10, but nonexistence of lim P_m(n) requires 1<r<10:
for r=1 and r=10 these integer sequences are constant. Also answer 8
has an exception at r=10, where (10) holds identically even for m=0.
For 1<r<10 its negative answer follows immediately by taking s=10;
for r=1 take s=1. These counterexamples use integer arguments only.

## Publication review, 12 November 2026

The strict leading-digit indicator counts eleven integers among 1,...,20 at r=s=2, while the exact equality on p. 258 counts twelve. Its other branch omits the new decade endpoint at s=10. The explicit allowed values r=1 and r=10 also need exceptions in the nonconvergence claims; answer 8 has the r=10 exception. Limiting formulas and the principal theorem survive these finite endpoint corrections.

Decision: `retained`. No matching prior report was found in the checked sources; absolute priority is not established.
