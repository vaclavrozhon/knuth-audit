# F159 — The convex-martingale proof needs another conditioning step

**Confirmed · low severity.** 1st edition · 2nd printing · 2022.

Location: 4B / Mathematical Preliminaries Redux, answer 84(a), p. 382

The stated equality in the proof is false. An additional application of the tower property repairs the proof; the convex-transformation theorem and the main-text statement remain true.

Answer84(a) identifies a conditional expectation given the full history with one given only the transformed history. For the martingale (Z0,Z1)=(-1,-1),(1,0),(1,2) with probabilities 1/2,1/4,1/4, frozen after time 1, and f(z)=z², the first conditional expectation is 1 or 2 while the second is 3/2. Both sides of the conditional Jensen inequality must instead be conditioned again on the transformed history.

## Publication review, 12 November 2026

Answer MPR84(a) explicitly equates conditioning on the Z history with conditioning on its transformed history. For f(z)=z^2 and atoms (-1,-1),(1,0),(1,2) of probabilities 1/2,1/4,1/4, the first conditional expectation is 1 or 2, while the second is 3/2. Conditioning Jensen's inequality once more repairs the proof; the theorem is true.
