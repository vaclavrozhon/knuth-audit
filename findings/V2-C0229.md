Candidate only: the recorded evidence does not yet establish a book error.

For example, consider the ascending chain
  1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 8, 14
with the selected operations
  2=1+1, 3=2+1, 4=2+2, 6=3+3, 8=4+4, 14=6+8.
Its reduced graph has vertices 1,2,3,4,14 and arcs
  1->2 twice; 1->3 once; 2->3 once; 2->4 twice;
  3->14 twice; 4->14 twice.
After reversal, the path-count labels, in reverse vertex order, are
  1, 2, 2, 6, 14.
No strictly ascending chain has this reduced graph, since two retained
vertices have the same path-count label. A computation with repeated
values does exist and has the required six additions. Identifying
repeated values can shorten the computation and change its graph.

There is a related distinction in the star-chain criterion on p. 480.
The same numerical sequence is a star chain if 4=3+1, 6=4+2, and
8=6+2 are selected instead. But the displayed selected representation
has incomparable vertices 3 and 4. The criterion is correct when
star operations and equivalence refer to the selected representation.

A possible clarification is to allow general computations, with selected
operand pairs and repeated values, throughout the graphical discussion.
The characterization of the optimal length remains valid: duplicate
removal gives an ascending chain of at most the graphical cost, while
an optimal ascending chain supplies a graph of exactly that cost.

This is kept as a candidate because the graphical discussion may already
intend to return to the general definition (1)/(2) and to treat selected
representations as the objects being compared. No claim is made that
its underlying minimum-length duality theorem is false.

found in the six errata layers through the archive checked 4 November
