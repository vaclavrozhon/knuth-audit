# V2-C0088 — Leading zeros after a division carry

**Candidate · medium severity.** 3rd edition · 1st printing · 1997.

Location: Volume 2 · §4.2.2

Candidate: the leading-zero requirement may apply before rounding rather than to the final representation. Both readings give the same numerical value in the example.

Candidate only: the recorded evidence does not yet establish a book error.

For b=10, p=2, .99/.01=100, retaining one leading zero needs exponent 4, while formula (50) permits 2 or 3. Requiring the zero only before rounding makes (3,.10) consistent. The interpretation remains unresolved.
