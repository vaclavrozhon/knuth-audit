# F024 — Algorithm R can take exponential time on 2SAT

**Confirmed · critical severity.** 1st edition · 2nd printing · 2022.

Location: 4B / p. 579, answer 7.2.2.2–164

A family of inputs requires 8·2^m − 5 visits to step R2.

Algorithm R can require exponentially many steps on 2SAT: m implication 3-cycles and an unsatisfiable core cause 8·2^m−5 visits to R2 with n=3m+2. Checked by a general proof and 11 direct runs, and confirmed in the PDF and the author’s PostScript. No matching correction was found in the complete 4B and Fascicle 6 errata; the physical copy has not been checked.

## Publication review, 12 November 2026

Answer 164, p. 579: Algorithm R can take exponential time on 2SAT, contrary to T_n=n. Use m disjoint three-variable implication cycles followed by an unsatisfiable two-variable core. With the permitted first-clause tie rule, the two autarky tests fail, both branches re-solve the remaining core, and R2 visits satisfy C_0=3, C_m=5+2C_(m-1). Thus C_m=8*2^m-5 for n=3m+2. This does not concern other 2SAT algorithms.

Decision: `retained`. No matching prior report was found in the checked sources; absolute priority is not established.
