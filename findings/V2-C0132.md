# V2-C0132 — The almost-sure quotient-sum asymptotic is false

**Confirmed · high severity.** 3rd edition · 1st printing · 1997.

Location: Volume 2 · Section 4.5.3 · Printed page 652

The main answer E[A_n]=infinity is correct.

The main answer E[A_n]=infinity is correct. The added assertion S_n/(n log_2 n)→1 for almost every real X is false: the ratio has infinite upper limit almost surely. A general proof uses conditional cylinder densities and divergent tail probabilities. The cited Khintchine 1935 paper explicitly distinguishes convergence in probability from this false stronger claim.

## Publication review, 12 November 2026

The original answer claims that the partial-quotient sum divided by n*lg(n) tends to one for almost all inputs. Khintchine's original p. 377 explicitly states convergence in measure and rejects almost-everywhere convergence. The stored conditional-cylinder proof independently gives infinite limsup almost surely: its conditional probability of a quotient at least m is (1+r)/(m+r)>=1/m. Retain as a misstatement of a known theorem, not a newly discovered probability result.

Decision: `retained`. No matching prior report was found in the checked sources; absolute priority is not established.
