When t = 0, there is exactly one empty choice of ministep pairs,
and binomial(r^2, 0) = 1. Thus the strict wording is false.
For example, the chain 1, 2, 4 has r = 2 and s = t = v = 0.
Chains consisting entirely of doublings give the same example
for arbitrarily large m, within the hypotheses of the theorem.

Please replace "fewer than" by "at most" in both occurrences.
In answer 18 on page 694, the case t = 0 should also be handled
as a factor of 1 before using expressions that divide by t.
This is a minor boundary issue; the theorem is unaffected.

Checked in the scanned first printing and against all six available
historical/current errata layers. A newer physical printing has not
been checked. Draft only; not sent.

## Publication review, 12 November 2026

The strict binomial bound on p. 472 fails for zero ministeps: there is one empty choice, also for arbitrarily long doubling chains. Use a non-strict inequality and treat the zero parameter separately in the answer. This is an endpoint wording correction.

Decision: `retained`. No matching prior report was found in the checked sources; absolute priority is not established.
