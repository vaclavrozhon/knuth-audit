# V2-C0068 — Zero variance and non-strict exceedance require care

**Confirmed · low severity.** 3rd edition · 1st printing · 1997.

Location: Volume 2 · §3.5 · Exercise 42(a)

The stated non-strict Chebyshev event has probability 1 at zero variance. Require positive variance or use a strict event.

The displayed Chebyshev inequality needs positive variance when its
probability uses a non-strict inequality. Take n=1, X_1=0 with
probability one, and t=2. All stated moment assumptions hold, but the
display asserts Pr(0 >= 0) <= 1/2, i.e. 1 <= 1/2.

One repair is to require sigma^2 > 0 (and the usual t > 0).
Alternatively, change >= to > inside the probability; then zero
variance is harmless. The deterministic case in the application to
Algorithm L is handled directly and does not invalidate Theorem G.

## Publication review, 12 November 2026

With one deterministic zero variable and `t=2`, the non-strict probability event has probability 1 and the stated bound is 1/2. Positive variance or a strict event repairs the boundary. The separate deterministic case in the downstream application remains valid.

Decision: `retained`. No matching prior report was found in the checked sources; absolute priority is not established.
