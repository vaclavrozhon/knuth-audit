# V4A-F0008 — The prime flip needs at least two variables

**Confirmed · low severity.** 1st edition · 1st printing · 2011.

Location: 4A / exercise 7.1.1–70, p. 89, and answer p. 547

The claim fails for one variable. The self-duality assertion remains true, and the complete monotonicity argument works for every n >= 2.

Take n = 1, f(x) = x and its only prime implicant, whose vector is t = 1. The printed construction gives g(0) = 1 and g(1) = 0, so g is not monotone. The exercise says any monotone self-dual function and does not exclude this case; the surrounding text explicitly includes projections. Adding n >= 2 repairs the statement: the minimal true point and its complement are then incomparable, and the two changes preserve monotonicity. Original typography and all five relevant errata layers were checked.

## Publication review, 12 November 2026

Exercise 7.1.1-70, p. 89: for n=1 the prime-point flip takes the monotone self-dual function x to its complement. The latter is not monotone. The nontrivial construction needs n at least two.

Decision: `retained`. No matching prior report was found in the checked sources; absolute priority is not established.
