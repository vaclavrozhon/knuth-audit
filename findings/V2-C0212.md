# V2-C0212 — The second remainder has modulus q2

Location: Volume 2 · §4.6.2 · Exercise 29,687

The modulus for the second residue t_2 should be q_2, not q_1.
For example, over F_3 let q_1=x^2+1, q_2=x^2+x+2 and t=(x+2)q_1.
Then t_1=0, t_2=x, t_2^4 mod q_1=1, but t_2^4 mod q_2=-1.
The printed condition predicts a split, although gcd(q_1*q_2,t^4-1)=1.
Correcting the index restores the intended CRT proof.

## Publication review, 12 November 2026

The second modulus in the first line of p. 687 must be q2. Over F3, q1=x²+1, q2=x²+x+2 and t=(x+2)q1 give t1=0,t2=x, but t2⁴ is 1 modulo q1 and −1 modulo q2. The printed test predicts a split where the actual gcd is 1.
