# V2-C0023 — Irrational coordinates can reach a coding boundary

**Confirmed · medium severity.** 3rd edition · 1st printing · 1997.

Location: Volume 2 · §3.3.2 · Answer 27; printed 567

Irrational coordinates alone do not guarantee a unique sequence of strict permutation codes.

Let a=sqrt(2)/4. The distinct irrational coordinates (a/3,5a/6,1−7a/6) transform to (a,a,1−2a), on the boundary of regions 4 and 5. No strict ordering applies there. Exclude equality at every iterate or specify a tie-breaking rule. The exceptional set has measure zero but includes irrational points. Rechecked against the first printing and all four errata layers on 6 November 2026. A recent physical printing and priority remain unverified.

## Publication review, 12 November 2026

Distinct irrational starting coordinates can acquire equal coordinates after one iteration, leaving the stated strict-order code undefined.

Decision: `retained`. No matching prior report was found in the checked sources; absolute priority is not established.
