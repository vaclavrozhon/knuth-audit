# V2-C0161 — The fixed base two cannot factor the stated example

**Confirmed · medium severity.** 3rd edition · 1st printing · 1997.

Location: Volume2 · printed 413, 661

The prescribed example fails. The saved base-three witness succeeds; a general method must allow a change of base.

For the example2^214+1,428 divides the first-stage exponent, so base2 gives1 modulo the entire input. Both remaining prime factors have order428; changing only the exponent cannot separate them.

## Publication review, 12 November 2026

The example asks the fixed-base-two method to recover a factor of 2^214+1, but its first-stage exponent is divisible by 428, making the residue one modulo the entire input. Even the stated remaining two-factor cofactor has order 428 at both primes, so every base-two gcd is trivial. The stored exact certificate and repair with base three support the finding; no new large factoring run was needed.

Decision: `retained`. No matching prior report was found in the checked sources; absolute priority is not established.
