# V1-C0041 — The minimum-spanning-tree update selects a vertex twice

**Confirmed · critical severity.** 3rd edition · 1st printing · 1997.

Location: Volume 1 · §2.3.4.1 · Answer 12 · printed 580

The algorithm can finish with a disconnected terminal and a repeated edge.

The update loop can overwrite the infinity marker of an already chosen
vertex. For four terminals take the symmetric finite costs

c14=1, c12=2, c24=3, c34=10, c23=20, c13=30.

Initially a=(1,3,10), b=(4,4,4). The first iteration chooses vertex 1
and edge 14, leaving a=(infinity,2,10). The second chooses vertex 2 and
edge 21. Its update restores a(1)=2 and b(1)=2, so a=(2,infinity,10).
The third and last iteration therefore chooses edge 12 again. Vertex 3
is disconnected. There are no ties, zero costs, or infinite input costs.
Undefined diagonal comparisons may harmlessly be omitted; the failure
does not depend on their interpretation.

Restrict the update to as-yet unchosen vertices other than i. With the
printed marking convention, require j!=i and a(j)<infinity before
testing c(i,j)<a(j). Already chosen markers then stay unchanged, while
each other a(j) remains the cheapest connection to the chosen set.
The next minimum extends that set by one vertex. The usual exchange
across this cut proves minimum total cost, and the time remains O(n^2).
The linked-list alternative in the next paragraph avoids this defect.

The root audit inspected the original answer and all four relevant
errata PDF pages, read the exercise's general cost contract, and checked
the three iterations and general repair. Exhaustive supplemental tests
compare the repair with an independent Kruskal implementation on all
760 complete graphs with n<=4 and costs in {1,2,3}; all agree.

All four historical/current errata layers were searched; none repairs
this loop. The 2023 Prim/Jarnik citation change concerns answer 11, and
the 2006 terminology correction concerns answer 13. The physical print
and priority have not been verified. No report has been sent externally. The November 5 sequential review reconfirmed the short counterexample and general repair without repeating the accepted exhaustive tests. The defect is in answer 12 on 580; the abstract exercise on 371 is correct.
