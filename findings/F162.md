# F162 — The mean-comparison proof counts equal values instead of equal distances

**Confirmed · low severity.** 1st edition · 2nd printing · 2022.

Location: 4B / Mathematical Preliminaries Redux, answer11, p.372

Change this one equality to ≥, or insert the correct distance-tie event. The exercise and every other proof step remain valid; discrete atom masses make the final inequality strict.

Answer11 equates P(|X₂−α|≤|X₁−α|) with (1+P(X₁=X₂))/2. For independent fair signs and α=0, the comparison always holds, while the printed expression is3/4. The correct tie event is equality of the two distances from α.

## Publication review, 12 November 2026

Answer MPR11 confuses equal distances with equal values. Independent fair signs at alpha=0 make the displayed sides 1 and 3/4. Replacing the equality by an inequality, or using the correct equal-distance event, repairs the otherwise valid argument.
