# V1-C0034 — The overflow-count bound is false

**Confirmed · high severity.** 3rd edition · 1st printing · 1997.

Location: Volume 1 · §2.2.2 · Exercise 17 · pages 254 and 544

Only one live item is needed to violate the stated bound; the discrepancy grows quadratically in the number of stacks.

For four stacks in four cells, compare initial capacities (0,0,0,4) with (1,1,1,1). The sequence I3 D3 I2 D2 I1 D1 I3 D3 I2 causes five overflows in the first layout and none in the second, contradicting 5 ≤ 0 + 4. At most one item is live. After successive pushes the first layout has capacities (0,0,1,3), (0,1,0,3), (1,0,0,3), (1,0,1,2), and (1,1,0,2). Popping releases the item without restoring the previous capacities. A general family gives n(n−1)/2 versus zero when M=n. The 2009 change from “less” to “more” does not fix this; every move uses the rightward-free-space case, so the 2011 left-copy correction is irrelevant. Original pages 254/544 and all four current errata layers were rechecked on November 5, 2026. A physical copy and priority of discovery remain unverified.

## Publication review, 12 November 2026

A four-stack trace gives five overflows against zero, violating the stated additive bound of four.

Decision: `retained`. No matching prior report was found in the checked sources; absolute priority is not established.
