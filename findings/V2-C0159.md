# V2-C0159 — Factoring completeness and probability need coprime relations

**Confirmed · medium severity.** 3rd edition · 1st printing · 1997.

Location: Volume 2 · §4.5.4 · Exercises 12–13, pp. 412–413 and 659–660

The printed assumptions allow inputs for which F loses a usable factor relation and the stated failure probability is wrong.

For N=155, k=29 and factor base 2,3,5, six genuine outputs of corrected E make F discard every tested pair. Yet outputs 4 and 6 give x=116, y=101 and gcd(x−y,155)=5. The note records the complete short trace. Exercise 13 also fails for N=15 with a nonunit square: both roots are discarded, giving failure probability 1 instead of 1/2. Require N to be coprime to the factor base, including 2, or remove these small factors first. Dixon’s method already states the needed assumptions.

## Publication review, 12 November 2026

The completeness argument for Algorithm F needs factor-base elements relatively prime to N, a condition not imposed on Algorithm E's stated inputs. The stored six-row N=155,k=29 example causes F to discard its basis solutions despite a useful dependency yielding gcd(116-101,155)=5. The printed setup was checked for hidden preconditions. Removing base factors first repairs it. The associated probability answer also needs odd N and invertibility; these are present in the later Dixon setup, not the earlier claim.

Decision: `retained`. No matching prior report was found in the checked sources; absolute priority is not established.
