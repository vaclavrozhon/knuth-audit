# V1-C0007 — Bilateral Vandermonde fails under the prescribed iterated limits

**Confirmed · high severity.** 3rd edition · 1st printing · 1997.

Location: Volume 1 · §1.2.6 · Exercise 42

The bilateral Vandermonde identity in the final paragraph is not valid for all convergent instances under the iterated-limit definition of binomial coefficients given earlier in the same answer.

The bilateral Vandermonde identity in the final paragraph is not valid
for all convergent instances under the iterated-limit definition of
binomial coefficients given earlier in the same answer.

Set r=s=alpha=beta=-1/2. The proposed identity becomes

sum_{k in Z} binom(-1/2,-1/2+k) binom(-1/2,-1/2-k)
    = binom(-1,-1).

For k>=1 the first factor is zero, since its denominator contains
Gamma(1-k), while Gamma(1/2) and Gamma(k+1/2) are finite. The second
factor is finite. For k<=-1 the second factor is zero and the first
is finite. At k=0 both factors equal 1. Thus the sum has just one
nonzero term and equals 1; in particular it converges absolutely.

But binom(-1,-1)=0 under the stated definition. For generic z near -1,
taking the lower-index limit w -> -1 first gives

lim_{w -> -1} Gamma(z+1)/(Gamma(w+1)Gamma(z-w+1)) = 0.

The subsequent limit z -> -1 is still zero. This also agrees with
the book's convention that a negative integral lower index gives zero.

Hence the asserted identity reads 1=0. Every coefficient in the example
is finite, and no complex power occurs, so the qualification about
choosing branches does not resolve the discrepancy. The issue is the
singular total upper index r+s=-1, together with the lower-index-first
limiting convention. Convergence alone is not a sufficient condition.
