# V2-C0127 — The fourth expansion diverges at the included endpoint

**Confirmed · medium severity.** 3rd edition · 1st printing · 1997.

Location: Volume 2 · §4.5.2 · Exercise 30

The stated common convergence domain includes a point where one series has terms that do not tend to zero.

At x=1 the kth term of the fourth expansion is(−1)^k(1/4+o(1)). A uniform bound on its Fourier term and the explicit binomial expression prove this limit. Restrict the convergent expansion to0<x<1 and specify continuous or Abel limits separately. The original double sum remains finite.

## Publication review, 12 November 2026

The fourth expansion's k-th term at x=1 approaches (-1)^k/4 and therefore does not tend to zero. Its displayed common domain must exclude this endpoint, or explicitly use an Abel limit there. The first three expansions are not implicated. The stored bound on the periodic contribution and binomial-sum asymptotic support this conclusion independently of floating-point plots.
