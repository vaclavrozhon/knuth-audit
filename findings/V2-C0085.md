# V2-C0085 — Theorem D's extension fails for base two and precision one

**Confirmed · low severity.** 3rd edition · 1st printing · 1997.

Location: Volume 2 · §4.2.2

The extension to ties-to-odd fails for one-bit binary arithmetic. The recommended ties-to-even binary rule is unaffected.

The extension of Theorem D to rounding to odd has an exception when
b = 2 and p = 1. Here "round to odd" is applied in step N5 before
any subsequent normalization of a rounding carry.

Take u = 1 and v = 1/4, with an exponent range wide enough to include
all intermediate values. Rounding 3/4 = (.11)_2 to one binary place
selects (.1)_2 = 1/2, whose retained digit is odd. Therefore

u (+) v = 1,
    (u (+) v) (-) v = 1/2,
    ((u (+) v) (-) v) (+) v = 1/2,
    (((u (+) v) (-) v) (+) v) (-) v = 1/4.

Thus the two sides of Theorem D differ. The assertion that it also
holds for rounding to odd needs to exclude this one-bit case (or
supply a different explicit convention there). This does not challenge
the default binary rule of rounding to even.

The one-bit format is explicitly allowed in exercise 29. I found no
applicable correction in all2-prepre, all2-pre, all2, or err2. A later
physical printing has not been checked. This draft has not been sent.

## Publication review, 12 November 2026

With base 2 and one digit, ties to odd at the pre-normalization rounding step violate the claimed drift identity. Starting at u=1,v=1/4 gives 1,1/2,1/2,1/4 after alternating addition and subtraction. Algorithm N explicitly resolves a tie before a possible carry and rescaling. The ordinary ties-to-even theorem remains valid.

Decision: `retained`. No matching prior report was found in the checked sources; absolute priority is not established.
