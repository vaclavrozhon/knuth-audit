# V2-C0235 — The two circulation directions are exchanged

**Confirmed · low severity.** 3rd edition · 1st printing · 1997.

Location: Volume 2 · §4.6.4 · Exercise 23(a)

In the third edition, first printing (1997), p. 702, the two directions in the contour argument appear to be interchanged.

In the third edition, first printing (1997), p. 702, the two directions
in the contour argument appear to be interchanged.

For pi/2 <= t <= 3pi/2, the large semicircle f(R exp(it)) has argument
increase approximately n*pi, hence moves counterclockwise. The return
path f(it), -R <= t <= R, has clockwise net rotation under the stated
root assumptions. This agrees with the arrows in Fig. 16 on p. 517.

For an explicit example take f(z)=(z-1)^4. It has four roots in the
open right half-plane and none on the imaginary axis, and

d arg f(it) / dt = -4/(1+t^2) < 0.

Thus the printed counterclockwise direction for this path is reversed.
Interchanging clockwise and counterclockwise in the two sentences
preserves the root counts and the conclusion of the exercise.

## Publication review, 12 November 2026

The two orientation descriptions on p. 702 are reversed. The large left semicircle, parametrized by increasing argument, is counterclockwise; the straight return segment contributes clockwise change. The diagram's arrows are already correct.

Decision: `retained`. No matching prior report was found in the checked sources; absolute priority is not established.
