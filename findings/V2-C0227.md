The statement that the optimum chains have total additive cost
(5/2)n + O(sqrt(n)) needs the qualification "for odd n".
For n = 2^k, the doubling chain has total cost 2n - 2, and this
is optimal: a_i <= 2*a_(i-1) implies
  sum(i=1..r) a_i >= 2*sum(i=1..r)(a_i-a_(i-1)) = 2(n-1).
The difference between 2n-2 and (5/2)n is not O(sqrt(n)).

Zantema's technical report RUU-CS-89-15 (June 1989), page 3,
distinguishes the lower bound for odd n from the upper bound
for all n. The abstract of the 1991 journal article also expressly
restricts the asymptotic equality to odd n.

Alternatively, for unrestricted n the statement can be changed
to an upper bound of (5/2)n + O(sqrt(n)). The following odd-n
lower bound and equality characterization in the book are correct.

The same unqualified equality appears in the second-edition errata
dated 18 October 1996, page 638, answer 35. No later correction was
found in the six available historical/current errata layers.
Checked in the scanned first printing; a newer physical printing
has not been checked. Draft only; not sent.

## Publication review, 12 November 2026

The 5n/2+O(sqrt(n)) minimum-cost assertion on p. 696 needs n odd. For powers of two the minimum sum of chain terms is 2n−2. Zantema's original 1989 report explicitly restricts its lower bound to odd n and gives an upper bound for all n. This is a misstatement of a known result, not a new chain bound.

Decision: `retained`. No matching prior report was found in the checked sources; absolute priority is not established.
