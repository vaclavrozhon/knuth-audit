# V3-C0008 — The hook-walk induction starts at the wrong indices

**Confirmed · low severity.** 2nd edition · 1st printing · 1998.

Location: Volume 3 / answer 5.1.4-38(a), p. 613

The base case says k=l=1 where the definitions require k=l=0.

The sets I and J contain k+1 and l+1 indices, so a path consisting only of its corner has k=l=0 and probability 1/n. In a 3×3 shape with I=J={1,3}, k=l=1 gives two paths of probability 1/72 each, totaling 1/36 rather than 1/9. The stated product formula is correct. Original typesetting and all relevant errata checked; the historical addition repeats the same typo.

## Publication review, 12 November 2026

The base case on p. 613 is k=l=0, since the sets start at index zero. For a 3 by 3 square and I=J={1,3}, the actual probability is 1/36, rather than the 1/9 asserted by the printed base case. The 1995 first-edition addition repeats the wrong base case.

Decision: `retained`. No matching prior report was found in the checked sources; absolute priority is not established.
