# V2-C0196 — The proof omits the case j>=k

**Confirmed · medium severity.** 3rd edition · 1st printing · 1997.

Location: Volume 2 · §4.6.1 · Exercise 14

The first coefficient implies p^min(j,k) divides a_1; it need not imply p^j divides a_1 when j>k.

The first coefficient implies p^min(j,k) divides a_1; it need not imply p^j divides a_1 when j>k.

A counterexample with primitive inputs is
  u=2x^5+10x^4+11x^3+x^2,
  v=2x^3+8x^2+4x+1,
  q=8x^2+8x-4, r=8x+4,
so that 8u=vq+r. For p=2, we have j=3,k=1,P=2 and a_1=4, which is not divisible by 8. Both degree gaps are 2.

The exercise remains true. If j>=k, the first coefficient gives P|a_1 and the second gives P|a_0. If j<k, p^j|a_1 and the third coefficient gives P|a_0 by comparing p-adic valuations.

## Publication review, 12 November 2026

The proof of answer 14 incorrectly asserts divisibility of a1 by p^j when j>k. The primitive inputs 2x⁵+10x⁴+11x³+x² and 2x³+8x²+4x+1 give 8u=v(8x²+8x−4)+(8x+4); here p=2, j=3, k=1 and a1=4. The exercise is true: separating the j>=k case repairs the proof.

Decision: `retained`. No matching prior report was found in the checked sources; absolute priority is not established.
