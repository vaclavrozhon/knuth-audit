# V2-C0052 — Incorrect ratio in the modulo-bias example

**Confirmed · medium severity.** 3rd edition · 1st printing · 1997.

Location: Volume 2 · §3.4.1 · Answer 3 · p. 584

The numerical example overstates the probability; its ratio k/m needs a factor of two.

For J uniform on 0,…,m−1, write m=qk+s and h=ceil(k/2). The exact probability Pr(J mod k < k/2) is (qh+min(s,h))/m. For m=3k with even k this is 1/2; m=256,k=86 gives 129/256. The stated value near 2/3 corresponds to k near 2m/3. Rechecked against the original scan and all errata layers on November 6, 2026. The published rejection-boundary correction is separate. Novelty and the latest physical printing remain unverified.

## Publication review, 12 November 2026

If `m=3k` with even `k`, uniform reduction modulo `k` gives probability 1/2 of landing below `k/2`, not 2/3. The example needs `k` near `2m/3`. The 2001 correction to the rejection comparison addresses a different expression.

Decision: `retained`. No matching prior report was found in the checked sources; absolute priority is not established.
