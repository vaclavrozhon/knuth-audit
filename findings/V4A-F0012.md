# V4A-F0012 — The multiple-output counting bound is too small

**Confirmed · medium severity.** 1st edition · 1st printing · 2011.

Location: 4A / answer7.1.2-47, p.570

A concrete counterexample disproves the finite counting inequality. The leading asymptotic lower bound is not disproved.

For n=256,m=r=4, the ordered tuples of four distinct one-gate functions already number11349915205231040601600, exceeding the printed upper bound10367357475042936177152. The single-output complement factor cannot be retained for multiple independently complemented outputs. The leading asymptotic conclusion is not refuted.

## Publication review, 12 November 2026

Answer 7.1.2-47, p. 570: the stated finite counting bound for several outputs misses output choices. At n=256,m=r=4, the ordered distinct choices of four essential binary functions already give 11349915205231040601600 functions, exceeding the stated upper bound 10367357475042936177152. This refutes that bound, not the leading asymptotic conclusion.
