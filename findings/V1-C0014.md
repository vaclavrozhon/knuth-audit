# V1-C0014 — Composed probability-generating coefficients need not be polynomials

**Confirmed · low severity.** 3rd edition · 1st printing · 1997.

Location: Volume 1 · §1.2.10 · Exercise 17(a)

A proof calls the coefficients polynomials, although infinite convergent sums may be required. The conclusion and moment formulas remain correct.

The coefficients of h=g(f(z)) need not be polynomials in the
coefficients of f and g. If f(z)=(1+z)/2 and g(z)=sum q_j z^j,
already the constant coefficient is sum q_j 2^(-j).
No polynomial in finitely many q_j gives this for every PGF g:
for N beyond all its indices, g=z^(N+1) and g=z^(N+2) agree on
all those coefficients but give different constant terms of h.

Replacing "polynomials" by "convergent sums of products" repairs
the proof. All terms are nonnegative and their total sum is one.
The stated conclusion that h is a probability generating function
and the mean/variance formulas remain valid (with finite moments
for the latter formulas).

The original page and all five available historical/current errata
sources were checked on November 1 and rechecked on November 5, 2026; no correction was found.
Priority and a physical copy are unverified.

## Publication review, 12 November 2026

The coefficients can require infinite convergent sums, so the polynomial wording is inaccurate; the conclusion is correct.

Decision: `retained`. No matching prior report was found in the checked sources; absolute priority is not established.
