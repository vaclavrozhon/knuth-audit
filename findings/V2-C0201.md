# V2-C0201 — Two constants have no common base of maximal degree

**Confirmed · medium severity.** 3rd edition · 1st printing · 1997.

Location: Volume 2 · §4.6.1 · Exercise 17(d)

The request to find W having the largest possible degree needs an exception when both input polynomials are constants.

The request to find W having the largest possible degree needs an
exception when both input polynomials are constants.

Take the alphabet {a} and U=V=1. These are nonzero homogeneous
polynomials and UV=VU. For every positive integer k, the choice
W=a^k, r=s=1, m=n=0 satisfies U=r W^m and V=s W^n. Thus the
possible degrees of W are unbounded. Zero exponents are also
needed by the answer's branch in which just V is constant.

One repair is to assume that at least one input is nonconstant
when asking for the greatest degree, handling two constants
separately without an optimality claim. The common-power theorem
and the algorithm for nonconstant inputs remain valid.

## Publication review, 12 November 2026

The maximal-degree common base in the string-polynomial exercise need not exist when both inputs are nonzero constants. For U=V=1, any homogeneous word a^k is a base with both exponents zero, so its degree is unbounded. The common-power statement and its nonconstant case remain sound; the answer explicitly permits a constant input.

Decision: `retained`. No matching prior report was found in the checked sources; absolute priority is not established.
