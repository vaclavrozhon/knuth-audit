# F109 — Uniform option size does not guarantee even distance

**Confirmed · medium severity.** 1st edition · 2nd printing · 2022.

Location: 4B / exercise 7.2.2.1–32(d), p. 127; answer p. 422

Exercise 32(d) must require equal numbers of primary items in each option.

Let a,b be primary and x,y secondary. The three options {a,b}, {a,x}, {b,y} all have size 2, but the complete solution set is 100 and 011, at Hamming distance 3. Direct subset enumeration and the unmodified author DLX1 agree. With equal primary size k>0, every solution has N1/k options and the even-distance proof works. The answer already uses that stronger condition. The PDF and original PS contain the omission; refreshed current and historical errata do not correct it. Physical copy not yet checked.

## Publication review, 12 November 2026

DLX 32(d): primary a,b and secondary x,y with options ab, ax, by give two solutions at Hamming distance three although every option has two items. Equal numbers of primary items, as already used by the answer, are the required hypothesis.

Decision: `retained`. No matching prior report was found in the checked sources; absolute priority is not established.
