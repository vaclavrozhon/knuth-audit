# V1-C0003 — Taking a square root loses the sign of the triangular product

**Confirmed · medium severity.** 3rd edition · 1st printing · 1997.

Location: Volume 1 · §1.2.3 · Exercise 26

A proof step added in the 2020 errata loses the sign for negative factors; the original 1997 final formula is correct.

The replacement dated 12 June 2020 in all1-pre includes

P1 = (P1 P2)^(1/2) = (product_{i=0}^n a_i)^(n+2),

where P1 and P2 are the lower and upper triangular products. Although
P1=P2, the first equality need not hold: the exercise does not require
positive a_i.

For n=1, a_0=-1, a_1=1, both triangular products are -1, but
(P1 P2)^(1/2)=1. The argument uses only real numbers and a positive
radicand, so no complex-root convention is involved.

The final formula is correct. A direct proof is to count a_r's
occurrences in P1: r+1 as the first factor and n-r+1 as the second,
giving exponent n+2. This also covers zero and negative factors.
Alternatively, one can first prove the identity for positive factors
and explicitly extend it as a polynomial identity.

The 1997 first-printing answer states the correct final formula without
this inserted square-root equality. The issue is in the 2020 addition,
visually verified in the author's all1-pre PDF. No subsequent correction
was located in all1-prepre, all1-pre, all1, err1, or current err1.textxt
available on November 1, 2026. No claim of priority, author acceptance,
or verification of a newer physical printing is made.

## Publication review, 12 November 2026

The erratum takes a square root of P1 squared without preserving its sign; final identity is correct.

Decision: `retained`. No matching prior report was found in the checked sources; absolute priority is not established.
