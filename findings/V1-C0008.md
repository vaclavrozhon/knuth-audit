# V1-C0008 — The added endpoint makes the strict inequalities false

**Confirmed · medium severity.** 3rd edition · 1st printing · 1997.

Location: Volume 1 · §1.2.6 · Exercise 66

The replacement answer dated November 8, 2005, combined with the April 17, 2015 change from y>n-1 to y>=n-1, still uses Y>0 and (x-n)/(n+1) < (z-n+1)/n. These strict inequalities fail at the newly included endpoint.

The replacement answer dated November 8, 2005, combined with the
April 17, 2015 change from y>n-1 to y>=n-1, still uses Y>0 and
(x-n)/(n+1) < (z-n+1)/n. These strict inequalities fail at the
newly included endpoint.

For example, take n=2, y=z=1, x=2. The defining equality is

binom(2,2) = binom(1,2) + binom(1,1) = 1,

but Y=binom(1,2)=0 and both (x-n)/(n+1) and (z-n+1)/n are zero.

The theorem remains true. In the proof, use Y>=0 and the weak
inequality (x-n)/(n+1) <= (z-n+1)/n. The comparison of logarithmic
derivatives is still strict: after adding j/(n+1) and j/n for
1<=j<=n-1, their difference is strict, and X'/X has the further
positive summand 1/x. Thus dx/dz < (n+1)Z_minus/(n X) still holds.
In the final chain, retain the first strict inequality but replace
the last '< 0' by '<= 0'; this is enough to conclude F'(z)<0.

This is a local proof correction, not a counterexample to either
stated binomial inequality. The archived original errata images
all1-prepre page 33 and all1-pre page 15 were inspected. No further
correction to this step was located in all1-prepre, all1-pre, all1,
err1, or err1.textxt available on November 1, 2026.
The corresponding text in a later physical printing has not been
verified. No claim of priority or author acceptance is made.
